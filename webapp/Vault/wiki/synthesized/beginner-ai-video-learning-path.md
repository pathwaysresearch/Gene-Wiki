# Beginner AI Video Learning Path: A Three-Tier Scaffolding

## Overview

For a beginner trying to orient themselves in the AI landscape, the sheer volume of available video content can be paralyzing. A useful organizing principle is a three-tier scaffolding that moves from strategic context, to visual intuition, to hands-on technical building. This progression maps naturally onto Bloom's taxonomy — from the lower-order skills of remembering and understanding, through applying and analyzing, up to the higher-order skills of evaluating and creating. Rather than diving immediately into technical tutorials or, conversely, staying permanently at the level of business punditry, beginners benefit most from moving deliberately through all three tiers in sequence.

## The Three Tiers

**Tier 1 — Strategic Context (Why AI Matters):** The logical entry point for any beginner is developing a mental model of what AI is changing and why it matters at a societal and organizational level. Karim Lakhani's talk *Competing in the Age of AI* (Harvard Business School, D³) is an exemplary resource here. Lakhani traces the evolution from early 2000s internet-era companies to today's trillion-dollar AI-native platforms, demonstrates live generative AI tools like Midjourney, and frames AI not as a narrow technical phenomenon but as a force reshaping entire competitive landscapes. This tier answers the question 'Why should I care?' and gives the beginner enough vocabulary and framing to make sense of everything that follows. Watching this first means that subsequent technical content lands in a meaningful strategic context rather than as decontextualized mechanics.

**Tier 2 — Visual Intuition (How AI Thinks):** Once a beginner understands why AI matters, the next step is developing intuition for how these systems actually work — without yet requiring the ability to build one. The canonical resource for this tier is 3Blue1Brown's visual explainer series on neural networks and transformers. These videos use animated mathematics to make concepts like gradient descent, attention mechanisms, and embedding spaces genuinely comprehensible to a non-specialist audience. This tier occupies the middle of Bloom's taxonomy — understanding and beginning to apply — by giving the learner a mental model they can reason with. After this tier, a beginner can follow technical conversations, read AI coverage more critically, and ask better questions.

**Tier 3 — Hands-On Building (How to Make AI):** The final tier moves from comprehension to creation. Andrej Karpathy's YouTube video *Let's Build GPT from Scratch* is the most cited technical explainer of large language models and is specifically noted as transformative for data engineers and technically inclined learners. At 3.5 hours, it walks through building a GPT model from fundamental matrix math and gradient descent upward. This tier is where Bloom's higher-order skills — analysis, evaluation, and creation — are activated. A beginner who has completed Tiers 1 and 2 will find this video far more accessible than if they had attempted it cold, because the strategic and intuitive scaffolding is already in place.

## Applications and Implications

This scaffolding is particularly valuable because it respects the genuine difficulty of AI literacy while refusing to treat it as inaccessible. Each tier is a standalone resource, so learners can self-assess where they are and enter at the appropriate level — a senior executive may spend most time in Tier 1, a curious generalist may find Tier 2 their productive edge, and a software engineer may sprint through Tiers 1 and 2 to reach Tier 3 quickly. The three-tier model also serves as a conversation starter for organizations designing internal AI literacy programs: rather than sending everyone to the same resource, program designers can differentiate by role, prior knowledge, and learning objective. The broader lesson is that AI video content is not a monolith — it exists on a spectrum from conceptual to technical, and deliberate sequencing dramatically improves both comprehension and retention.
