# W25148 — The Productivity J-Curve: How Intangibles Complement General Purpose Technologies

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This NBER working paper by Erik Brynjolfsson, Daniel Rock, and Chad Syverson (2018, revised 2020) proposes that the adoption of general purpose technologies (GPTs) triggers a "productivity J-curve": an initial period of seemingly low measured productivity growth followed by a later surge, driven by unmeasured intangible capital investments that complement the new GPT. The authors apply Tobin's q-theory of investment to infer productivity mismeasurement attributable to unmeasured intangible capital, using market value regressions to estimate intangible investment as the residual between observed market value increases and observed tangible investment. The paper provides a formal framework explaining Solow's Paradox — the phenomenon where transformative technologies appear not to boost productivity — by showing how standard growth accounting fails to capture unmeasured inputs producing unmeasured outputs during GPT adoption periods.

## Key Claims

- When a new GPT emerges, measurable resources are committed and measurable output is forgone to build new, unmeasured intangible inputs that complement the GPT, creating a prolonged period of apparent productivity stagnation
- Historical GPT adoptions (British industrial revolution, electrification) exhibited similar extended lag periods before productivity gains materialized — e.g., "Engels' Pause" lasted half a century
- Standard Solow Residual growth accounting misses productivity contributions from unmeasured inputs producing unmeasured output and from measured inputs producing unmeasured output
- The authors use Tobin's q-theory to infer intangible capital: when a firm's market value rises more than observed investment explains, the difference reflects "intangible correlates"
- The framework handles recursive cases where intangible capital is used to produce more intangible capital
- True productivity growth differs from measured productivity growth as a function of four channels: measured inputs → measured output, measured inputs → unmeasured output, unmeasured inputs → measured output, and unmeasured inputs → unmeasured output
