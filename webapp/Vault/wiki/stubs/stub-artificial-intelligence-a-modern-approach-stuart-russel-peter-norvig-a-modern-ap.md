# Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach (3rd Edition) — Stuart Russell & Peter Norvig

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This is the third edition (2010) of the definitive textbook on artificial intelligence by Stuart Russell (UC Berkeley) and Peter Norvig (Google Research). The book provides a comprehensive, unified treatment of AI organized around the concept of intelligent agents that perceive their environment and act to achieve goals. This edition places increased emphasis on partially observable and nondeterministic environments, introduces belief states and state estimation in non-probabilistic settings before extending them with probabilities, and distinguishes among atomic, factored, and structured representations for agent reasoning. It is the most widely adopted AI textbook worldwide and covers the field from foundational search and logic through modern probabilistic methods, machine learning, robotics, and natural language processing.

## Key Claims

- AI can be unified under the framework of rational agents operating in different types of environments (fully/partially observable, deterministic/nondeterministic, etc.)
- Agent representations can be categorized into atomic representations (states as black boxes), factored representations (attribute/value pairs), and structured representations (objects and relations), each enabling different reasoning capabilities
- Belief states and state estimation are fundamental concepts that apply first in non-probabilistic search and planning settings, before being extended with probabilistic reasoning later
- Probability, particularly Bayesian reasoning (following Thomas Bayes), underlies much of modern AI technology
- Practical AI milestones—such as Deep Blue defeating Kasparov in 1997 and Mars Exploration Rovers—demonstrate the real-world impact of AI techniques covered in the book

## Topics Covered

Intelligent agents and environments, search algorithms and problem solving, constraint satisfaction, game playing, knowledge representation and logic, probabilistic reasoning and Bayesian networks, planning under uncertainty, machine learning, natural language processing, robotics, computer vision, belief states and state estimation
