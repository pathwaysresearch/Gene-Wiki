# Keynote: Stefano Puntoni (The Wharton School) — AI, Platforms, and the Future of Work at Reshaping Work 2025

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

Stefano Puntoni of The Wharton School delivers a keynote at Reshaping Work 2025 examining how AI and digital platforms are reshaping organisations and the future of work. He presents empirical research showing shifting beliefs about AI's role—from replacement fears toward enhancement optimism—while warning about unintended consequences of algorithmic management such as reduced prosocial behaviour among workers. The talk bridges experimental consumer research (skill-replacing vs. skill-allowing automation) with organisational implications, arguing that AI should be understood as a driver of organisational change requiring responsible leadership at the intersection of human decision-making and machine intelligence.

## Key Claims

- Between 2023 and 2024, survey data showed a significant increase in beliefs that AI will enhance employee skills, alongside a soft decrease in beliefs about AI replacing workers
- Skill-replacing automation (technology that "takes care of everything") affects user identity differently than skill-allowing automation (technology that involves users in the process)
- Algorithmic management leads to reduced prosocial and helping behaviour among workers—people are less willing to help colleagues when managed by algorithms
- Algorithmic management can be perceived as objectifying by workers, with measurable negative consequences for organisational cooperation
- AI should be viewed not merely as a technology but as a driver of organisational change, requiring new leadership mindsets and capabilities
