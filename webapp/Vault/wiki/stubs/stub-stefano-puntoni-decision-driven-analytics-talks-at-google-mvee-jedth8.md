# Stefano Puntoni | Decision-Driven Analytics | Talks at Google

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

Professor Stefano Puntoni of The Wharton School discusses his book "Decision-Driven Analytics: Leveraging Human Intelligence to Unlock the Power of Data" in a Talks at Google presentation. He argues that organizations should start from the decision rather than from the data — identifying business alternatives and courses of action first, then deploying analytics to evaluate them. The talk covers a framework for the division of labor between decision makers (who formulate questions) and analysts (who conduct the technical work), and addresses pitfalls like preference-driven analytics where preexisting desires contaminate the analytical process.

## Key Claims

- The data-driven approach of building data lakes first and then hoping to find useful insights is less effective than starting from the decision
- Decision-driven analytics begins by identifying the business objective, enumerating courses of action, and defining what "good" looks like before involving analysts
- Formulating good analytical questions is a non-technical job that belongs to managers and domain experts, not data specialists
- There is a sharp divide between data-oriented people who love exploring spreadsheets and decision-oriented people who ask "what are we trying to do here" — both perspectives are needed
- Organizations must take deliberate steps to safeguard against preference-driven analytics, where decision makers' preexisting wishes bias the analytical process
