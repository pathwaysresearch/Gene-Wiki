# Being Human In Age Of Ai

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This source is an introduction to a JACR (Journal of the Association for Consumer Research) special issue on the Automation of Marketing and Consumption. It examines how the growing diffusion of automation and AI affects consumers in two key ways: through the automation of marketing activities (e.g., pricing, customer support) and through the automation of consumption activities (e.g., LLMs for writing, recommender systems, self-driving cars). The piece reflects on psychological tensions consumers face with AI, the shifting discourse around human exceptionalism from cognitive abilities to emotions and social intelligence, and calls for more nuanced research that differentiates between types of algorithms rather than treating them interchangeably.

## Key Claims

- AI and automation affect consumers both through automated marketing activities (impacting welfare and well-being) and through automated consumption activities (LLMs, recommender systems, autonomous products)
- AI acts as a double-edged sword: it enhances efficiency and personalization but also risks constraining consumers' expressive range, choices, and personal development opportunities beyond the commonly discussed issues of discrimination and privacy
- The reference comparison category for human exceptionalism has shifted from animals (emphasizing cognition) to AI (emphasizing emotions and social intelligence), as machines increasingly match human cognitive abilities
- Consumer research has typically failed to differentiate between various forms of automation—from simple algorithms to AI—using the terms interchangeably, which limits scholarly understanding
- Consumer past consumption variety and other psychological predispositions influence receptiveness to personalized recommendations and algorithmic decision support

## Topics Covered

Automation of marketing activities, automation of consumption, recommender systems, large language models (LLMs), consumer psychology and AI, human exceptionalism in the age of AI, algorithmic decision support, AI and consumer well-being, personalization and consumer preferences, expressive range and AI constraints, research methodology in consumer-AI studies, emotional intelligence versus cognitive abilities
