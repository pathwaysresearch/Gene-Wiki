# Ideation With Generative Ai (Published) 75Dccafd 9C43 46F5 9F56 06A11Da7A7Cf

## Abstract

This academic paper by Julian De Freitas, Gideon Nave, and Stefano Puntoni explores the use of generative AI (genAI), particularly large language models (LLMs), as tools for creative ideation in consumer research and beyond. Drawing on the human creativity literature, the authors argue that LLM ideation effectiveness stems from two capabilities—productivity (analogous to human persistence) and semantic breadth (analogous to human flexibility)—and propose a framework of metaphorical "ideation roles" (e.g., Designer, Writer) that distinguish between genAI as key ideator versus humans as key ideator. The paper provides practical prompting interventions, discusses levels of automation in human-AI ideation, and addresses techniques such as retrieval-augmented generation and temperature manipulation for enhancing creative output.

## Key Claims

- LLM ideation capabilities mirror two established dual pathways of human creativity: persistence (via productivity) and flexibility (via semantic breadth)
- The number of original ideas generated by an LLM rises with volume but plateaus (e.g., after ~500 ideas), indicating exhaustion of the persistence approach for a given prompt
- Semantic breadth enables LLMs to generate ideas spanning widely different concepts, analogous to flexibility in human creativity where connecting disparate ideas leads to novelty
- The authors propose distinct "ideation roles" for LLMs—including Designer and Writer—conceptualizing varying levels of automation from genAI as primary ideator to human as primary ideator
- Prompting techniques (e.g., low/medium/high temperature prompts) can influence idea originality, though the correlation between the literal temperature parameter setting and originality appears weak; LLMs also benefit from few-shot exemplars and retrieval-augmented generation for producing specialized ideas
