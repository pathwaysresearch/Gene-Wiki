# AI Needs Clinical Trials: Harvard's Findings on Democratization | Karim R. Lakhani | TEDxBoston

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

Harvard Business School professor Karim R. Lakhani presents research from Harvard's Digital Data Design Institute on conducting rigorous "clinical trials" of generative AI in the workplace at organizations like BCG and Procter & Gamble. The talk argues that GenAI is akin to a drug introduced to hundreds of millions of people without understanding its proper dose, efficacy, or side effects, and that controlled studies are essential to understand its real impact on knowledge workers. Key findings reveal that AI lowers the cost of expertise—enabling average performers to reach 95th percentile levels and giving individuals cross-disciplinary knowledge previously requiring entire teams—while also warning of a widening gap between AI's exponential capability growth and organizations' linear absorption capacity.

## Key Claims

- Generative AI functions like a drug introduced at massive scale without adequate understanding of dosage, efficacy, side effects, or toxicity, necessitating clinical-trial-style research
- AI lowers the cost of expertise, enabling average performers to achieve 95th percentile performance levels
- AI gives individuals access to cross-disciplinary knowledge that previously required entire teams, democratizing expertise
- AI is evolving from a tool to a teammate, providing coordination capabilities analogous to those of human teams
- AI model performance increases exponentially (every 6-9 months), but organizational absorption of AI capabilities remains linear, creating a dangerous and widening gap
- New jobs will emerge alongside AI transformation (e.g., Amazon warehouse "robot wranglers"), and AI agents will proliferate as teammates in the workplace
