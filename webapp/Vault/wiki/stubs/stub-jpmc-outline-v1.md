# Jpmc Outline V1

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This document is a detailed curriculum outline for a JPMC (JPMorgan Chase) AI Leadership program, structured into multiple modules and sessions. It covers how leaders should work with AI at the individual, team, and organizational levels — spanning topics from task-level AI augmentation to AI governance, fairness, risk management, and the economics of AI-driven process redesign. The program is designed to equip financial services leaders with frameworks for adopting, governing, and strategically leveraging AI within their functions.

## Key Claims

- AI's impact should be understood at the task level using a prediction-vs-judgment decomposition, mapping roles onto an automate/augment/innovate/leave-alone framework
- The capability boundary of AI (the "Jagged Frontier") is usually unclear, and crossing it in the wrong direction costs more than not using AI at all
- There is an important distinction between passive use and cognitively engaged use of AI
- AI fairness decisions require applying three fairness notions and specifying what evidence is needed to act with confidence
- AI in financial services operates across overlapping regulatory regimes with four layers of risk: model, operational, compliance, and strategic/reputational
- When AI collapses the cost of prediction, the unit economics of every process built on prediction change fundamentally
- Process redesign with AI is distinct from mere process automation and demands data pipelines, algorithmic decision loops, and continuous experimentation (the "AI factory model")

## Topics Covered

AI task-level augmentation vs. substitution, prediction-vs-judgment decomposition, Jagged Frontier exercise, AI fairness and bias in decision-making, AI governance operating model design, AI risk and regulatory landscape in financial services, process automation vs. process redesign, AI factory model, unit economics of AI, organizational competitive position, new work emerging from AI redesign, cognitively engaged AI use
