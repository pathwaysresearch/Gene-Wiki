# How Gen AI Is Revolutionizing Market Research With Stefano Puntoni

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

Stefano Puntoni (Co-Director, Wharton Human-AI Research) and Nick Graham (Founder, Vertemis) discuss how generative AI should move market research beyond mere efficiency gains toward enabling entirely new capabilities. They argue that GenAI's true value lies not in doing today's work faster but in enabling "better things"—including just-in-time insights, synthetic data as a substitute for missing data, and reaching hard-to-sample or restricted audiences. The conversation covers hybrid approaches combining human and synthetic inputs, the distinction between synthetic personas and real respondents, identity threat among researchers, and the Lewinian framework for understanding when synthetic data is most reliable.

## Key Claims

- GenAI's full benefits in market research require doing things differently, not just shaving off time from existing processes
- Synthetic data should be framed as an alternative to "no data" rather than a replacement for real human data
- GenAI enables access to hard-to-sample or restricted audiences that traditional research methods cannot reach
- Use cases involving eliciting beliefs (e.g., replicating surveys about beliefs) are most likely to work well with synthetic approaches, based on the Lewinian equation distinguishing person-side vs. situation-side factors
- Hybrid approaches combining human and synthetic inputs are recommended over fully replacing human respondents
