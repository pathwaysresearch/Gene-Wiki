# Automation and New Tasks: How Technology Displaces and Reinstates Labor

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This paper by Daron Acemoglu and Pascual Restrepo (Journal of Economic Perspectives, Spring 2019) presents a task-based framework for understanding how technological change affects employment and wages. The framework decomposes the effects of technology into automation (which displaces labor from existing tasks), the creation of new labor-intensive tasks (which reinstates labor demand), and factor-augmenting advances. The authors formalize how the displacement effect of automation reduces labor's share while the introduction of new tasks counteracts this, showing that roughly half of employment growth over 1980–2015 occurred in occupations with new job titles or tasks, making this framework essential for understanding modern labor market dynamics.

## Key Claims

- Automation enables tasks previously performed by labor to be produced by capital, generating both a productivity effect (raising overall value added and thus labor demand) and a displacement effect (shifting tasks away from labor, reducing labor share)
- The introduction of new labor-intensive tasks is a major countervailing force that reinstates labor demand and can offset the displacement effect of automation
- About half of employment growth over 1980–2015 took place in occupations in which job titles or tasks performed by workers were new, based on data from Lin (2011)
- Output can be represented as a CES (constant elasticity of substitution) function of capital and labor, with task allocation boundaries determining the shares of automated versus non-automated tasks
- New labor-intensive tasks span a wide range including software/app development, database design and analysis, computer security, and more specialized functions in existing occupations such as loan application analysis and medical equipment technician roles
