# Economics Manag Strategy 2024 Mcelheran Ai Adoption In America Who What And Where

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This paper by McElheran, Li, Brynjolfsson, Kroff, Dinlersoz, Foster, and Zolas (published in the Journal of Economics & Management Strategy, 2024) characterizes AI adoption across the U.S. economy using the largest and most representative firm-level data to date, drawn from U.S. Census Bureau sources. It examines who adopts AI, what specific AI technologies are adopted, and where adoption is concentrated geographically and by sector. The study finds that as of 2017, just under 6% of U.S. firms used AI, but adoption was heavily concentrated among very large firms, with employment-weighted adoption at over 18%. The paper disentangles commonly confounded firm characteristics (size, age, industry, technological sophistication) and explores the role of enabling technologies like cloud computing and data availability in AI diffusion.

## Key Claims

- Just under 6% of U.S. firms used AI as of 2017, but most very large firms (over 5,000 employees) reported at least some AI use, and employment-weighted adoption was over 18%
- AI adoption intensity varied from merely testing (1.1%) to using AI in more than a quarter of production (2.2%), with more-intensive use more prevalent among very large firms
- Manufacturing and information were leading sectors with adoption rates of roughly 12% each, yet AI was present across every sector of the U.S. economy
- Enabling technologies such as data availability and cloud computing are important interdependencies shaping AI diffusion and should not be studied in isolation from AI adoption
- Capital intensity, startup innovation strategies, and business strategy dimensions help disentangle financing, firm quality, and growth potential as factors in AI adoption decisions
