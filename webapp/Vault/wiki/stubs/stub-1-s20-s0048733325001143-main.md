# Artificial Intelligence, Tasks, Skills, and Wages: Worker-Level Evidence from Germany

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This paper (Engberg, Koch, Lodefalk, & Schroeder, published in *Research Policy* 54, 2025) examines how artificial intelligence technologies are linked to changes in the detailed task content, skill requirements, and wages of workers using uniquely granular and representative worker-level data from Germany. Drawing on the task-based framework of Autor et al. (2003), Acemoglu and Autor (2011), and Acemoglu and Restrepo (2019), the authors analyze displacement, productivity, and reinstatement effects of AI on occupations. The paper uses an AI occupational exposure measure from Engberg et al. (2024) and detailed ISCO-08 occupation classifications, contributing rare empirical evidence on how AI reshapes work content within narrowly defined occupations. It addresses a significant gap in the literature caused by the prior absence of granular individual- and firm-level data on AI's labor market impacts.

## Key Claims

- AI reshapes the task content and skill requirements within narrowly defined occupations, and the paper provides worker-level empirical evidence for these changes
- The framework identifies three main effects of new technologies on labor: displacement (substitution of labor by capital), productivity (increased demand for non-automated tasks), and reinstatement (creation of new tasks)
- The relative importance of displacement, productivity, and reinstatement effects determines AI's net impact on wages
- Prior empirical evidence on AI's effects on work content and wages has been limited due to lack of granular data, and research has relied on proxies like online job ads with additional assumptions and limitations
- The study leverages representative German worker-level data and an AI occupational exposure measure, avoiding limitations of approaches based on online job ads data
