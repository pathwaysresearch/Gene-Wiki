# Consumers And Artificial Intelligence

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This is a 2021 article published in the *Journal of Marketing* (Vol. 85, Issue 1) by Stefano Puntoni, Rebecca Walker Reczek, Markus Giesler, and Simona Botti, part of the JM-MSI Special Issue on marketing priorities. The paper conceptualizes AI as an ecosystem of data collection, computational techniques, and output systems, and develops an experiential framework examining how consumers psychologically and sociologically experience AI across capabilities such as listening, predicting, producing, and communicating. It identifies key psychological tensions (e.g., feeling served vs. exploited) and contextual factors (e.g., surveillance society) that shape consumer reactions to AI, and proposes a comprehensive research agenda with specific emerging research questions for the marketing discipline.

## Key Claims

- AI can be conceptualized as an ecosystem comprising three elements: data collection/storage, statistical/computational techniques, and output systems that perform tasks typically requiring intelligence
- AI capabilities map to four functions: listening, predicting, producing, and communicating, each generating distinct consumer experiences
- Data capture experiences create a psychological tension where consumers recognize AI's ability to serve them through customization but simultaneously feel exploited due to AI's lack of transparency
- Feelings of exploitation in data capture are fueled by actual and perceived loss of personal control, leading to negative affect, demotivation, and helplessness
- Socioeconomic status, cultural norms, intersectionality, and the nature of data collected (environmental, behavioral, physiological) all influence the likelihood and intensity of feeling exploited

## Topics Covered

Consumer AI experience, data capture and surveillance, psychological tensions (served vs. exploited), AI capabilities (listening, predicting, producing, communicating), loss of personal control, customization and personalization, sociological context of surveillance society, algorithmic prediction and preference satisfaction, emerging marketing research questions, consumer affect and helplessness, cultural and socioeconomic influences on AI perception, AI transparency
