## Abstract

This is Episode 21 of The IJK Podcast (June 2025), in which host Imad Jack Karam interviews Professor Kartik Hosanagar of the Wharton School about how AI algorithms invisibly shape human choices, from online content consumption to purchasing decisions. Hosanagar, author of *A Human's Guide to Machine Intelligence* and host of the *Creative Intelligence* podcast, discusses filter bubbles, algorithmic nudging, the ethical challenges of AI, and the emerging era of autonomous AI agents. The conversation explores the societal implications of algorithmic decision-making, including effects on voter behavior and political polarization.

## Key Claims

- Filter bubbles function as feedback loops: the more a user engages with certain content or viewpoints, the more the algorithm reinforces that pattern, narrowing exposure to diverse perspectives
- A study showed that nudging social media users to see more hard news stories increased voter turnout by a margin potentially large enough to affect election outcomes
- Algorithms are not just recommending content but increasingly making decisions on behalf of consumers and businesses
- AI agents are evolving toward a model where they autonomously research, curate, and even complete purchases on a user's behalf without requiring explicit approval
- Algorithms can be designed to introduce diverse perspectives and break filter bubbles, but current implementations often fail to do so effectively
