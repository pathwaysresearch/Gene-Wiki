# 342 The Secrets To High Ai Adoption With Stefano Puntoni Professor At Wharton

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This is an episode of DataCamp's Data Framed podcast (published 2026-01-19) featuring Stefano Puntoni, Sebastian S. Kresge Professor of Marketing at Wharton and co-director of Wharton's Human-AI research department. The conversation explores the current state of enterprise AI adoption based on a Wharton report, examining why adoption rates vary across functions and company sizes, and what separates organizations that successfully integrate AI from those that struggle. Puntoni discusses the psychological factors—identity, autonomy, and perceived threat—that shape worker responses to AI, argues that CEO narratives around AI are "uniquely uninspiring" when focused solely on cost-cutting, and advocates for more ambitious visions of what AI can deliver beyond productivity gains.

## Key Claims

- An MIT report suggested 95% of AI initiatives never make it from prototype to production, prompting the Wharton team to conduct their own adoption study
- IT professionals are furthest ahead in AI adoption, with about 80% reporting daily use, while other functions like marketing lag significantly behind
- Technology creates opportunities alongside threats, but opportunities are less apparent early on because people must invent new jobs and discover the value of human expertise
- CEO proclamations about AI are often tailored to investors and focused on cost-cutting, which is uninspiring and insufficient as a vision for the technology
- Psychological factors including identity, autonomy, and perceived job threat significantly influence how workers respond to AI implementation and whether they resist or embrace it
