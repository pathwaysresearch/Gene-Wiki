# Occupational, Industry, and Geographic Exposure to Artificial Intelligence: A Novel Dataset and Its Potential Uses

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This research article by Edward Felten (Princeton), Manav Raj, and Robert Seamans (NYU Stern), published in the Strategic Management Journal, introduces a novel measure called the AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE) index that quantifies how much different occupations are exposed to artificial intelligence. The methodology links 10 AI applications tracked by the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) to 52 O*NET occupational abilities via crowd-sourced survey data from Amazon Mechanical Turk, then aggregates these scores to the occupation, industry, and geographic levels. The dataset is publicly available on GitHub and is designed to enable strategy and economics researchers to study the labor market and firm-level impacts of AI advancement.

## Key Claims

- The AIOE measure links AI applications to human occupational abilities to create a quantitative index of AI exposure at the occupation level
- The methodology uses EFF-tracked progress across 10 specific AI applications matched to 52 O*NET occupational abilities via mTurk surveys
- Ability-level AI exposure is calculated as the equally weighted sum of application-ability relatedness scores across all 10 AI applications
- Occupations with similar aggregate AI exposure (e.g., surgeons and physicists) can have very different patterns of exposure because they rely on different portfolios of abilities (cognitive, physical, etc.)
- The measure can be extended to construct AI exposure at the firm level and complements existing datasets that focus on how AI substitutes or complements workplace abilities
