# Not All Creativity Is Worth Saving With David Droga

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This episode of Creative Intelligence features host Kartik Hosanagar (Wharton professor) in conversation with David Droga, CEO of Accenture Song and founder of Droga5, exploring how AI is reshaping the creative and advertising industries. Droga argues that not all creativity is worth preserving—AI can help get past mediocre work faster—and that the real value lies in originality rather than mere efficiency. The discussion covers the evolving role of CMOs, the future of ad agencies, why creativity and technology must move hand in hand, and why the best creative professionals today must also be doers.

## Key Claims

- AI will change creative jobs but not the fundamental necessity for communication, brand, design, and storytelling
- Both expensive traditional creative work and cheap AI-generated replicas can be equally mediocre—AI's "parlor trick" of fast replication is not the ultimate value proposition
- The advantage of AI in creative work is that you can "get to crap faster" and move on, accelerating iteration rather than replacing originality
- The CMO role was already morphing before AI, shifting from growing brand to growing companies, and now carries more weight in the C-suite
- Droga sold Droga5 to Accenture because of his belief in the necessity of technology to weave together all facets of the creative world
