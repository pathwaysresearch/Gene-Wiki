# Rise of AI: How AI Shapes Human Identity — Wharton Prof. Stefano Puntoni (Ripple Effect Podcast)

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This is a Knowledge at Wharton Ripple Effect podcast episode (May 2023) featuring Wharton marketing professor Stefano Puntoni discussing how artificial intelligence reshapes human identity, consumer behavior, and the labor force. Puntoni argues that AI should play a complementary role in human lives rather than being an all-or-nothing proposition, and that the more interesting question is not what people think of AI but how AI changes the way people think about themselves. The conversation also addresses the significance of ChatGPT's launch as a watershed moment for mass AI adoption and the risks of deskilling through automation.

## Key Claims

- The most important question about AI is not consumer acceptance of the technology but how AI changes the way humans think about themselves and their identity
- There is a real risk of deskilling as automation replaces tasks humans previously performed, following a "use it or lose it" principle observed in professional contexts like airline operations
- AI triggers socio-emotional processes of feeling replaced, which people may find highly aversive and even threatening
- ChatGPT's launch in November 2022 was a pivotal moment that democratized access to generative AI, requiring only an email address and a three-minute registration
- Younger generations are expected to normalize AI as a standard part of life, though the topic remains universally recognized as a momentous and historical change
