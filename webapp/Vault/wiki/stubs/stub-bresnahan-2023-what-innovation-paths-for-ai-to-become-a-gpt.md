# Bresnahan 2023 What Innovation Paths For Ai To Become A Gpt

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

Timothy Bresnahan (Stanford) examines the innovation paths through which artificial intelligence technologies (AITs) might become a General Purpose Technology (GPT), triggering broad economic growth. He argues that while early commercial AI applications—particularly by Internet Giants—were narrow but extremely profitable, extending these successes economy-wide faces substantial obstacles including low visibility of enterprise production systems, organizational adjustment costs (OACs), and the difficulty of digital transformation. The paper evaluates multiple adoption mechanisms (job automation, consumer-product matching, digital tools/augmentation) and concludes that augmentation of existing workers and incremental integration into enterprise software may be more realistic paths than transformative automation, though these paths are slower to ignite a GPT loop.

## Key Claims

- Early AI applications were narrow but highly profitable; comparable deployment across the economy would produce a growth boom, but diffusion faces major barriers
- AIT-based product/consumer matching systems worked well in low-stakes contexts but poorly when stakes were higher, limiting their range of application
- The job-automation path is unpromising because enterprise production systems lack visibility, organizational structures are poorly understood, and digital transformation remains difficult
- Supply-side advances in AITs (improved technologies, development platforms, engineering talent, entrepreneurship) were impressive, but the hard part remains deciding specifically what commercial systems should do
- Augmentation of individual workers with AI-powered digital tools follows a longstanding pattern of incremental enterprise computing adoption and is more feasible than organizational-level transformation, though less transformative
