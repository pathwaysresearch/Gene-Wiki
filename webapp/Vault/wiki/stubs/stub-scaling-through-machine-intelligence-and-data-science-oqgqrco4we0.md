# Scaling Through Machine Intelligence And Data Science Oqgqrco4We0

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This is a Wharton Scale School event (the ninth in the series) hosted at the Wharton San Francisco campus, focused on how companies can scale operations, customer acquisition, servicing, and retention through machine intelligence and data science. The session features panelists with firsthand experience, including a co-founder of Yodle who built algorithmic marketing for small businesses and faced early skepticism about scalability. The discussion covers practical strategies for embedding AI/ML across organizations—including decentralizing machine learning knowledge and launching many small AI initiatives—as well as the ethical challenges of AI bias, illustrated by Amazon's gender-biased hiring algorithm.

## Key Claims

- Activities that work well at the startup stage (e.g., close founder-customer interactions) become too expensive as companies grow and must be automated through machine intelligence
- Early VCs dismissed Yodle's small-business marketing platform as "not scalable," despite its algorithmic approach to keyword bidding and ad placement
- Successful AI adoption at scale involves decentralizing machine learning knowledge across product teams rather than keeping it centralized in one team
- Organizations benefit from mandating that every team launch some AI initiative—even without clear ROI—to build organizational competency in gathering and analyzing data
- Amazon's AI-driven recruiting system had to be shut down (or at least not rolled out) because gender bias from historical data could not be adequately removed from the machine learning model
