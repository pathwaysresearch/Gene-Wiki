# Prediction Machines: The Simple Economics Of Artificial Intelligence By Ajay Agrawal

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This book reframes artificial intelligence through the lens of economics, arguing that AI is fundamentally a prediction technology — and that as prediction becomes cheap, it transforms decision-making, business strategy, and the economy. The authors contend that just as the internet made communication, distribution, and search cheap, AI is making prediction dramatically cheaper, with profound implications for judgment, labor, and organizational design. The book is structured to serve business leaders, students, investors, and policy makers with frameworks for understanding AI's economic impact. It draws on historical analogies (such as how cheap arithmetic from semiconductors reshaped industries) to demystify AI and ground it in standard economic reasoning.

## Key Claims

- AI is best understood not as general intelligence but as a dramatic drop in the cost of prediction, which follows standard economic principles of how cheap inputs reshape industries
- When prediction becomes cheap, the value of complements — especially human judgment — rises, creating a new division of labor between machines and humans
- Decisions can be unpacked into component parts (prediction, judgment, data, action), and AI's role is specifically in the prediction component
- The economic framework of cheap prediction applies broadly across business strategy, career planning, investment analysis, and public policy
- Despite hype around AI, Lady Lovelace's Objection still holds relevance: computers do what we order them to perform and do not originate anything autonomously

## Topics Covered

Prediction as the core economic function of AI, the economics of cheap inputs and complements, decision-making frameworks (prediction vs. judgment), the role of data as a key AI input, fully automated decision making, the new division of labor between humans and machines, AI's impact on business strategy and management, career and job evolution in an AI economy, AI investment and venture capital frameworks, AI policy and societal implications, taming complexity through AI, historical analogies for technological disruption (internet, semiconductors)
