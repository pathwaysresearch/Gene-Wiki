## Abstract

This is a conversation between Wharton professor Kartik Hosanagar and New York Times reporter Cade Metz, author of *Genius Makers*, recorded live at Wharton's San Francisco campus as the second episode of the Creative Intelligence podcast. The discussion traces the personal stories and philosophies of the key researchers behind modern AI—including Geoffrey Hinton and Demis Hassabis—exploring how their convictions, rivalries, and career decisions shaped the trajectory of deep learning and generative AI. The episode also touches on the role of AI in art and journalism, the dramatic corporate bidding wars for AI talent, and the seemingly contradictory viewpoints held by today's AI leaders.

## Key Claims

- Geoffrey Hinton and other deep learning pioneers persisted with neural network approaches at a time when the mainstream AI community favored logic-based and other non-neural-network methods
- A bidding war among major tech companies (including Google and Microsoft) for Hinton and two colleagues escalated to $44 million over two days, ending only because Hinton chose to go to Google
- Technology is not neutral—it reflects the philosophies, assumptions, and goals of its creators, shaping how it organizes power and meaning (citing researcher Langdon Winner)
- Demis Hassabis, a chess player and DeepMind founder, is characterized as someone who thinks strategically several moves ahead, including in a notable exchange with Elon Musk about colonizing Mars to escape Earth's dangers
- DeepMind's work on AlphaFold (protein structures) and AlphaGo (defeating Go world champion Lee Sedol) led to Nobel-level recognition in chemistry
