## Abstract

A conversation between Dr. David Maslach (R3ciprocity Project) and Professor Karim Lakhani (Harvard Business School) exploring the science of science, biases in scientific funding, and the case for lottery-based grant allocation. Lakhani draws on his research into how scientific communities simultaneously collaborate and compete, revealing systemic biases against novel ideas in traditional peer review. The discussion argues that lottery-based funding could counteract novelty bias and empower scientists to pursue more innovative research, while also touching on time allocation studies, social comparison among scientists, and COVID-era research collaboration.

## Key Claims

- Scientific communities exhibit dual dynamics of competition and collaboration — competing for grants, lab space, and discovery priority while cooperating on research
- Traditional peer review and grant funding systems contain a systematic bias against novel ideas, effectively truncating the distribution of funded innovations
- Lottery-based funding systems could correct novelty bias by not assuming that unfamiliar or unprecedented ideas are inherently low-quality
- Bias against novelty assumes novel ideas are left-tail distributed, but they may be normally distributed; the bias itself chops off potentially high-value work
- Time allocation studies of scientists (analogous to CEO time allocation research by Rafael Sadun) can reveal how inputs to science are distributed and where inefficiencies lie
