# Noy Zhang 1

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This is a 2023 working paper by Shakked Noy and Whitney Zhang (MIT) presenting experimental evidence on the productivity effects of ChatGPT on mid-level professional writing tasks. In a preregistered online experiment with 444 college-educated professionals across six occupations, randomly exposing half to ChatGPT reduced task completion time by 0.8 standard deviations and increased output quality by 0.4 standard deviations. The study finds that ChatGPT compresses the productivity distribution by disproportionately benefiting lower-ability workers, reducing inequality. It also investigates human-machine complementarity, examining whether ChatGPT substitutes for or complements worker effort, and how it reshapes the structure of writing tasks (shifting time from rough-drafting to editing).

## Key Claims

- ChatGPT substantially raises average productivity: time taken decreases by 0.8 SDs and output quality rises by 0.4 SDs
- Inequality between workers decreases because ChatGPT compresses the productivity distribution by benefiting low-ability workers more
- ChatGPT shifts the structure of writing tasks: time spent on rough-drafting drops significantly (from ~55% to ~30%) while time spent on editing increases (from ~25% to ~40%)
- ChatGPT could function as either a substitute for worker effort (producing satisfactory output directly) or a complement to worker skills (e.g., aiding brainstorming or producing rough drafts for human editing)
- The experiment uses incentivized, occupation-specific writing tasks (press releases, short reports, analysis plans, delicate emails) rated on a 1-7 scale, with participants from marketing, grant writing, consulting, data analysis, HR, and management
