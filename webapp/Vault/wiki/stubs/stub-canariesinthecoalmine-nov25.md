# Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This November 2025 paper by Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar, and Ruyu Chen uses high-frequency administrative payroll data from ADP to document six facts about labor market shifts following the widespread adoption of generative AI. The central finding is that early-career workers (ages 22-25) in AI-exposed occupations experienced 16% relative employment declines, while experienced workers remained stable. The paper distinguishes between automating and augmenting uses of AI, finding that employment declines concentrate in automating applications while augmenting applications are associated with employment growth — making early-career workers potential "canaries in the coal mine" for broader AI-driven labor market disruption.

## Key Claims

- Early-career workers (ages 22-25) in AI-exposed occupations experienced 16% relative employment declines, controlling for firm-level shocks, while employment for experienced workers remained stable
- Labor market adjustments occur primarily via employment levels rather than compensation changes
- Employment declines are concentrated in occupations where AI automates rather than augments labor; augmentative AI uses are associated with employment growth
- The automation vs. augmentation distinction is measured empirically using observed queries to Claude (the LLM) and the extent to which they substitute or complement occupational tasks
- Results are robust to excluding technology firms and to excluding occupations that are remotable, suggesting the effects are not driven by tech-sector dynamics or remote work trends alone
