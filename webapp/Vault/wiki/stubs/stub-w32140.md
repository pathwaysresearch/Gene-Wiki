## Abstract

This is NBER Working Paper 32140 by David Autor (February 2024), titled "Applying AI to Rebuild Middle Class Jobs" (published in NOEMA Magazine as "How AI Could Help Rebuild The Middle Class"). The paper argues that AI, unlike previous waves of automation that hollowed out middle-class employment by routinizing tasks, has the potential to democratize expert judgment and thereby restore middle-skill, middle-class occupations. It traces the historical arc from artisanal expertise through mass production to computerization, showing how each technological wave reshaped the value of different forms of human knowledge, and contends that AI's capacity for improvisation and tacit judgment could be deployed to augment rather than replace workers in decision-making roles.

## Key Claims

- Expertise is in constant flux: skills that once commanded market premiums (farriery, typesetting, spell-checking) have been antiquated or automated, while new high-value expertise continually emerges
- Mass production in the 18th–19th centuries decimated artisanal expertise by breaking complex work into discrete, simple, mechanistic steps carried out by production workers aided by machinery
- Digital computing eroded the value of "mass expertise" (tools and rules) just as the Industrial Revolution eroded artisanal expertise, but tasks requiring tacit knowledge (Polanyi's paradox) remained resistant to automation
- AI's capacity to depart from script and improvise based on training enables it to engage in expert judgment — a capability previously reserved for elite experts — and this will become a near-ubiquitous presence in working lives
- AI's primary role will be to advise, coach, and alert decision-makers as they apply expert judgment, potentially expanding access to expertise and rebuilding middle-class jobs
