# Competing in the Age of AI: Strategy and Leadership When Algorithms and Networks Run the World

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This book by Marco Iansiti and Karim R. Lakhani (Harvard Business Review Press, 2020) examines how artificial intelligence, algorithms, and digital networks are fundamentally transforming the nature of firms, industries, and the broader economy. The authors argue that firms designed and architected to fully leverage digital networks, data, algorithms, and AI unlock unprecedented potential for scale, scope, and learning in their operating models. The book explores how even "weak AI"—imperfect but practical—is already sufficient to reshape how enterprises operate, compete, and create value, and it provides strategic and leadership frameworks for navigating this transformation.

## Key Claims

- Even relatively basic ("weak") AI is enough to transform the nature of firms and how industries function, without requiring human-level artificial general intelligence.
- Digital representations of human activities can learn and improve in ways that traditional analog processes cannot, fundamentally changing how firms should operate.
- Firms that are designed to optimize the impact of digitization achieve greater potential for scale, scope, and learning—and create and capture more value.
- The intrinsic scalability, scope amplification, and learning potential of technology was historically limited by organizational architecture, but a new generation of firms has emerged to release that full potential.
- AI-driven algorithms (such as Amazon's product recommendation system) overcome the limitations of traditional human-driven processes like staffing, expertise constraints, and personnel budgets.
