# Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of AI on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This paper, published in *Organization Science* (INFORMS), presents a large-scale field experiment with 758 Boston Consulting Group (BCG) consultants examining how access to AI (large language models) affects knowledge worker productivity and quality. The authors introduce the concept of a "jagged technological frontier" — the idea that AI capabilities are uneven, excelling at some tasks while failing at others in unpredictable ways. The study tests two distinct sets of realistic consulting tasks: one within and one beyond the current frontier of AI capabilities, finding that AI integration can both significantly benefit and harm performance depending on task type. The research was funded by Harvard Business School and involved tasks designed by senior BCG professionals to replicate real-world consulting assignments.

## Key Claims

- AI capabilities form a "jagged frontier" where LLMs have surprising strengths in some areas and unexpected weaknesses in others, making it difficult for knowledge workers to predict AI's value ahead of time
- The experiment tested 758 elite BCG consultants (~7% of their individual-contributor consulting workforce), randomly assigned to conditions involving tasks within or beyond AI's capability frontier
- Tasks were designed by senior BCG professionals and validated as representative of real daily consulting activities, including some used in company screening processes
- Knowledge work involves interdependent tasks, some of which fit current LLM capabilities well while others do not, creating complex dynamics when AI is integrated into workflows
- Organizations may face negative outcomes when AI is applied to tasks beyond the frontier of its capabilities, not just benefits from tasks within the frontier
