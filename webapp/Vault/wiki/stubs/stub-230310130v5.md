# 2303.10130V5

## Abstract

This paper by Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin, and Rock (OpenAI / University of Pennsylvania, 2023) investigates the potential implications of large language models (LLMs), particularly GPTs, on the U.S. labor market. Using a novel rubric combining human expertise and GPT-4 classifications, the authors assess occupations based on their alignment with LLM capabilities, finding that approximately 80% of the U.S. workforce could have at least 10% of their work tasks affected by LLMs. The paper draws a deliberate analogy between Generative Pre-trained Transformers and general-purpose technologies (hence "GPTs are GPTs"), arguing that much of LLMs' economic potential will emerge across a broad range of use cases, and it distinguishes between the impact of LLMs alone versus LLM-powered software.

## Key Claims

- Around 80% of the U.S. workforce could have at least 10% of their work tasks exposed to LLM capabilities
- LLM-powered software extends the impact potential significantly beyond what standalone LLMs can achieve
- Human annotations and GPT-4 classifications show similar agreement levels when aggregated to the task level for measuring occupation exposure
- LLMs function as general-purpose technologies whose impact will emerge across a broad range of economically valuable use cases, including the creation of new types of work
- The research measures what is technically feasible now but acknowledges it will necessarily miss the evolving impact potential of LLMs over time
