# Rethinking the AI Stack: Data, Agentic AI, and Organizational Design – Wharton Global Forum

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

A panel discussion from the 2025 Wharton Global Forum in San Francisco, moderated by Kartik Hosanagar, featuring leaders from Microsoft, T. Rowe Price, GrowthCurve Capital, NewFront, and HSG (formerly Sequoia China). The conversation explores where AI is headed, the "Gen AI paradox" of widespread adoption without clear ROI, debates around AGI timelines and definitions, and how products, teams, and organizations must be reimagined for an AI-first world. The panel draws parallels to historical IT productivity lags (the Solow Paradox) and discusses practical strategies like "thin product, thick model" design and building for agentic AI.

## Key Claims

- AI will progressively replace more tasks, workflows, jobs, and potentially subsectors of industries, but AGI timelines depend heavily on how rigorously you define AGI
- The "Gen AI paradox" describes a situation where AI adoption is widespread but measurable ROI remains elusive, similar to the Solow Paradox of the 1980s where computers were everywhere except in the productivity statistics
- Historical research (by Wharton's Lorin Hitt and Stanford's Erik Brynjolfsson) showed IT investment returns took roughly a decade to materialize, suggesting organizations should continue investing in AI despite early problems
- Product design should shift to a "model first" approach — "thin product, thick model" — where products are built not just for human users but also for AI agents and LLMs
- Building products for agentic AI is a greenfield opportunity with no established playbook, requiring a learning-by-doing approach
