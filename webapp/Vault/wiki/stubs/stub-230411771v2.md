# Generative AI at Work (2304.11771V2)

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This paper by Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, and Lindsey Raymond (Stanford, MIT, NBER) studies the impact of a generative AI tool deployed in a customer support contact center setting. It examines how AI assistance affects worker productivity, with particular attention to heterogeneous effects across worker skill levels, and also investigates how AI changes agents' experience of work—including customer sentiment, supervisor escalation requests, and worker attrition. The study contributes to a growing literature on human-AI collaboration and the real-world labor market effects of large language models.

## Key Claims

- Access to a generative AI assistant improves customer support agent productivity, with the largest gains accruing to less experienced and lower-skilled workers
- AI assistance helps compress the skill distribution by enabling lower-skilled workers to perform more like higher-skilled workers
- AI assistance markedly improves how customers treat agents, as measured by the sentiment of customer chat messages
- Customers are less likely to request to speak to a supervisor (questioning agent competence) when agents have AI assistance
- AI access is associated with decreased worker attrition, driven by improvements for the least experienced agents
