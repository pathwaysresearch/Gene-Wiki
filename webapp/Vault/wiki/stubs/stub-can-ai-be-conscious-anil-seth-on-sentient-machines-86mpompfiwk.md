# Can AI Be Conscious? Anil Seth on Sentient Machines

**Type**: RAG stub — full content in vector index, not in wiki

## Abstract

This episode of Creative Intelligence features a conversation between host Kartik Hosanagar (Wharton School professor and faculty co-director of Wharton Human-AI Research) and neuroscientist Anil Seth (author of *Being You*) exploring whether machines can ever be conscious. They discuss what consciousness actually means, the "hard problem" of how physical brain processes give rise to subjective experience, and how current AI systems like LLMs relate (or don't) to consciousness. The conversation bridges neuroscience, philosophy, and AI ethics, examining why the absence of a clear understanding of consciousness makes it profoundly difficult to determine whether AI could ever be sentient.

## Key Claims

- LLMs are not believed to have consciousness, and transformer models are not seen as a path toward achieving it
- The experienced world is a construction by the brain, closely tied to real things happening in the world and body, not a direct readout of reality
- The core challenge of consciousness (the "hard problem") is explaining how physical processes in the brain give rise to subjective experience — independent of questions about perception
- Without a checklist or mechanistic understanding of how processes produce consciousness, answering whether AI is or could be conscious remains fundamentally difficult
- LLMs can perform information processing, predictive modeling, and narrative construction, but whether they have beliefs or genuine experience is unclear and contested, with thinkers like Yosha Bach offering alternative perspectives
