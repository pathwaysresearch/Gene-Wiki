# J. R. Lucas

## Overview
The text identifies J. R. Lucas as a philosopher who, in 1961, put forth a significant claim that machines are mentally inferior to humans.

## Argument from Gödel's Theorem
Lucas's central argument is that computers, being formal systems, are fundamentally limited by Gödel's incompleteness theorem. He asserted that a machine cannot establish the truth of its own "Gödel sentence," while a human mind can, thus demonstrating a capability beyond any formal system.

## Legacy and Rebuttals
This claim is noted to have sparked decades of controversy and a vast literature on the topic. The text highlights the enduring nature of the debate but also presents direct rebuttals to Lucas's position, including one that challenges the applicability of the theorem to finite computers and another that constructs a logical paradox to show that humans also face limitations in what they can consistently assert.
