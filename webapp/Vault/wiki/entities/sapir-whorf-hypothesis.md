# Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis

## Overview
The Sapir-Whorf hypothesis is a well-known claim from linguistics which posits that a person's understanding of the world is heavily influenced by the language they speak. It suggests that people categorize and organize their perceptions of nature according to agreements that are codified within the patterns of their specific language community.

## Core Tenet
The text quotes Whorf (1956) to summarize the hypothesis: “We cut nature up, organize it into concepts, and ascribe significances as we do, largely because we are parties to an agreement to organize it this way—an agreement that holds throughout our speech community and is codified in the patterns of our language.” This highlights the idea that language is not just a tool for expressing thought, but a framework that shapes it.

## Context in AI
The hypothesis is discussed in the context of the limitations of natural language as a formal representation language for AI. While it is true that different languages divide the world differently (e.g., French has “chaise” and “fauteuil” for the English “chair”), the text questions the strong form of the hypothesis by noting that speakers of one language can still form and name concepts from another, suggesting that thought is not entirely constrained by words.
