# Galton Board

## Overview
The Galton board is a pinball-like apparatus where balls are dropped from the top and bounce randomly left or right off a series of pins. The balls accumulate in bins at the bottom, forming a bell-shaped curve, also known as the normal distribution.

## Demonstration of Statistical Principles
The device serves as a visual demonstration of the central limit theorem, which states that the sum of a large number of independent random events will approximate a normal distribution. Even though the path of any single ball is unpredictable, the collective distribution of many balls is highly predictable.

## Analogy for Heredity
Galton used the board as an analogy for the inheritance of human traits like height. He imagined a two-stage process to model two generations, noting that the distribution would get wider. To counteract this, he added hypothetical "chutes" to move the second generation of balls back toward the center, which represented his mistaken causal explanation for regression to the mean.
