# Peter Bickel

## Overview
Peter Bickel is a statistician who was asked in 1973 to analyze graduate admissions data from the University of California, Berkeley, which appeared to show bias against female applicants.

## Role in the Berkeley Admissions Case
Bickel immediately recognized the data as a form of Simpson's paradox. While the overall data showed a higher admission rate for men, a department-by-department analysis consistently showed admission rates were more favorable to women. Bickel and his colleague Eugene Hammel argued that partitioning the data by department was the correct way to analyze the question of discrimination, as it aimed to estimate the direct effect of gender on admission outcome, separate from the indirect effect of gender on department choice.

## The Debate with William Kruskal
Following the publication of their paper, Bickel's analysis was challenged in a letter from William Kruskal of the University of Chicago. Kruskal questioned whether the observational study could truly exonerate Berkeley, raising the possibility of confounding between department choice and admission outcome. This exchange between the two statisticians is highlighted as a fascinating example of great minds struggling with causal concepts for which they lacked a formal vocabulary.
