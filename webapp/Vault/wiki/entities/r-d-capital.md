# R&D Capital

## Overview
R&D capital represents the stock of knowledge derived from past investments in research and development. In this study, it is treated as a specific, measurable variety of capital whose data is taken from Peters and Taylor (2017) and extended through 2017. It is a key input for analyzing the link between intangible investment and productivity.

## Role in Productivity Analysis
R&D capital serves as a primary example for understanding Productivity J-curve dynamics. Because corporate R&D leads to new technologies that diffuse over time and has seen steady investment for decades, it provides a useful context for analysis. The study notes that the link between R&D investment and market value is well-established, and because investment has persisted over the long term, the associated intangible-related challenges for productivity estimation are likely to be minimal at present.

## Market Valuation
The paper uses market value regressions to estimate the "shadow value" of R&D-related intangible assets. By running year-by-year regressions of market value on R&D capital, the study finds substantial variation in the R&D coefficient over time, with notable peaks around 1962 and 2000. This time-varying coefficient is then used to compute adjustments to Total Factor Productivity (TFP) growth.
