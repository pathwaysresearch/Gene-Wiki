# Asimov's Laws of Robotics

## Overview
Proposed by Isaac Asimov, the Laws of Robotics are a set of prescriptive rules intended to govern the behavior of intelligent machines. The first law, for example, is "A robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm."

## Critique and Failure
The text argues that this rule-based approach to robot morality is fundamentally flawed. It is noted that science fiction has repeatedly shown how these absolute laws always lead to contradictions and unintended negative consequences.

## Rejection by AI Scientists
From the perspective of AI science, the failure of Asimov's laws is unsurprising, as rule-based systems are generally considered ineffective for handling the complexity of real-world situations. The text uses this failure to argue against prescriptive ethics for AI and in favor of a different approach based on equipping machines with cognitive abilities like empathy and causal prediction.
