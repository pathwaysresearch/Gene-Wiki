# Moneyball

## Overview
"Moneyball" refers to the story of the Oakland Athletics (A's) baseball team and its general manager, Billy Beane, as documented by Michael Lewis. Faced with a modest budget, the team used a statistical system to predict player performance and build a highly competitive team that outperformed rivals.

## Challenging Expert Prediction
The case is presented as a centerpiece example of poor expert prediction. Traditional baseball scouts relied on heuristics, some of which were bizarre and unreliable, such as judging a player's confidence by his girlfriend. The Moneyball approach replaced this flawed human judgment with data-driven predictions.

## The Sabermetrics System
The A's used a system called "sabermetrics," developed by Bill James. This new prediction machine shifted focus from traditional indicators like stolen bases and batting average to more impactful metrics like on-base performance and slugging percentage. This allowed the team to identify players who were undervalued by traditional evaluation methods.
