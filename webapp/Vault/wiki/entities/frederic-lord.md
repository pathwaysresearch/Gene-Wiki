# Frederic Lord

## Overview
Frederic Lord was a statistician who, in 1967, stated a paradox that became a classic example in the statistical literature. The text presents it as a cousin of Simpson's paradox, useful for probing the limits of understanding in causal analysis.

## Lord's Paradox
Lord's contribution was a fictitious but powerful thought experiment about a school studying the effects of its diet on student weight gain. The paradox emerges from the conflicting results obtained by two different, and seemingly reasonable, statistical approaches to analyzing the same data to determine if the diet had a different effect on boys and girls. This highlights the ambiguity that can arise when causal questions are addressed with purely statistical methods.

## Significance
The text uses Lord's paradox to demonstrate the necessity of a causal framework. While Lord himself may have been pessimistic about a resolution, the author shows that the paradox can be answered in a rigorous way by first constructing a causal diagram. The diagram makes the underlying assumptions clear and dictates the correct method of analysis, thereby resolving the ambiguity that Lord originally posed.
