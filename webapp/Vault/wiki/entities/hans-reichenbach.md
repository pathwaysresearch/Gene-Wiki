# Hans Reichenbach

## Overview
Hans Reichenbach was a prominent philosopher whose work addressed fundamental questions of causality, time, and science. His book *The Direction of Time*, published posthumously in 1956, is cited as the source of one of his key ideas.

## The Common Cause Principle
Reichenbach is credited with making the daring conjecture called the "common cause principle." He posited that "No correlation without causation," arguing that any correlation between two variables must be explained either by a direct causal link between them or by a third variable that is a common cause of both. This was a significant strengthening of the more common statistical warning that correlation does not imply causation.

## Legacy and Limitations
While highly influential, the text demonstrates that Reichenbach's principle was ultimately too strong. His failure to account for collider structures and the process of data selection meant his principle could not explain all observed correlations. This error is presented as being particularly illuminating because it pinpoints a common flaw in human intuition regarding cause and effect.
