# Dennis Lindley

## Overview
Dennis Lindley was a leading statistician whose views are presented as representative of the statistical community's historical struggle with the concept of causation. His 1981 work with Melvin Novick is highlighted as a key example of this perspective.

## Views on Causation
In a 1981 paper, Lindley and Novick considered using causal language to resolve issues like Simpson's paradox but ultimately decided against it. They confessed, "We have not chosen to do this; nor to discuss causation, because the concept, although widely used, does not seem to be well-defined." This statement is said to summarize the frustration of five generations of statisticians who needed causal information but lacked a formal language to express it.

## Later Reflections
The text recounts a personal anecdote from 2009, where Lindley, four years before his death, confided in the author. He stated that he would not have written those words in 1981 if the author's book on causal inference had been available at the time. This reflects a significant evolution in his thinking and underscores the impact of the new science of causality on the field of statistics.
