# William Kruskal

## Overview
William Kruskal was a statistician at the University of Chicago who engaged in a critical discussion with Peter Bickel following the publication of the 1973 Berkeley admissions study.

## Critique of the Berkeley Analysis
Kruskal wrote a letter to Bickel arguing that the analysis, which partitioned data by department, did not necessarily exonerate Berkeley from the charge of discrimination. He astutely questioned whether any purely observational study could do so. Kruskal's key insight was to propose the existence of a potential confounder, such as "State of Residence," that could influence both the choice of department (the mediator) and the admission outcome, thereby invalidating the conclusion drawn from simply conditioning on the department.

## Contribution to Causal Inference
Kruskal's critique identified what the author terms the "Mediation Fallacy"—the error of conditioning on a mediator instead of holding it constant. By recognizing that confounding between the mediator and the outcome could completely reverse an analysis, he anticipated a central problem in modern causal mediation analysis. His exchange with Bickel illustrates the struggle to articulate complex causal ideas before the development of a formal language for causation.
