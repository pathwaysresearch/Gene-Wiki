# Warren Weaver

## Role in Machine Translation
Warren Weaver was a pivotal figure in the early history of machine translation. In March 1947, while at the Rockefeller Foundation, he corresponded with Norbert Wiener, suggesting that automated translation might be a feasible endeavor for the new computing machines.

## The "Decoding" Analogy
Weaver's most significant contribution was a conceptual framework that shaped the field for a decade. Drawing on his background in cryptography and information theory, he proposed viewing translation as a decoding problem. He famously articulated this idea: "When I look at an article in Russian, I say: 'This is really written in English, but it has been coded in strange symbols. I will now proceed to decode.'"

## Influence and Legacy
This powerful analogy framed the challenge of machine translation in terms that were familiar to information theorists and cryptographers of the era. It spurred a decade of enthusiasm and research, leading to early demonstrations such as a rudimentary system exhibited by IBM in 1954. His perspective dominated the initial approach to the problem.
