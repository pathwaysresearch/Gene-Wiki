# Sewall Wright

## Overview and Research
Sewall Wright was a geneticist whose early work at the USDA on the inheritance of coat color in guinea pigs became the springboard for his entire theory of evolution and causal analysis. The complex variation in pigmentation, which refused to follow simple Mendelian rules even in highly inbred families, presented a puzzle that led to his most significant breakthroughs. He later took a faculty position at the University of Chicago, though he remained devoted to his guinea pigs.

## Invention of Path Analysis
To disentangle the complex web of influences on guinea pig traits, Wright invented path analysis. He created path diagrams to visually map every conceivable causal factor, including hereditary (H), environmental (E), and developmental (D) influences for both parents and offspring. He then developed a mathematical method to calculate "path coefficients" representing the strength of these causal links from the observed correlations in the data. This was the first time mathematics was used to formally connect correlation and causation, allowing him to compute biologically meaningful quantities like the direct effect of gestation time on birth weight.

## Defending Causation in Statistics
Wright's work was a direct challenge to the anti-causal statistical establishment dominated by Karl Pearson's philosophy. He faced criticism from Pearson's followers, like Niles, for assuming causal relationships. Wright's clear counterargument was that one cannot draw any causal conclusions without making some causal hypotheses to begin with. The text emphasizes his intellectual courage, noting that he defended his methods based on an internal conviction of their correctness, without the support of established theorems or scientific authorities, who had abandoned causation decades earlier.
