# Automated Mathematician (AM)

## Overview
The Automated Mathematician, or AM, was a discovery system created by Doug Lenat. It was designed to model the process of scientific discovery by performing a direct search in the space of concept definitions. Its specific domain of application was elementary number theory.

## Method of Operation
AM operated by using a set of discovery heuristics that were expressed as expert system rules. These rules guided its search for new mathematical concepts and potential relationships between them. A defining feature of AM's architecture was its lack of a formal concept of proof; it was designed to generate interesting conjectures, not to prove their validity.

## Key Discoveries and Legacy
Despite its inability to produce proofs, AM was successful in its exploratory mission. It independently rediscovered several well-known and important results from number theory, including Goldbach's conjecture and the Unique Prime Factorization theorem. The architecture of AM was later generalized in the EURISKO system, also developed by Lenat.
