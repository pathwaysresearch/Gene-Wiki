# John Snow

## Overview
Dr. John Snow was a physician practicing in London during the severe cholera epidemics of the 1850s. At the time, the prevailing wisdom was the "miasma" theory, which held that the disease was caused by unhealthy air. Snow was a skeptic of this theory, arguing instead that the symptoms pointed to a different mode of transmission.

## Investigation of the Cholera Epidemic
Through painstaking detective work, Snow gathered evidence suggesting that cholera was a waterborne disease. His investigation during the 1853-1854 epidemic is a landmark in the history of public health and epidemiology, as he traced the outbreak to a contaminated water supply.

## Contribution to Causal Inference
Snow's work is presented as a classic, intuitive application of the instrumental variable method. He used the 'Water Company' supplying a household as an instrument to study the effect of 'Water Purity' on 'Cholera'. He implicitly assumed that the choice of water company was independent of confounding factors like poverty (miasma), that it affected cholera only through the purity of the water it supplied, and that the two were strongly linked. This allowed him to demonstrate a causal link between contaminated water and cholera.
