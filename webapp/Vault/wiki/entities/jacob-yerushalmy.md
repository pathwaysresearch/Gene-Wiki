# Jacob Yerushalmy

## Overview
Jacob Yerushalmy was a biostatistician who, like R. A. Fisher, was a key skeptic in the debate over whether smoking causes lung cancer. He is identified in the text as "Yak" in a cartoon depicting two figures with opposing views on the matter.

## Role in the Smoking-Cancer Debate
Yerushalmy was committed to the idea that the statistical association between smoking and lung cancer was spurious and did not prove causation. He and Fisher argued that a hidden confounder could be responsible for the observed correlation, and that without a randomized controlled trial, the skeptics could not be convinced.

## Stance on Evidence
The skepticism of Yerushalmy and Fisher highlighted the limitations of observational data in establishing causality. They contended that even prospective studies failed to compare smokers to otherwise identical nonsmokers, as smokers are a self-selecting group who may be genetically or "constitutionally" different. Their persistent skepticism forced the public health community to amass an overwhelming amount of consistent evidence before a causal conclusion was widely accepted.
