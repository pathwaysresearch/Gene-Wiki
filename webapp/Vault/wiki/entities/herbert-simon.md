# Herbert Simon

## Overview
Herbert Simon is presented in the text as a pivotal figure who challenged classical models of perfect rationality. He was awarded the Nobel Prize in economics for this work, which has had a profound impact on economics, cognitive science, and artificial intelligence.

## Core Contributions
Simon's primary contribution was the theory of bounded rationality. He argued that the "capacity of the human mind for formulating and solving complex problems is very small compared with the size of the problems whose solution is required for objectively rational behavior in the real world." This insight recognized that real-world decision-making is constrained by limited time, information, and computational ability.

## The Concept of Satisficing
To explain how decisions are made under these constraints, Simon introduced the concept of "satisficing." He proposed that agents do not search for the optimal solution but rather deliberate only long enough to find an answer that is "good enough." This provides a more psychologically realistic model of human behavior.
