# Luddite Movement

## Overview
The Luddite movement is described as an early response to the technological changes of the Industrial Revolution. It emerged near Nottingham, England, in 1811 and spread rapidly, composed of workers who railed against new automated technologies that were disrupting their traditional way of life and work.

## Motivations and Grievances
The movement's participants were primarily traditional textile workers, including weavers, croppers, and cotton spinners. These artisans had traditionally worked from home, enjoying good pay and leisure. They protested the introduction of coal-powered automated handlooms and high-volume factories, which replaced their skilled craft with specialized equipment operated by a smaller number of less-skilled workers in often squalid factory conditions.

## Historical Context
The Luddites' protest was a reaction to the shift from handcrafted artisan production to specialized, mass production methods. This transformation, a hallmark of the Industrial Revolution, redefined required skills, industry boundaries, and competitive dynamics, leading to significant economic, social, and political change.
