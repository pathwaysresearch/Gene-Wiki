# Barbara Burks

## Overview

Barbara Burks was a Stanford graduate student in 1926 and is described as a little-known pioneer in women's science and a true hero of causal inference. Her work on mediation and causal diagrams was decades ahead of its time.

## Pioneering Work on Mediation

To the best of the author's knowledge, Burks was the first person to explicitly represent a mediator with a diagram in 1926. There is reason to believe she invented path diagrams independently of the famous geneticist Sewall Wright. In the specific area of mediation, her understanding and formalization were more advanced than Wright's.

## Discovery of Collider Bias

Burks's most significant contribution was her early characterization of collider bias. In her work on the nature-nurture debate, she formulated criteria for avoiding bias when estimating direct and indirect effects. Her criterion (b) is one of the earliest known general descriptions of collider bias, warning against conditioning on a variable that is a common effect of the cause and another unmeasured factor. This insight ran counter to the prevailing statistical wisdom of her era, which held that controlling for variables was generally a safe way to move toward causal explanation.
