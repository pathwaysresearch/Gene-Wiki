# Casimir Funk

## Overview

Casimir Funk was a Polish biochemist who, in 1912, made a foundational contribution to the field of nutrition and biochemistry. His work provided the theoretical framework that led to the discovery of vitamins.

## The "Vitamines" Theory

In 1912, Funk proposed the existence of micronutrients that are essential for health, which he named "vitamines." This concept was a major breakthrough, suggesting that certain diseases were caused not by pathogens or toxins, but by the absence of these vital substances.

## Role in the Discovery of Vitamin C

Funk's theory of "vitamines" was the direct precursor to the eventual discovery and isolation of Vitamin C by Albert Szent-Gyorgyi. By proposing the existence of such compounds, Funk's work initiated the scientific search for the specific substances that could prevent deficiency diseases like scurvy, ultimately leading to Szent-Gyorgyi's Nobel Prize-winning discovery.
