# Bloom's Two Sigma Problem

## Overview
Bloom's Two Sigma Problem, also referred to as Bloom's two sigma study, is an educational finding originating from research conducted at MIT in the 1980s. The "problem" refers to the challenge of making the highly effective methods of one-on-one tutoring scalable and affordable for mass education.

## Core Finding
The central conclusion of the study is that the average student tutored one-on-one achieves learning outcomes that are two standard deviations (or two sigma) higher than the average student taught in a conventional classroom environment. This demonstrates the profound impact of personalized coaching and mentorship on learning effectiveness.

## Relevance to AI and Modern Education
The text connects this historical finding to the present by highlighting AI's potential to solve the scalability problem. It posits that AI can dramatically lower the cost of delivering personalized, one-on-one coaching. This would make it possible to create scalable systems that provide the kind of individualized guidance needed to achieve the two-sigma performance gain for a much larger population.
