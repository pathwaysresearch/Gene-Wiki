# FF Planning System

## Overview
The FF (Fast-Forward) planning system is a system for automated plan generation, developed primarily by Jörg Hoffmann. It gained prominence for its speed and effectiveness, as described in a 2001 article in AI Magazine titled "FF: The fast-forward planning system."

## Core Approach
The central technique of the FF system is heuristic search, which guides the search for a valid plan in a forward direction from the initial state. A 2001 JAIR paper by Hoffmann and Nebel details this approach, explaining how it enables "fast plan generation through heuristic search." The system's performance has also been analyzed in the context of planning benchmarks, for example by studying the effects of "ignoring delete lists" on the local search topology.

## Extensions and Applications
The core ideas of heuristic forward search pioneered in FF have been extended to more complex planning problems. Research by Hoffmann and Brafman applied the approach to conformant planning (planning with incomplete information) and contingent planning (planning with sensing), demonstrating the versatility of the underlying search method.
