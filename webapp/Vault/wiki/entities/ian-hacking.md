# Ian Hacking

## Overview
Ian Hacking is a philosopher whose work is referenced in the text to provide historical context for the rise of probabilistic methods in computer science. He is the author of the book *The Taming of Chance*.

## The Taming of Chance
According to the text's summary of Hacking's work, the concept of probability underwent a significant evolution. Before the nineteenth century, it was primarily the domain of gamblers. The nineteenth century saw the application of the new mathematics of probability to the social sciences, spurred by the rise of government census data. This was followed in the twentieth century by a fundamental reordering of the physical sciences, moving from a deterministic Newtonian perspective to the uncertainties of quantum mechanics.

## Significance to AI
Hacking's historical analysis is used to frame the current shift in computer science as a similarly profound paradigm change. The text suggests that the recognition that algorithms work best when structured probabilistically and based on data is the most important advance in twenty-first-century computer science, matching the earlier probabilistic revolutions in the social and physical sciences.
