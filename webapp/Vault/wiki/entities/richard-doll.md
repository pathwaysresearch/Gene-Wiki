# Richard Doll

## Overview
Richard Doll was a British epidemiologist who played a critical role in establishing the causal relationship between smoking and lung cancer. Initially, he suspected that motor car exhausts or the tarring of roads were more likely culprits for the rise in lung cancer than smoking.

## Key Research
Realizing that simple replication of case-control studies would not overcome hidden biases, Doll and his colleague A. B. Hill initiated a large-scale prospective study in 1951. They surveyed 60,000 British physicians about their smoking habits and tracked their health outcomes over time. This study design was intended to provide more robust evidence than previous retrospective studies.

## Major Findings and Impact
The results of the British doctors' study were dramatic. Within five years, it showed that heavy smokers had a lung cancer death rate twenty-four times that of nonsmokers. The study also demonstrated a dose-response effect, where risk increased with the amount smoked and decreased upon quitting. This consistent and powerful evidence was instrumental in building the case for causality and countering the arguments of skeptics.
