# Albert Szent-Gyorgyi

## Overview

Albert Szent-Gyorgyi was a biochemist who received the Nobel Prize in 1937 for his work on the biological processes related to scurvy. His discovery was a landmark in nutritional science and a clear demonstration of a causal mechanism.

## Discovery of Vitamin C

By 1930, Szent-Gyorgyi had successfully isolated the specific nutrient responsible for preventing scurvy. He identified it as a particular acid, which is now known as vitamin C or ascorbic acid. The name "ascorbic" is a direct reference to its "antiscorbutic" (anti-scurvy) properties.

## Significance for Mediation Analysis

Szent-Gyorgyi's work provides a classic example of the importance of identifying the correct mediator in a causal chain. He definitively established the actual causal path: Citrus Fruits → Vitamin C → Scurvy. This discovery corrected a major scientific error where a false mediator (ptomaine poisoning) had been blamed, and it solidified the understanding of the mechanism by which citrus fruits prevent the disease.
