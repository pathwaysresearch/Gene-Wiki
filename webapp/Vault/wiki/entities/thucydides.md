# Thucydides

## Overview
Thucydides was an ancient Greek historian, often described as a pioneer of a “scientific” approach to history due to his meticulous observations and causal analysis in his work, 'History of the Peloponnesian War'.

## Analysis of the 426 BC Tsunami
The text highlights a specific passage where Thucydides describes a tsunami that struck Orobiae. He provides a precise observation of the sea first receding and then returning as a huge wave. He then seeks a causal explanation for the phenomenon.

## Early Use of Counterfactual Reasoning
Thucydides's analysis is presented as a remarkable early example of counterfactual thinking. He concludes his observation by stating, "The cause, in my opinion, of this phenomenon must be sought in the earthquake... Without an earthquake I do not see how such an accident could happen." This final sentence is a clear counterfactual claim, imagining an alternative world without the earthquake to establish its necessity as a cause, thereby demonstrating a sophisticated form of causal reasoning far ahead of its time.
