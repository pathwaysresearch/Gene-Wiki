# Greenland and Robins

## Overview
Sander Greenland and James Robins are epidemiologists who made a foundational contribution to the understanding of confounding. Their work challenged the prevailing statistical definitions and introduced a new approach rooted in counterfactual reasoning.

## Key Contribution: Exchangeability
In their landmark 1986 paper, Greenland and Robins proposed the concept of "exchangeability" as a formal definition for the absence of confounding. This approach required researchers to consider whether the treatment and control groups were comparable by asking a counterfactual question: would the treatment group have had the same outcome as the control group if they had not received the treatment?

## Significance
Their work represented a "completely new approach" to confounding that was powerful enough to detect it correctly. Introducing counterfactuals to an audience of epidemiologists still heavily influenced by classical statistics was a courageous step that helped advance the field of causal inference and provided a more rigorous foundation for understanding bias in observational studies.
