# Chess

## Role in AI Research
The text highlights chess's historical importance in AI, quoting Alexander Kronrod's 1965 statement that it is the "*Drosophila* of artificial intelligence." While John McCarthy disagreed with the analogy's implication for broader discoveries, the text compares state-of-the-art chess programs to Grand Prix racing cars: highly optimized machines that incorporate the latest engineering advances and drive innovation within the wider community.

## Key Programs and Milestones
The text chronicles several key chess programs. Early examples include Herbert Simon and Allen Newell's NSS program (1958) and the Kotok-McCarthy program (1967). The most famous milestone is IBM's DEEP BLUE defeating world champion Garry Kasparov. Later programs like HYDRA, DEEP FRITZ, and RYBKA continued to advance, defeating top grand masters and achieving extremely high Elo ratings by 2009.

## Technical Approach
The dominant technical approach described for chess programs is alpha-beta search. DEEP BLUE, for example, used a massively parallel implementation of alpha-beta search, augmented by custom VLSI chess processors for move generation and leaf node evaluation. This approach contrasts with the methods used for the game of Go.
