# Computer Hardware Capital

## Overview
Computer hardware capital is a variety of measured capital stock that includes physical assets like computers and related equipment. The data for this capital stock and its associated investments are sourced from Fernald (2014) and extended through 2017 for the study's analysis.

## Associated Intangibles and J-Curve
The paper analyzes computer hardware as a source of a significant Productivity J-curve. Investment in hardware is assumed to be correlated with large, unmeasured intangible investments, such as process redesign and employee training. The analysis concludes that the Productivity J-curve for hardware-related intangibles has yet to reach positive territory in terms of its effect on Total Factor Productivity (TFP) levels.

## Impact on Productivity Measurement
The divergence between measured TFP and TFP corrected for hardware-related intangibles became most noticeable in the 1990s. The study models this effect by assuming a ratio of intangible value for each dollar of hardware investment (e.g., a value of $10, $5, or $3). The analysis shows that accounting for both the "missing intangible inputs" and "missing intangible outputs" effects significantly alters the perceived path of productivity growth.
