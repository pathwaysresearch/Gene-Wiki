# US Surgeon General's Report on Smoking and Health (1964)

## Overview
The 1964 report by the US Surgeon General was a pivotal moment in the history of public health and the culmination of over a decade of research into the health effects of smoking. It represented the final breach in the stalemate between the public health community and skeptics like R. A. Fisher, who argued the evidence was inconclusive.

## Key Declaration
The report's most significant contribution was its blunt and unambiguous statement: “Cigarette smoking is causally related to lung cancer in men.” This declaration moved beyond mere association and made a definitive causal claim, which forever shut down the argument that the link was "not proven."

## Impact and Legacy
The report had a profound and immediate impact on public behavior and policy. The rate of smoking among men in the United States began to decline the following year and is now less than half what it was in 1964. The report is credited with saving millions of lives and is considered a great triumph for public health and epidemiology.
