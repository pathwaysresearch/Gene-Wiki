# Alan Turing

## Overview

Alan Turing is identified in the text as the figure who became famous, or 'more widely known,' for proposing the 'imitation game,' which is more commonly referred to as the Turing Test. His work provides a foundational concept for evaluating artificial intelligence.

## Contribution: The Imitation Game

Turing's key contribution discussed in the text is the test he proposed for machine intelligence. The test is based on behavior and performance, operating on the principle that if a machine can interact in a way that is indistinguishable from a human, it should be considered intelligent. The text frames this as a pragmatic approach focused on a machine's ability to 'do' things.

## Significance and Critique

The text positions Turing's test as a 'more defensible' standard for intelligence but a 'more dangerous' one for consciousness. While his behavioral approach is suitable for assessing functional capabilities, the text argues it is a flawed metric for inferring internal states like subjective experience. This highlights the ongoing debate about the limits of Turing's framework in the context of modern AI that can convincingly mimic human interaction.
