# PENGI

## Overview
PENGI was a system developed by Philip Agre and David Chapman in 1987 as an example of a reactive planning system. It was designed to operate in a dynamic environment, specifically a fully observable video game.

## Architecture
As a reactive agent, PENGI did not engage in slow, deliberative planning. Instead, it used Boolean circuits combined with a "visual" representation of its current goals and internal state to make rapid decisions and take action.

## Significance
PENGI was a key example in the mid-1980s movement that proposed reactive planning as an alternative to traditional planning systems, which were often criticized for their slow run times. It demonstrated the feasibility of building agents that could act effectively in real-time without complex lookahead search.
