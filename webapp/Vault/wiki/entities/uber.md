# Uber

## Business Model Characteristics
Uber's platform exhibits indirect network effects, where riders value a large number of drivers and vice versa. However, its network is highly clustered around individual urban locations, meaning its global scale does not create a strong, unified network effect. The company also benefits from learning effects, using its extensive data to optimize pricing due to traffic conditions, predict supply and demand, and improve service quality.

## Strategic Challenges
A primary challenge for Uber is extensive multihoming on both the rider and driver sides of its network. A large proportion of users have multiple ride-hailing apps and switch between them for the most economical service, which limits profitability. The clustered nature of its network also fosters intense local competition, as challengers can achieve critical mass in a single city without needing global scale. Disintermediation, however, is not described as a common problem.

## Strategic Responses and Opportunities
To combat multihoming, Uber has introduced usage-based rewards programs for riders and features that reduce driver idle time, such as providing the next ride request during an in-progress trip. Despite a challenged core business, the text suggests that Uber's intrinsic value lies in potential bridging opportunities, such as connecting its rider network with retail through services like Cargo.
