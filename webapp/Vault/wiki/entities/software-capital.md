# Software Capital

## Overview
Software capital is a variety of measured capital that has experienced faster growth than overall capital investment. The data for software capital stock and investment is sourced from Fernald (2014). Due to its rapid accumulation and large investment level, it is hypothesized to have a substantial effect on productivity mismeasurement.

## The "Least Mature" J-Curve
The paper characterizes the Productivity J-curve associated with software capital as the "least mature" of the three varieties investigated (along with R&D and hardware). This implies that the initial negative impact on measured productivity from unmeasured intangible investment is still dominant, and the subsequent positive productivity effects have not yet been fully realized.

## Magnitude of Mismeasurement
The analysis reveals that the differences between measured Total Factor Productivity (TFP) and TFP adjusted for software-related intangibles are "starkly larger" than those arising from R&D. This suggests that the scale of unmeasured investment and organizational change correlated with software adoption is particularly large, making it a key factor in explaining recent productivity puzzles.
