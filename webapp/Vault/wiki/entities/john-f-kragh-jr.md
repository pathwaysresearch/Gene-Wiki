# John F. Kragh Jr.

## Overview
John F. Kragh Jr. is an army surgeon whose work during the Iraq and Afghanistan wars is praised as a "shining light" for practitioners of evidence-based medicine. He is recognized for his presence of mind to collect data and scientifically test a standard medical treatment under the most difficult battlefield conditions.

## Research on Tourniquets
Kragh's research focused on the use of tourniquets, a practice that became widespread in the military after 2005, reversing a long-standing policy that discouraged their use. He observed the sea change in trauma care and sought to evaluate its effectiveness. Based on his data, Kragh estimated that tourniquet use saved approximately 2,000 military lives between 2002 and 2012.

## Methodological Challenges
Despite his strong belief in their effectiveness, the text highlights the "bitter irony" that Kragh's study ultimately had to report a "null result." This was because his study could not succeed in definitively proving that tourniquets save lives due to a critical data limitation: he had no way to collect data on soldiers who did not survive to the hospital. This missing data represents a form of selection bias that prevented a complete causal conclusion, even though his scientific instincts and data collection efforts were sound.
