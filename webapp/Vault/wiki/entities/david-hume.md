# David Hume

## Overview
David Hume was a Scottish philosopher whose 1748 work on causality is cited as a foundational moment in understanding the concept. His ideas are particularly important for establishing the role of counterfactuals in causal reasoning. The philosopher David Lewis later analyzed Hume's work, clarifying its dual nature.

## Definition of Causation by Regularity
Hume's first definition of causation is based on regularity. It states, “We may define a cause to be an object followed by another, and where all the objects, similar to the first, are followed by objects similar to the second.” This definition focuses on the consistent, observable pattern of one event following another.

## The Counterfactual Definition
Hume's second definition, which he presents as an alternative phrasing, is fundamentally counterfactual: “Or, in other words, where, if the first object had not been, the second never had existed.” This formulation shifts the focus from mere observation of regularity to a hypothetical consideration of what would have happened in the absence of the cause. This idea is central to the highest level of the Ladder of Causation.
