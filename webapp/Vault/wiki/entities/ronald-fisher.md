# Ronald Fisher

## Overview
Ronald Fisher was a statistician whose ideas revolutionized the design of scientific experiments. He is presented as a key figure who understood the critical importance of asking the right causal questions of nature, even if the answers came with some uncertainty.

## Contributions to Experimental Design
Fisher's primary contribution discussed in the text is his advocacy for randomization. He realized that by randomly assigning treatments, such as different fertilizers to subplots of a field, he could eliminate the bias from confounding variables like soil quality. His colleagues were initially horrified by the idea of using chance, like drawing from a deck of cards, to make scientific decisions.

## Rationale and Legacy
Fisher's insight was that randomization achieves two goals: it eliminates confounder bias and allows for the mathematical quantification of uncertainty. While the text suggests his primary motivation was the latter, his intuitive understanding of deconfounding was profound. He argued that it is better to get an uncertain answer to the correct causal question than a highly certain answer to the wrong one, a principle that has since made randomized trials the gold standard in many fields.
