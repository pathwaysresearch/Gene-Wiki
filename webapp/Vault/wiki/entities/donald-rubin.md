# Donald Rubin

## Overview
Donald Rubin is a Harvard statistician whose work was instrumental in preparing the statistical community for the Causal Revolution. His research introduced formal methods for dealing with counterfactuals, which had previously been considered outside the scope of traditional statistics that holds all answers are in the data.

## Key Contribution: Potential Outcomes Framework
In 1974, Rubin proposed the "potential outcomes" framework. This framework treats counterfactuals—such as the outcome a person would have experienced under a different treatment—as legitimate variables for statistical analysis. It provides a formal language to define and estimate causal effects by comparing these potential outcomes.

## Legacy and Significance
Rubin's work is described as "pioneering" and was crucial for overcoming the resistance of classical statisticians to counterfactual reasoning. By legitimizing the study of "what might have been," his framework laid the essential groundwork for later developments in causal inference, including the concept of exchangeability used in epidemiology.
