# General Problem Solver

## Overview
The General Problem Solver (GPS) was a highly influential AI program developed in the late 1950s. The index references it on pages 3, 7, and 18, situating it as a foundational project in the early history of AI, and again on page 393, likely in the context of modern planning techniques.

## Problem-Solving Approach
GPS was designed to be a universal problem-solving engine based on the technique of means-ends analysis. It worked by comparing the current state to the goal state to find the most important difference, and then selecting an operator (an action) specifically to reduce that difference. This approach of breaking down a large problem into smaller, more manageable sub-goals was a significant contribution to AI.

## Legacy and Significance
While GPS was limited to well-formalized problems and did not scale to complex real-world tasks, its conceptual framework had a profound impact on the field. It was one of the first programs to separate its problem-solving strategy from its knowledge of specific domains, and the means-ends analysis technique it pioneered became a fundamental concept in AI planning and problem-solving.
