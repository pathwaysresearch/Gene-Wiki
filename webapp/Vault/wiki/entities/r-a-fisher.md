# R. A. Fisher

## Overview
R. A. Fisher was a highly influential statistician who played the role of a prominent skeptic during the debate over the health hazards of smoking. He argued that the observed association between smoking and lung cancer could be spurious and not causal.

## Role in the Smoking-Cancer Debate
Fisher, along with other skeptics like Yerushalmy, was committed to the idea that a lurking third factor could explain the correlation between smoking and lung cancer. He resisted the conclusions drawn from observational studies, including prospective ones, arguing that without a randomized controlled trial, causality could not be proven because smokers are a self-selecting group.

## The "Smoking Gene" Hypothesis
Fisher's primary counter-argument was the possibility of a genetic or "constitutional" confounder. He suggested that a "smoking gene" might exist that both caused people to crave cigarettes and, independently, made them more susceptible to developing lung cancer. This argument placed the burden of proof on the anti-smoking contingent to disprove the existence of any such confounder, which is almost impossible.
