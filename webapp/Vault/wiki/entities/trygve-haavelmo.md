# Trygve Haavelmo

## Overview
Trygve Haavelmo was an economist whose work was crucial for introducing formal causal and probabilistic thinking into economics. The text identifies him as a key figure in the history of causal inference, particularly within econometrics.

## Contributions to Econometrics
Haavelmo's 1943 paper, "The statistical implications of a system of simultaneous equations," was a landmark publication. He argued that economic models should be interpreted as probabilistic and causal systems, not just sets of descriptive equations. This perspective was essential for understanding how to estimate the parameters of structural models from economic data and for analyzing the effects of policy interventions.

## Legacy and Significance
Haavelmo's work helped lay the foundation for modern econometrics and the use of structural equation models in economics. His insistence on a clear distinction between statistical association and causal structure was a critical step in the development of causal inference in the social sciences, influencing later work on causal identification and modeling.
