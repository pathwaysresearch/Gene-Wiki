# Sussman Anomaly

## Overview
The Sussman anomaly is a well-known benchmark problem in the blocks-world domain of AI planning. It involves a specific initial and goal configuration of three blocks (A, B, and C).

## Historical Significance
The problem was deemed an "anomaly" because the noninterleaved planners of the early 1970s were incapable of solving it. These early planners struggled because solving the problem requires temporarily undoing a previously achieved subgoal (e.g., moving a block off another block where it should ultimately reside) to achieve another necessary subgoal. This interaction between subgoals highlighted the limitations of simple, linear planning strategies and motivated the development of more flexible planners.
