# Torsten Wiesel

## Overview
Torsten Wiesel is a neurophysiologist who shared the 1981 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with David H. Hubel for their discoveries regarding information processing in the visual system.

## Collaborative Research
In collaboration with Hubel, Wiesel conducted experiments on the visual systems of cats and monkeys in the late 1950s and early 1960s. Their research revealed that neurons in the visual cortex are organized to detect specific features within small 'local receptive fields.' They showed that some neurons respond only to horizontal lines, while others respond to lines with different orientations.

## Legacy in Artificial Intelligence
The work of Wiesel and Hubel suggested a hierarchical structure for visual processing, where complex patterns are recognized by combining simpler, lower-level patterns. This concept of local feature detection and hierarchical composition became a foundational principle for the architecture of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which are central to modern computer vision.
