# Francis Galton

## Overview
Francis Galton was a scientist who, in the late 19th century, pursued a causal explanation for the laws of heredity. His research into how traits like height are passed from one generation to the next led to several foundational statistical discoveries.

## Contributions to Statistics
In his quest to understand heredity, Galton developed the concepts of regression to the mean and correlation. He used the Galton board as a physical analogy to demonstrate how random processes result in a normal distribution, which he observed in data on human heights. His work provided, for the first time, an objective measure (correlation) of how two variables are related.

## From Causation to Correlation
The text portrays Galton's intellectual journey as an "irony of history." He began by searching for causation but ended up discovering correlation, a concept that is indifferent to cause and effect. While his disciple Karl Pearson would champion correlation as a replacement for causation, Galton himself seemed to retain a causal intuition, suggesting that correlations arise from common causes. He also initially misinterpreted regression to the mean as a direct causal force.
