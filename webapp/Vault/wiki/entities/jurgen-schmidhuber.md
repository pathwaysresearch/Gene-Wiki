# Jürgen Schmidhuber

## Overview
Jürgen Schmidhuber is a key researcher in the field of neural networks, with foundational contributions particularly in the area of recurrent neural networks for sequence learning.

## Development of LSTM
Schmidhuber's most cited contribution in these excerpts is the co-invention of the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network with Sepp Hochreiter in 1997. This architecture was a breakthrough for learning long-term dependencies in sequential data. Further work with Gers and Cummins in 2000 explored the "learning to forget" mechanism within LSTMs.

## Applications in Sequence Tasks
His research has consistently focused on applying RNNs to challenging sequence problems. This includes framewise phoneme classification with bidirectional LSTMs (Graves & Schmidhuber, 2005), offline handwriting recognition with multidimensional RNNs (Graves & Schmidhuber, 2009), and co-developing Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) for labeling unsegmented sequences (Graves et al., 2006).
