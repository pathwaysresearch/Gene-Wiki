# Kenneth Craik

## Overview
Kenneth Craik was a student and successor to Frederic Bartlett at Cambridge's Applied Psychology Unit. His work was instrumental in the development of cognitive psychology as a field that views the brain as an information-processing device.

## Major Contributions
In his 1943 book, *The Nature of Explanation*, Craik forcefully reestablished the scientific legitimacy of using "mental" terms such as beliefs and goals. He argued that these terms are just as scientific as concepts like pressure and temperature in physics, despite being about unobservable internal states.

## The Knowledge-Based Agent
Craik specified the three essential steps of what would now be called a knowledge-based agent. He stated that: (1) a stimulus must be translated into an internal representation, (2) this representation is manipulated by cognitive processes to derive new internal representations, and (3) these new representations are retranslated back into action.
