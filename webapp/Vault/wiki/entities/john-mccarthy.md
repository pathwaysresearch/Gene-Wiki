# John McCarthy

## Overview
The provided bibliography cites John McCarthy as a prolific and foundational author in artificial intelligence, with publications spanning several decades from 1958 to 2007. His work appears in seminal collections and top journals, indicating a long and impactful career shaping the core ideas of the field.

## Contributions to Logic and Reasoning
McCarthy's work heavily influenced AI's approach to logic and reasoning. His 1980 paper, "Circumscription: A form of non-monotonic reasoning," introduced a key formalism for dealing with exceptions in commonsense knowledge. Earlier, his 1963 memo on "Situations, actions, and causal laws" laid groundwork for representing and reasoning about dynamic environments, a precursor to modern planning and robotics.

## Vision for AI
Beyond specific techniques, McCarthy's work addressed the long-term vision of the field. His 1958 paper "Programs with common sense" articulated a central goal for AI that remains relevant today. This forward-looking perspective is also evident in his 2007 paper, "From here to human-level AI," reflecting his continued engagement with the grand challenges of artificial intelligence. In collaboration with P. J. Hayes in 1969, he also explored the "philosophical problems from the standpoint of artificial intelligence," grounding the engineering discipline in deeper conceptual questions.
