# Nokia

## Overview
Nokia was a leading mobile phone manufacturer that operated as a great product company, optimized to produce laser-focused, differentiated products. This approach ultimately led to its decline with the rise of smartphone platforms like Apple's iOS and Google's Android, which were built on a different operating model.

## Strategic Failures
Nokia's primary failure was its lack of a single, consistent digital foundation. Despite investing in the Symbian operating system, the company used multiple OS versions, and even within Symbian products, each phone's software was fine-tuned to a different design. This fragmentation created an unstable and user-unfriendly developer interface, meaning any app had to be redesigned for virtually every product. As a result, its Ovi app store never attracted a critical mass of developers or apps.

## Consequences of a Siloed Model
By operating in product-based silos, Nokia failed to gather scale benefits from a standard digital foundation, scope benefits from a platform ecosystem, and learning benefits from a consistent data architecture. When faced with the threat from iOS and Android, it failed to execute a transition to a software-optimized operating architecture or to successfully pivot to being a hardware complement like Samsung.
