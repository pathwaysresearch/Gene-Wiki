# Thomas Verma

## Overview
Thomas Verma was a highly talented student who began working with Judea Pearl at the age of sixteen. His father brought him to Pearl's office seeking a challenge for him beyond what his high school math teachers could provide.

## Key Contribution
Verma's most significant contribution was proving the d-separation property. This property establishes that the rules of path blocking in a causal diagram can be used to determine all the statistical independencies that should hold in the data generated from that causal model. The text notes the astonishing fact that Verma proved this unsolved conjecture because he thought it was a homework assignment.

## Legacy
Verma's proof of d-separation is a cornerstone of modern causal inference. It provides the formal link between the causal assumptions encoded in a graph (rung three of the Ladder of Causation) and the patterns of association observed in data (rung one). His work is directly incorporated into Rule 1 of the do-calculus, demonstrating its fundamental importance to the field.
