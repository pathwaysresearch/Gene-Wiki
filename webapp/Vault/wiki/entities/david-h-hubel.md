# David H. Hubel

## Overview
David H. Hubel was a neurophysiologist who, along with his colleague Torsten Wiesel, was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1981 for their groundbreaking work on the visual system.

## Key Research and Findings
In a series of experiments on cats and monkeys beginning in 1958, Hubel and Wiesel made crucial discoveries about the structure of the visual cortex. They demonstrated that many neurons in this area have a small 'local receptive field,' meaning they only respond to visual stimuli within a limited region of the visual field. Furthermore, they found that some neurons react specifically to lines of a certain orientation, while others respond to more complex patterns that are combinations of these lower-level features.

## Influence on Machine Learning
The observations made by Hubel and Wiesel led to the idea that the visual cortex processes information in a hierarchical manner, where higher-level neurons build upon the outputs of neighboring lower-level neurons. This powerful architecture, capable of detecting complex patterns in any area of the visual field, became a direct inspiration for the design of modern convolutional neural networks (CNNs).
