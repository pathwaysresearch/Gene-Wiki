# AI Divide

## Overview
The text identifies the potential for an "AI divide" as a major theme, describing it as a visible pattern of disparity between different types of firms and geographies. This divide is characterized by a substantial geographic concentration in the adoption and use of AI, which the authors suggest is in need of careful assessment.

## Geographic Concentration
The study finds that AI use is not evenly distributed across the United States. Instead, it is concentrated in specific hubs, with a higher rate of use in Core-Based Statistical Areas (CBSAs) located in the southern and western parts of the country. This includes well-known technology centers like Silicon Valley and the Research Triangle, but also other large metro areas such as Nashville, San Antonio, Las Vegas, and Tampa. This concentration suggests that new, high-growth startups using AI are often attracted to locations with pre-existing high levels of AI activity.

## Potential Drivers and Implications
The text posits that the forces driving the agglomeration of AI *use* may be distinct from those that promote co-location for AI *invention* or early commercialization, which are often measured by patents and publications. The concentration of AI in already-leading locations raises concerns that this "AI divide" may be reinforced over time, potentially exacerbating regional economic disparities.
