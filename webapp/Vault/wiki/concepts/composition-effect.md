# Composition Effect

## Definition

The composition effect is a component in the decomposition of changes in the aggregate wage bill that captures shifts in labor demand resulting from the reallocation of value added across different sectors of the economy. It isolates the impact of changing industry structure on total labor demand.

## How It Works

This effect is driven by differences in labor intensity across industries. If economic activity shifts from a sector with a high labor share (e.g., services) to one with a low labor share (e.g., capital-intensive manufacturing), the aggregate demand for labor will decrease, even if total economic output remains the same. Conversely, a shift towards more labor-intensive sectors will increase aggregate labor demand.

## Empirical Measurement

In the authors' empirical analysis of the US economy, the composition effect is measured by summing the change in each industry's share of total value added, weighted by that industry's labor share. The effect is zero only if all sectors have the same labor share or if no reallocation of value added occurs.
