# Structural Equation Modeling (SEM)

## Origins and Development
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) is the name given to path analysis within the field of sociology. Sociologists initially embraced the use of diagrams inherent in Wright's method and used them extensively until the 1970s.

## Automation and Rote Application
The development of computer packages like LISREL automated the calculation of path coefficients, which led to a significant shift in how SEM was used. The practice turned into a rote method, with researchers becoming software users who often had little understanding of the underlying causal assumptions.

## Causal Disavowal
This mechanical application of SEM led to a crisis of identity for the method. In the late 1980s, a public challenge by statistician David Freedman to explain the assumptions behind SEM went unanswered. This culminated in some leading SEM experts disavowing that the method had any connection to causality at all, a betrayal of Wright's original vision.
