# Threat-Opportunity Time Gap

## Definition
The Threat-Opportunity Time Gap is a concept describing the natural delay between the perception of a new technology's threats and the realization of its opportunities. When a new technology like AI emerges, its capabilities can be easily mapped onto existing job tasks, making the threat of job displacement immediately and viscerally apparent to workers. This creates an initial period dominated by concern and anxiety.

## Asymmetry in Perception
The framework highlights a fundamental asymmetry in how threats and opportunities are perceived. Threats are concrete and easy to see because they relate to the automation of current, well-understood work. In contrast, opportunities are not as apparent early on because they require imagination and invention. People must figure out new ways to use the technology and discover new forms of value for human expertise, a process that takes time.

## Implications for AI Adoption
This time gap explains why conversations around new technologies are often initially dominated by fears of job loss, even if the technology ultimately creates new roles and industries. While acknowledging that some occupational categories will face real pressure, the concept suggests that a focus solely on immediate threats is shortsighted. It underscores the need for imagination and proactive effort to discover and build the new opportunities that technology enables, which are not obvious on day one.
