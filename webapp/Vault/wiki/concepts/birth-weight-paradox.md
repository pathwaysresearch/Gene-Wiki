# Birth-Weight Paradox

## The Paradox
The birth-weight paradox, based on data analyzed by Yerushalmy, is the counter-intuitive observation that while smoking contributes to low birth weight, within the group of low-birth-weight babies, those whose mothers smoke have a better survival prognosis than those whose mothers do not. This appears to contradict the known harms of smoking.

## Causal Explanation as Collider Bias
The text explains this paradox as a classic example of collider bias. The variable "Birth Weight" is a collider, influenced by two main types of causes: maternal smoking and other causes, such as serious genetic abnormalities. By looking only at babies with low birth weight, researchers are conditioning on this collider. This opens a spurious statistical path between smoking and mortality.

## The Causal Model
A causal diagram clarifies the situation: Smoking → Low Birth Weight ← Birth Defect → Mortality. When observing a low-birth-weight baby, if the mother is a smoker, this fact "explains away" the low weight, making a more dangerous cause like a birth defect less likely. Conversely, if the mother does not smoke, there is stronger evidence that the low birth weight is due to a more harmful cause like a birth defect, leading to a worse prognosis. This explains the paradoxical statistical finding without suggesting that smoking is beneficial.

## Scientific Controversy
The text notes that scientist Allen Wilcox challenged the idea that low birth weight is itself a direct cause of infant mortality in the 1970s. His suggestion, which helps explain the paradox, was highly controversial and he was accused of racism, delaying publication of his work until 2001.
