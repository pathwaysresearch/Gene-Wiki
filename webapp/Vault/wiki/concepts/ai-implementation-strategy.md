# AI Implementation Strategy

## Bottom-Up Innovation and Top-Down Commitment
A successful AI implementation requires a "meet in the middle" approach that combines two forces. The identification of potential use cases and the development of initial proofs-of-concept (POCs) should come from the bottom-up, from employees who understand the day-to-day operations. However, these POCs often become "dead zombie projects" without a firm top-down commitment from leadership to scale the successful ones and change the company's operating model accordingly.

## Sequencing over Prioritization
When undertaking a large-scale AI transformation, the challenge is often framed as prioritizing which projects to fund. The text, citing perspectives from the Boston Consulting Group, reframes this as a question of sequencing. In a true transformation, an organization will likely need to do everything eventually. The key is to sequence the projects and their scaling in a way that cleverly aligns with the overarching business strategy, rather than getting stuck in a simple prioritization game.

## Strategy-Driven Sequencing
The sequence of AI project implementation should be dictated by the company's core strategy. This means the projects chosen and the order in which they are scaled will differ based on strategic goals. For example, a company whose strategy is to be the low-cost provider would sequence a very different set of AI projects compared to a company aiming to be number one in customer satisfaction or new business model creation.
