# General-Purpose Technologies

## Definition and Characteristics
General-purpose technologies are innovations that have a wide-ranging impact across many sectors of the economy. Their potential often emerges over time through a broad variety of valuable use cases, which can make their ultimate effects difficult to predict and regulate. A key characteristic is their capacity not only to augment or replace existing work but also to enable the creation of entirely new types of work.

## Application to LLMs
Large Language Models (LLMs) are framed as a contemporary example of a general-purpose technology. Their ability to perform a wide array of complex language tasks means their applications are not confined to a single industry or occupation, but rather span the entire economy. This broad applicability is a core reason for the significant research interest in their potential labor market impacts.

## Adoption Barriers
The diffusion of general-purpose technologies into widespread economic applications is not always immediate. The text notes that "co-invention barriers" may initially impede their rapid adoption. This implies that for technologies like LLMs to realize their full potential, complementary innovations and adjustments in business processes and systems are often required.
