# Task Automation

## Definition
Task Automation is a form of innovation characterized by the displacement of labor from existing job tasks. It occurs when technology is developed to perform work previously done by humans. The conceptual framework, drawing on Acemoglu and Restrepo (2019), defines automation as a "labor-displacing" or "task-displacing" force, as it erodes employment in the occupations it affects.

## Measurement and Exposure
The study quantifies this force using an "automation exposure" (AutX) measure. This variable captures the degree to which occupations are exposed to automation innovations. Similar to the augmentation measure, it is calculated from weighted counts of matched patents, specifically identifying those innovations likely to substitute for human labor.

## Economic Impact
The empirical results confirm that automation has countervailing causal effects to augmentation. It is shown to erode employment in automation-exposed occupations. While the analysis of wagebills is complex, the findings suggest that automation has a negative effect on observed wagebills. The paper's central conclusion is that despite often co-occurring with augmentation, automation has a distinct and opposing causal effect on sectoral labor demand, reducing employment and wagebills.
