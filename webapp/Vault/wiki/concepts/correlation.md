# Correlation

## Galton's Discovery
Francis Galton's search for the causal laws of heredity led him to the discovery of correlation. It provided an objective, mathematical measure of how two variables are related to one another, independent of human judgment. The correlation coefficient, later formalized by Karl Pearson, became a universal tool for quantifying the strength of a relationship in a data set.

## Oblivious to Causation
A key point emphasized is that correlation is "oblivious of causation." It simply measures the degree of predictability between two variables, regardless of whether their relationship is causal, reverse-causal, or due to a common cause. For Karl Pearson, the mathematical precision of correlation made the "slippery old concepts of cause and effect" seem unscientific and outdated.

## The Abandoned Quest for Causation
The discovery of correlation is presented as an "irony of history," as it marked a shift away from the search for causal mechanisms toward purely statistical descriptions. However, the text notes that Galton himself retained some causal thinking, writing that correlation must be the consequence of "common causes" affecting both variables.
