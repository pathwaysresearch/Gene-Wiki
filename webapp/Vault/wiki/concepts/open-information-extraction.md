# Open Information Extraction

## Definition
Open Information Extraction (OIE) is an approach to information extraction that operates on a large scale, such as the entire web, to discover relational facts. As described by Etzioni et al. in a 2008 CACM article, its goal is to extract information without requiring a pre-defined schema or ontology.

## Methodological Context
OIE is part of a broader research agenda on "Machine Reading," which aims to enable computers to understand text, as outlined in a 2006 AAAI paper by the same research group. The development of OIE was informed by experimental work on "Unsupervised named-entity extraction from the web," which demonstrated the feasibility of extracting structured data from unstructured text without supervision, as detailed in a 2005 AIJ article.

## Key Characteristics
A key feature of OIE is its unsupervised nature. Unlike traditional information extraction systems that are trained to find specific relations, OIE systems identify a wide range of relations automatically, making them highly scalable for web-scale text processing.
