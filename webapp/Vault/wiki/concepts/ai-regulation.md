# AI Regulation

## Rationale for Regulation
The text argues that AI needs to be regulated because the companies that control it are poised to become extremely powerful. This concentration of power necessitates the creation of "guard rails to protect citizens" from potential negative consequences of AI deployment. The speaker, despite being pro-business, strongly advocates for some form of regulation.

## Proposed Principle of Awareness
One key concept proposed for regulation is "awareness." This principle would require a company to reveal when it is using AI to make decisions, particularly in socially consequential situations. Examples cited include using AI for loan approvals, recruiting, or criminal sentencing. This transparency would also involve revealing details about the AI system being used.

## Proposed Governance Body
The text suggests the creation of an "algorithmic safety board," modeled after an institution like the Federal Reserve. This independent entity would be tasked with monitoring the AI landscape, identifying emerging issues such as intellectual property concerns with generative AI, and determining when and how to intervene to mitigate risks.

## Implementation Approach
The speaker advocates for a phased approach to implementation, starting with policy prescriptions and self-regulation rather than immediate, binding legislation. This model, similar to suggestions made by the White House to companies, would allow for more flexibility in a rapidly evolving technological field.
