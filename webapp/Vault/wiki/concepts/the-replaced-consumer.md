# The Replaced Consumer

## Overview

'The Replaced Consumer' is a psychological perspective on the experience of AI delegation. The core idea is that the mere recognition of AI's capability to act as a substitute for human labor can be psychologically threatening to an individual's sense of self and efficacy.

## Contextual Factors

The feeling of being replaced is not uniform and depends on context. The threat to the self is stronger in symbolic consumption contexts where a person's identity is tightly linked to the task, such as a dedicated housekeeper being replaced by a Nest thermostat. It is also influenced by whether the consumer values the process of an activity (e.g., the skill of fishing) or just the outcome (e.g., a bigger catch), as AI tools might devalue the process.

## Managerial Implications

The text recommends that companies address this phenomenon through organizational learning. Firms should collaborate with social scientists to understand the consequences of human replacement and engage directly with consumers to learn which activities they prefer to reserve for themselves. This learning can be facilitated by hiring experts in human creativity into AI design roles and by drawing inspiration from institutions like museums and universities that enhance intrinsically 'human' abilities.
