# Automation (as a form of innovation)

## Definition
Within the provided economic framework, automation is a specific type of innovation aimed at replacing existing tasks previously performed by labor. It is measured empirically using "automation patents" and is represented by the variable I in the model. The incentives for firms to pursue automation are denoted by V_j^I.

## Relationship with Demand
Automation has an inverse relationship with positive demand shifts when compared to new task creation. Proposition 2 demonstrates that a demand shift towards a sector decreases new task creation relative to automation in other sectors, and decreases automation relative to new task creation within the favored sector. This suggests that as a sector's demand grows, the incentive to automate its existing tasks becomes relatively weaker than the incentive to create new ones.

## Economic Impact
The text links automation to potential contractions in employment and the sectoral wagebill for exposed skill groups. A key empirical test mentioned is whether the wagebill falls by at least as much as employment in occupations exposed to automation. The model shows that the rate of automation directly influences sectoral employment and wages, driven by specific innovation incentives.
