# Digital Transformation

## Core Engine
Digital transformation is characterized as an "inexorable engine of systemwide transformation" fueled by the continuous improvement of digital machines, a trend rooted in the principle of Moore's Law. This process is accelerated by advances in software, AI and ML algorithms, and computing architecture, leading to ever-increasing power and broader applicability of digital technology.

## Contrast with Industrial Revolution
Unlike the inventions of the Industrial Revolution which were often specific to certain industries (e.g., the steam engine in manufacturing and transportation), digital transformation is unique in that it cuts across every industrial environment at the same time. The text cites examples ranging from producing music and interpreting X-rays to trading stocks and performing predictive maintenance, highlighting its ubiquitous impact.

## Impact on Industry Structure
This transformation is causing traditional industry boundaries, which were formed to support vertical specialization, to disappear, making recombination the new rule. The text argues that success now depends less on traditional industry-specific expertise and more on a firm's network position, its accumulation of differentiated data, and its use of analytics. This allows companies like Amazon and Tencent to compete in disparate sectors like financial services and health care, all of which now rely on a similar technological foundation.
