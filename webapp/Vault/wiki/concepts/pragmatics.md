# Pragmatics

## Definition
Pragmatics is the process of completing the semantic interpretation of an utterance by incorporating context-dependent information about the current situation. After a grammar is used to derive possible semantic interpretations, pragmatics addresses the problem of selecting and enriching the correct meaning based on context.

## Resolving Indexicals
A key task in pragmatics is resolving the meaning of indexicals, which are phrases that refer directly to the context of the utterance. For example, in "I am in Boston today," the words "I" and "today" are indexicals. The hearer must use the current situation to determine who the speaker ("I") is and what the date ("today") is.

## Interpreting Speech Acts
Another crucial part of pragmatics is interpreting the speaker's intent, which is known as a speech act. The hearer must determine whether an utterance is a statement, a question, a command, a promise, or another type of action. For instance, a command like "go to 2 2" implicitly refers to the hearer as the subject, and the grammar must be extended to distinguish this command from a declarative statement.
