# Tolerance for Error

## Definition
Tolerance for error refers to the level of inaccuracy people will accept from a prediction machine. The text emphasizes that there is no single standard for what constitutes a 'good enough' prediction; success depends on strategically approaching the trade-offs inherent in each application.

## High Tolerance Scenarios
Users have a high tolerance for error when the cost of an incorrect prediction is low and the benefit of a correct one is tangible. The example given is Google's Inbox app, which suggests short email responses. At the time of writing, the feature had a 70 percent failure rate, yet users enjoyed it because the benefit of reduced typing for the 30 percent of useful suggestions outweighed the minor cost of ignoring the wrong ones.

## Low Tolerance Scenarios
Conversely, users have a very low tolerance for error in high-stakes domains like autonomous driving. The potential cost of a predictive error is extremely high, involving safety and human lives. This necessitates a much higher standard of accuracy and requires extensive, safe training methods, such as using specialist human drivers to supervise the machine's learning over millions of miles in varied environments.
