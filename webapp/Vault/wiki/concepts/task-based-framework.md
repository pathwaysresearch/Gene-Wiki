# Task-Based Framework

## Core Principles

The Task-Based Framework, introduced by Autor et al. (2003) and extended by Acemoglu, Autor, and Restrepo, models how new technologies change the task content of occupations. It posits that technology can displace tasks, alter the importance of remaining tasks, and lead to the creation of entirely new tasks for human labor. The framework highlights that new technologies will change the task content of occupations as some activities are performed by automation, while workers can focus on the remaining non-automated and potentially new activities.

## Key Technological Effects

The model identifies three primary effects of technology on labor. The **displacement effect** occurs when capital, such as AI or robots, substitutes for human labor in performing specific automated tasks. The **productivity effect** increases the demand for labor in non-automated tasks that are complementary to the new technology. Finally, the **reinstatement effect** involves the creation of new tasks in which labor has a comparative advantage.

## Implications for Wages

The overall impact of automation on wages is determined by the relative strength of these three effects. The framework provides a valuable structure for empirically investigating how technologies like AI reshape the nature of work and contribute to wage inequality by altering the demand for different types of tasks and skills. It is crucial for understanding which tasks and skill requirements are potentially replaced by new technologies and how these technologies subsequently affect the task and skill mix of occupations.
