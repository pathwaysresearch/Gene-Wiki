# Data-Driven Approach in AI

## Core Principle
The data-driven approach is based on the finding that system performance can be dramatically improved by increasing the amount of training data. This principle suggests that data quantity can be more impactful than algorithm choice.

## Supporting Evidence
A key finding by Banko and Brill (2001) showed that a mediocre algorithm with 100 million words of unlabeled training data could outperform the best-known algorithm that had only 1 million words. This demonstrates that the increase in performance from using more data can exceed any difference between algorithms.

## Example Applications
This approach has proven effective in various tasks. Yarowsky (1995) achieved high accuracy in word-sense disambiguation using a large corpus of unannotated text. Similarly, Hays and Efros (2007) used a large collection of photos to successfully fill in holes in a photograph by finding matching content.
