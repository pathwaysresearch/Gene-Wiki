# Internet of Things (IoT)

## Overview
The Internet of Things (IoT) refers to a class of product innovations where physical objects are embedded with technology to connect and exchange data, often to automate tasks previously performed by humans. These 'amazing machines' bring benefits by taking over routines and simplifying daily life. However, their value proposition is not purely functional and can intersect with deep-seated consumer motivations.

## Consumer Adoption Challenges
The adoption of IoT technology by consumers is not guaranteed and can be met with opposition. A key factor influencing adoption is the personal relevance of the tasks that the IoT device automates. While these innovations offer convenience, they can also be perceived as a burden if they interfere with activities that are important to a person's sense of self.

## The Role of Identity Motives
The likelihood of IoT adoption is strongly linked to consumer identity motives. When a task is highly relevant for a person's identity signaling, they are less likely to adopt an IoT device to automate it. For example, someone who takes pride in their cooking skills may resist an automated smart kitchen device. However, the same person might willingly accept IoT applications in other areas of their life that have less personal relevance. Therefore, managers must recognize that the consumer's quest for meaning can be a significant barrier or enabler for IoT adoption.
