# Solow Paradox

## Overview
The Solow Paradox is a concept articulated by Nobel laureate Robert Solow in the mid-1980s to describe the apparent contradiction between rapid investment in information technology and the slow growth of productivity in national economic statistics. His famous observation was that "you see the computer age everywhere except in the productivity stats."

## Resolution and Time Lag
The paradox was eventually resolved, but it highlighted that a significant time lag can exist between technological investment and measurable economic impact. Research in the mid-1990s by economists like Erik Brynjolfsson and Lorin Hitt showed that it took roughly a decade for the returns on IT investments to become clearly visible in productivity data.

## Role of Complementary Investments
The resolution of the paradox demonstrated that realizing productivity gains from a new technology requires more than just the initial capital outlay. It necessitates significant complementary investments in human labor, such as re-skilling the workforce, and fundamental changes in organizational workflows and business processes. It took about ten years for these complementary factors to mature and unlock the productivity potential of IT.
