# Qualitative Probabilistic Networks

## Overview
Qualitative probabilistic networks, developed by Michael Wellman, are a purely qualitative abstraction of Bayesian networks. They were created in part to address philosophical objections that probability theory's reliance on numerical calculations presumed an unrealistic level of precision in human uncertain knowledge and was not apparent to introspection.

## How They Work
Instead of using precise numerical values in conditional probability tables, these networks model the relationships between variables using the concepts of positive and negative influences. This captures the qualitative nature of dependencies (e.g., one variable's increase makes another more likely) without requiring exact probability specifications.

## Significance
This framework demonstrates that in many cases, purely qualitative information about influences is sufficient for an agent to perform optimal decision-making. By removing the need for precise probability values, these networks can simplify model construction and reasoning while still providing valuable inferences for decision problems.
