# Mediation Analysis

## Overview

Mediation analysis is the search for a mechanism, seeking to understand and quantify the pathways through which a cause produces an effect. It decomposes the total effect of a cause into a direct effect and an indirect effect that is transmitted through one or more intermediate variables, known as mediators. The Causal Revolution has developed clear and simple rules for this quantification, transforming mediation from a poorly understood concept into a widely used tool for scientific analysis.

## The Importance of the Correct Mediator

The historical example of scurvy highlights the critical importance of identifying the correct mediator. For decades, the scientific community was misled by an incorrect theory that ptomaine poisoning from tainted meat was the mediator causing scurvy. This led to the dismissal of the correct preventative measure, citrus fruits. The problem was only solved when the true mediator, Vitamin C, was discovered. This demonstrates that a misunderstanding of the mechanism can have severe real-world consequences.

## Modern Applications

Despite initial skepticism due to their reliance on counterfactuals, direct and indirect effects are now seen as overwhelmingly useful tools. Mediation analysis has been applied in various disciplines to gain new insights. Examples mentioned include re-evaluating an education reform called "Algebra for All" that initially seemed like a failure, and explaining why a study on tourniquet use in recent wars failed to show a benefit, which may have been masked without a careful mediation analysis.
