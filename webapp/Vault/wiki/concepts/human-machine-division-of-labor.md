# Human-Machine Division of Labor

## Core Principle
As prediction machines improve, businesses must adjust the division of labor between humans and machines. This collaborative approach works by combining the strengths of each to overcome their respective weaknesses, such as human weaknesses in speed and attention and machine weaknesses in interpreting text or understanding the data generation process.

## Distinct Strengths and Weaknesses
Prediction machines are better than humans at factoring in complex interactions among different indicators, especially in settings with rich data. Conversely, humans are often better when understanding the data generation process confers a prediction advantage, particularly in settings with thin data, because they possess cognitive models of how the world works.

## Example: Legal Redaction
The startup Chisel provides an example of this division of labor. Its machine suggests what information to redact in legal documents, and a human lawyer then accepts or rejects the suggestions. This collaboration saves significant time while achieving a lower error rate than humans could on their own.
