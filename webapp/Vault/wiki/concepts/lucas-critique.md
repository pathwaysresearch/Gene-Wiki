# Lucas Critique

## Overview
The Lucas Critique, formulated by economist Robert Lucas in 1976, is a principle that cautions against using historical statistical relationships to forecast the effects of new policies. It argues that if rational agents anticipate a policy change, they will adjust their behavior accordingly, which can cause the previously observed relationships in the data to break down.

## Original Economic Context
The text explains the critique using its original macroeconomic example concerning inflation and employment. Historically, data showed a tendency for high employment to occur when inflation was high. The critique points out that if a central bank were to implement a policy of deliberately increasing inflation to try to raise employment, people would anticipate this inflation, and the relationship would no longer hold. Therefore, policy cannot be based on simple extrapolation.

## Application to Prediction Machines
This economic principle is presented as highly relevant to the deployment of prediction machines in environments with strategic actors. AI systems that fight webspam or offensive comments, for instance, face adversaries who actively change their behavior to evade detection. The text notes that Instagram has had to repeatedly overhaul its AI-based filters to combat spammers and trolls. This demonstrates that, just as citizens react to economic policy, users can react strategically to an AI, making the problem a moving target and requiring that AI systems account for this dynamic.
