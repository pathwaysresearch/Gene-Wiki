# Adjustment Costs

## Definition and Integration
Adjustment costs are the economic frictions or costs a firm faces when it alters its rate of investment. The text incorporates this concept into its firm-level production model by modifying the production function following the framework developed by Lucas (1967). This allows the model to capture the reality that changing the capital stock is not a frictionless process and involves real resource costs, which can be modeled alongside other factors like unmeasured intangibles.

## Marginal Adjustment Cost
The text provides a specific formulation for the marginal adjustment cost, drawing on work by Hall (2000). The marginal cost is given by the equation c'(x) = q_t - 1, where c'(x) is the marginal adjustment cost function and q_t is related to the marginal value of installed capital (Tobin's q). This equation establishes that at the optimal investment rate, the marginal cost of adjusting the capital stock is equal to the premium of the market value of a unit of capital over its replacement cost.

## Relationship with Intangibles
The framework presented in the text is designed to accommodate both adjustment costs and unmeasured intangible investments simultaneously. It is noted that one interpretation of this combined model is that capitalized convex adjustment costs can be seen as a "nonlinear component of correlated intangible investments." This suggests a deep economic link where the costs of installing new physical capital are intertwined with the creation of firm-specific organizational or knowledge capital.
