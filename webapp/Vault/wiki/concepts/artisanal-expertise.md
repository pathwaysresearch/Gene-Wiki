# Artisanal Expertise

## Overview
Artisanal expertise refers to the deep, specialized skill of craftspeople, such as weavers and textile workers, which was highly revered prior to the Industrial Revolution. This form of expertise required years of apprenticeship to master a trade and was typically the domain of adult men, reflecting both the training time and restrictive gender norms of the era.

## Decline due to Mass Production
The value of artisanal expertise was ultimately decimated by the rise of mass production. The new factory system broke down complex craft processes into simple, mechanistic steps that could be performed by low-skilled workers, including women and children, aided by machinery. This made the holistic skills of the artisan economically uncompetitive.

## Historical Significance
The text uses the erosion of artisanal expertise as a historical parallel for understanding the potential impact of modern technology. The Luddites, skilled British textile workers who protested mechanization, are cited as having a justified fear of this technology because it rendered their specialized livelihoods obsolete. This historical event serves as a case study for how a dominant form of expertise can be displaced by a new production paradigm.
