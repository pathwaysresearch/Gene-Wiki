# Speech Act

## Definition
A speech act is an action performed by a speaker through an utterance. The theory of speech acts treats utterances not just as statements of fact but as actions intended to have an effect on the hearer.

## Interpretation
A key part of pragmatics is for the hearer to interpret the speaker's intent and decipher what type of speech act is being performed. This involves recognizing whether an utterance is a statement, a question, a command, a promise, a warning, or another type of action.

## Grammatical Realization
Different speech acts can be realized through different grammatical forms. While declarative sentences are typically statements, a grammar can be extended to handle other types. For example, a command can be formed from a verb phrase (VP) where the subject is implicitly the hearer. The grammar rules for the top-level sentence symbol `S` can be modified to include the type of speech act, distinguishing commands from statements.
