# Positive and Negative Phase

## Definition
When training an undirected model via maximum likelihood, the gradient of the log-likelihood with respect to the parameters θ can be decomposed into two parts: ∇θ log p(x; θ) = ∇θ log p̃(x; θ) - ∇θ log Z(θ). The first term, ∇θ log p̃(x; θ), is called the positive phase, and the second term, -∇θ log Z(θ), is called the negative phase.

## Role in Learning
The learning process can be viewed as a balance between two forces. The positive phase pushes up on the model's probability distribution in regions corresponding to the training data, maximizing log p̃. The negative phase, which involves samples from the model's own distribution, pushes down on the probability everywhere to minimize log Z and ensure the distribution remains normalized.

## Computational Difficulty
For most undirected models of interest, such as Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), the positive phase is tractable because it only involves evaluating the unnormalized probability for the given data. However, the negative phase is intractable because it requires computing an expectation over the model's distribution, which in turn depends on the partition function Z(θ). Various algorithms like MCMC-based methods, Contrastive Divergence, and Persistent Contrastive Divergence are designed to approximate this difficult negative phase.
