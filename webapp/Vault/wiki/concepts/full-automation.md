# Full Automation

## Definition
Full automation occurs when a task is performed entirely by machines and humans are completely removed from the loop. The introduction of AI to a task does not automatically imply full automation, as prediction is only one component of a decision, which also requires judgment and action.

## Conditions for Full Automation
A decision can be fully automated when three conditions are met. First, the prediction component must be good enough. Second, the judgment of payoffs can be pre-specified, either through hard coding by a person or by a machine learning to predict judgment from sufficient examples. Third, the action itself can be performed by a machine.

## Limitations and Exceptions
Full automation is not always desirable or possible, even when technically feasible. Some actions, particularly those involving social interaction like caring for the elderly or playing with children, may be inherently better when delivered by a human. Furthermore, legal or ethical constraints, as anticipated by Isaac Asimov's Three Laws of Robotics, may require a human to remain involved in certain actions to prevent potential harm.
