# Digital Amplification

## Definition
Digital Amplification refers to the way algorithms embedded in large-scale digital operating models can vastly increase the impact of certain messages or ideas. These algorithms, often designed to optimize for engagement metrics like clicks and views, can inadvertently promote content that reinforces biases, spreads misinformation, or encourages flawed thinking by targeting users most likely to be influenced.

## Mechanism
The process works through learning algorithms that are rewarded for user engagement. By identifying and serving content that aligns with a user's pre-existing views, these systems can create echo chambers. A 2017 study of 2.6 million Facebook users found that consumption of anti-vaccine content was significantly boosted by these echo chamber effects, where users primarily saw posts affirming their beliefs and joined groups that reinforced their biases.

## Scale and Impact
The vast scale and learning capabilities of modern digital platforms mean that harmful messages can be tailored and targeted to hundreds of millions of people. The anti-vaccination movement, for example, has existed for centuries but its impact has been magnified in recent years by social networks, video streaming, and ad-targeting technology, leading to measurable real-world consequences like a twentyfold increase in nonmedical vaccination exemptions for schoolchildren in Texas by 2018.
