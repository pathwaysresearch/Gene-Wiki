# Job Reconstitution

## Definition
A job is defined as a collection of tasks. Job reconstitution occurs when the implementation of AI tools leads to a change in this collection. This process involves some tasks previously performed by humans being automated, the ordering and emphasis of the remaining tasks changing, and entirely new tasks being created to complement the AI tools.

## How It Works
AI tools typically automate specific tasks within a workflow rather than an entire job. This transforms the nature of the job for the human worker. A key example is the introduction of the Automatic Teller Machine (ATM) in banking. The ATM automated the task of dispensing and collecting cash, but it did not eliminate the job of bank teller. Instead, the job was reconstituted; tellers were freed from transactional duties and shifted their focus to higher-value tasks such as marketing, customer service, and selling other financial products.

## Implications for Skills
Job reconstitution significantly shifts the relative economic returns to certain skills. As routine or predictive tasks are automated, the skills associated with them become less valuable. Conversely, skills related to human judgment, creative problem-solving, and the ability to effectively use the new AI tools become more valuable. For example, the arrival of the spreadsheet for bookkeepers diminished the returns to being able to perform many calculations quickly on a calculator, while it increased the returns to being good at asking the right questions to run strategic scenario analyses.
