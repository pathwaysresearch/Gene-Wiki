# Complements

## Definition
Complements are an economic concept where the value of one thing is enhanced when another thing becomes cheap. The text explains this principle with the analogy: "Just as a drop in the cost of coffee increases the value of sugar and cream," a drop in the cost of an input increases the value of its complements.

## Application to AI
This principle is central to understanding the broader impact of AI. The book applies it directly to the rise of cheap prediction. As AI lowers the cost of prediction, the economic value of its complements—such as data, sensors, and human judgment—increases significantly.

## Key Examples
The text provides specific examples to illustrate the concept. For autonomous vehicles, a drop in the cost of prediction increases the value of sensors that capture data. The multi-billion dollar acquisition of Mobileye by Intel is cited as evidence of the high value placed on a complement (data-collection technology) to prediction. Data and judgment are also identified as key complements.
