# Inverse Probability

## Definition
Inverse probability is the problem of determining the probability of a cause or hypothesis based on observed evidence or effects. This is in contrast to "forward probability," which involves calculating the probability of an effect given a known cause.

## Bayes's Billiard Table Example
Thomas Bayes illustrated this concept with a billiard table example. The forward-probability question is: given a table of known length L, what is the probability a ball lands within distance x of the end? The inverse-probability question is: having observed that a ball landed at distance x, what is the likelihood that the table's total length is L?

## Causal Motivation
The analysis of inverse probability was driven by causal aspirations. Thomas Bayes was concerned with the probabilities of two events, one (the hypothesis) occurring before the other (the evidence). His work was motivated in part by a desire to provide a mathematical framework for justifying belief in causes (like miracles) based on observed effects (like eyewitness testimony), in response to philosophers like David Hume.
