# Skill Generalization

## Definition
Skill generalization is the transformation of a specialized skill, previously the domain of a dedicated professional, into a generalist capability possessed by a much broader population. This shift is driven by technologies that lower the barrier to acquiring or performing the skill, effectively leveling the playing field. The key outcome is not that the skill becomes obsolete, but that the specialized role centered on it is no longer necessary.

## Mechanism and Examples
The text illustrates this concept with the historical example of typists. The invention of the word processor did not lead to typists being replaced by other typists using the new tool; instead, typing became a generalist skill that most professionals were expected to have. The specialized job function of a typist disappeared. The text posits that AI is driving a similar transition for modern specialized skills, such as certain aspects of coding and design.

## Impact on the Architecture of Work
When a specialized skill becomes a generalist one, it fundamentally changes the architecture of work. The specialized job function is eliminated, and the skill is integrated into the roles of many other workers. This is not a one-for-one replacement but a systemic restructuring of how work is done and how teams are composed. It represents a broad change in the division of labor within an organization or industry.
