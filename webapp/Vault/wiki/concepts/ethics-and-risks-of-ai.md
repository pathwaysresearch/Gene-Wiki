# Ethics and Risks of AI

## Overview
The development of artificial intelligence raises significant ethical questions about whether it *should* be pursued, given its potential for negative consequences. Like other powerful technologies such as nuclear fission and the internal combustion engine, AI could have unintended, harmful side effects, making it a moral responsibility for researchers to consider these risks.

## Societal and Psychological Risks
One major concern, articulated by Joseph Weizenbaum, is that AI research promotes the idea that humans are automata, potentially leading to a loss of human autonomy and uniqueness. This challenge to humanity's self-perception is compared to the societal shifts caused by the theories of Copernicus and Darwin. Another risk is the use of AI for undesirable ends, such as accentuating wealth inequality or for military purposes like autonomous weapons.

## Existential Risk
A more profound risk is that the success of AI might mean the end of the human race. With AI and robotics, a new problem arises where the technology itself, not just a human user, might constitute the 'wrong hands'. This theme, common in science fiction, raises the question of whether an AI system poses a greater risk than traditional software, for example if an ultraintelligent machine's goals are not aligned with human survival.
