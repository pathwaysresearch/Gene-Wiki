# Autonomy (in Agents)

## Definition
An agent's autonomy refers to its ability to operate based on its own experiences rather than being solely guided by the prior knowledge hard-coded by its designer. A rational agent should be autonomous, meaning it should learn what it can to compensate for partial or incorrect initial knowledge.

## The Role of Learning
Learning is the mechanism by which an agent achieves autonomy. By experiencing its environment, an agent can update its knowledge and adapt its behavior. For example, a vacuum-cleaning agent that learns to anticipate where and when new dirt will appear will perform better than one that relies on a fixed, pre-programmed strategy. This allows a single agent design to succeed in a vast variety of environments.

## From Initial Knowledge to Independence
Complete autonomy from the start is generally not required or practical. An agent with little or no experience would have to act randomly without some initial guidance. Therefore, it is reasonable to provide an artificial agent with some initial knowledge and the ability to learn, similar to how evolution provides animals with built-in reflexes. Over time, with sufficient experience, the agent's behavior can become effectively independent of its initial programming.
