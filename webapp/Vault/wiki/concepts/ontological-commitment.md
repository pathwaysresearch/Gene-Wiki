# Ontological Commitment

## Definition
The ontological commitment of a formal language describes what it assumes about the nature of reality. It specifies the categories of things that are presumed to exist in the world being represented by the language.

## Comparison Across Languages
Different formal languages carry different ontological commitments. For example, propositional logic's commitment is limited to facts. First-order logic has a richer commitment, assuming the existence of facts, objects, and relations. Other languages, like temporal logic, extend this further to include times.

## Significance
This commitment is a fundamental property that distinguishes various knowledge representation languages. It determines what can be expressed naturally and concisely within a given formal system, making it a crucial consideration for a student of AI when choosing a language for a specific task.
