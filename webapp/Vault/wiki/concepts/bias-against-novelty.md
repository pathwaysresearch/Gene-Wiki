# Bias Against Novelty

## Definition
Bias against novelty is the tendency within scientific funding and evaluation systems to disfavor ideas that are new and have never been tried before. As described by Karim Lakhani, this bias manifests when evaluators conclude that if an idea is novel, 'maybe it's not a good idea.' This perspective creates a significant barrier for researchers proposing unconventional or groundbreaking work.

## Impact on Scientific Exploration
Lakhani argues that this bias has a detrimental effect on the progress of science. He suggests that evaluators implicitly assume novel ideas are 'left tail distributed,' meaning they are overwhelmingly likely to be bad. This assumption leads to the rejection of a wide range of exploratory research. Lakhani contends that this bias is 'chopping off' a large and potentially valuable part of the scientific exploration landscape, preventing the pursuit of ideas that fall outside of established paradigms.

## Relationship to Funding Models
This bias is the primary justification for proposing alternative funding mechanisms like the lottery-based funding system. Such a system is intended to bypass the subjective judgments of reviewers that are prone to this bias, thereby giving novel ideas a chance to be funded and tested based on their potential rather than their conformity to existing knowledge.
