# Unmeasured Intangible Investment

## Definition
Unmeasured Intangible Investment refers to the creation of intangible capital that is not recorded as investment in standard economic statistics. The text models this as a component of true economic output, where adjusted total output (Y*) is the sum of measured output (Y) and unmeasured intangible investment (ΦI_U). The share of this unmeasured investment in true total output is represented by the parameter η. This form of investment creates an input, unmeasured intangible capital (U), which is a factor of production alongside traditional capital (K) and labor (L).

## Impact on Productivity Measurement
Standard productivity growth calculations (g_A) are distorted by the omission of intangible investment. The text demonstrates that an adjusted productivity growth measure (g_A*) is a convex combination of the standard measure and the growth rate of unmeasured intangible investment relative to ordinary capital. This adjustment accounts for an "investment effect," related to the growth of intangible investment itself, and a "capital stock effect," related to the growth of the accumulated intangible capital stock. Failing to account for these factors leads to an incomplete and potentially misleading picture of true productivity changes in the economy.

## Modeling Approaches
The text presents two primary methods for incorporating unmeasured intangible investment into economic models. The "right-hand side method" focuses on adjusting empirical estimates of productivity growth directly, as shown in the derivation of equation (6). Alternatively, the "left-hand side approach" redefines total output to include both measured output and unmeasured capital investment from the outset, treating Y* = Y + ΦI_U as the total output produced by the economy's factors of production. Both approaches aim to correct the mismeasurement and lead to the same adjusted productivity formulas.
