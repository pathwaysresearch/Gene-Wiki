# Judgment (in AI context)

## Definition
In the context of AI and decision-making, judgment is the critical component that involves assessing the payoffs associated with different outcomes. It is distinct from prediction. For example, while a machine might predict the probability of rain, judgment is the human process of weighing the happiness or unhappiness (the "payoff") of getting wet versus the inconvenience of carrying an umbrella. This assessment is essential for choosing an action.

## Increasing Value
As machine prediction becomes better, faster, and cheaper, it is used to make more decisions. Since judgment is a necessary complement to prediction in the decision-making process, the demand for and value of human judgment increases. The text argues that as we get more prediction, we will need more human judgment to make sense of it and determine the appropriate actions based on the associated payoffs.

## Cognitive Costs and Codification
Assessing payoffs for every possible outcome has cognitive costs for humans, requiring time and effort. For frequent or simple decisions with clear outcomes, judgment can be pre-specified and hard-coded into a machine. The customer service bot Ada does this for easy questions. However, for unusual, complex, or high-stakes situations (like a credit card network assessing fraud risk for a high-net-worth customer on vacation), nuanced human judgment is required because it is too time-consuming or difficult to specify all possible payoffs in advance.
