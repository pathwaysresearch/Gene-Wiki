# New Work Creation

## Definition and Measurement
New work creation refers to the emergence of novel job titles and occupational tasks within an economy. The text discusses measuring this phenomenon by identifying occupation titles that newly appear in historical records between different periods, such as between the 1930 and 1940 U.S. Censuses. 'Occupational new title shares' are used as a proxy to approximate the level of employment in this new work, and this approach is validated against individual-level data.

## Economic Incentives
The economic model referenced in the text suggests that there are persistent incentives for innovation that lead to new work. Under the assumption that the wage-to-productivity ratio is bounded, the text states it is 'always profitable to automate and create new tasks,' indicating that new work creation is an endogenous response to economic conditions and opportunities for innovation.

## Characteristics of Workers in New Roles
Empirical analysis of 1940 Census data reveals distinct characteristics of individuals employed in new work. These workers were found to be more educated and earned a wage premium relative to those in preexisting titles, supporting the hypothesis that new work demands scarce expertise. For instance, a $1,000 increment to annual earnings was associated with a 0.185 percentage point (a 13% increase relative to the mean) higher probability of being employed in a new occupation title.
