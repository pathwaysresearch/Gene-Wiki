# Decision-Driven Analytics

## Definition
Decision-Driven Analytics is a process framework arguing that to make good decisions with data, one must start from the decision itself rather than the data. It inverts the common data-driven approach by first identifying a business objective and the potential actions to achieve it, and only then determining the data and analysis required to choose the best course of action.

## The Process
The framework outlines a clear sequence of steps. First, a decision-maker must define the business objective and identify a set of feasible alternative courses of action that could achieve it. Second, they must formulate the specific questions that, if answered, would help rank these alternatives and determine which is best. Only after these first two non-technical steps are completed does the process move to the analyst, who is then tasked with finding the necessary data and performing the analysis to answer those well-defined questions.

## Contrast with Data-Driven Approaches
This methodology is presented in direct opposition to what is described as the 'data-driven approach'. The data-driven approach typically involves investing heavily in data infrastructure, such as data lakes and data oceans, with the general belief that something useful will eventually be found within the vast amounts of information. Decision-Driven Analytics critiques this as often being directionless and argues for a more targeted and efficient use of analytical resources by tying them directly to a specific decision from the outset.
