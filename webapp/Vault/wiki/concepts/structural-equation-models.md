# Structural Equation Models (SEMs)

## Overview
Structural Equation Models (SEMs) are a modeling technique used by economists and sociologists since the 1950s and 1960s. They consist of a set of equations intended to represent causal relationships among a set of variables.

## Historical Interpretation
The pioneers of these models, such as Trygve Haavelmo in economics and Otis Dudley Duncan in sociology, intended them to represent causal relationships. However, over time, this causal interpretation was often lost. Practitioners began to confuse the structural equations with simple regression lines, stripping them of their causal meaning and substance.

## Causal Reinterpretation
By the late 1980s, the causal foundation of SEMs was so obscured that many researchers in the field could not explain how to apply an intervention to their own models. The work on Structural Causal Models (SCMs) is presented as a restoration and formalization of Haavelmo's and Duncan's original causal intentions, re-establishing the equations as representing response functions to interventions and thus giving them clear causal and counterfactual interpretations.
