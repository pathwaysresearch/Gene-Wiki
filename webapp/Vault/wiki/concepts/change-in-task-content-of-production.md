# Change in Task Content of Production

## Definition
The change in the task content of production is a measure that captures how the set of tasks involved in production evolves due to technology. It reflects the net outcome of two opposing forces: the displacement effect, where automation eliminates labor-intensive tasks, and the reinstatement effect, where new technologies create new tasks for labor. A negative change indicates that displacement is outpacing reinstatement, leading to a decline in labor's share of income.

## Historical Trends
The analysis reveals a significant shift in this measure over time. While relatively stable in the 1947-1987 period, the change in task content of production became a major factor depressing labor demand between 1987 and 2017. During this latter period, it cumulatively reduced labor demand by 10 percent, contributing to the decoupling of the wage bill from productivity growth. This shift was driven by an acceleration of displacement and a simultaneous deceleration of reinstatement.

## Relationship with Economic Factors
The authors empirically test the relationship between their measure of task content change and various economic factors. They find a strong negative correlation with proxies for automation technologies and a positive correlation with proxies for the introduction of new tasks. The measure is also correlated with the offshoring of labor-intensive tasks but is found to be unrelated to imports of final goods from China, suggesting it specifically captures technological and organizational shifts in the production process.
