# Change as a Skill

## Definition

"Change as a Skill" is the concept that the ability to manage and undergo change is not just a temporary project but a core, ongoing competency for individuals and organizations. Karim Lakhani identifies this as a completely underrated but essential element for success in the current era, arguing that the best companies will be those that understand how to cultivate this skill throughout their workforce.

## Key Components

Lakhani breaks down this skill into three core components. First is the *acquisition* of the skill, which requires investment in learning what it means to change. Second is *practice*, meaning that organizations and individuals must engage in change continuously, not just sporadically. Third is *adjustment*, which involves learning how to project and scale the ability to change across the entire organization once it has been learned.

## Overcoming Resistance

This concept stands in direct opposition to the common organizational tendency to view change programs with skepticism and resistance, often dismissing them as a "flavor of the month." By framing change as a learnable and practicable skill, it shifts the focus from a passive resistance to an active capability that is vital for navigating rapid technological advancements. Lakhani cites Rakuten as an example of a company that has embraced this philosophy.
