# Scaling

## The Challenge of Scaling
As a company’s customer and employee count grow, scaling poses new challenges where activities that worked well in a startup stage are no longer practical or become too expensive. [Chunk 0] For example, close and frequent interactions with customers by founders are ideal early on but cannot be maintained at a large scale. This transition was highlighted by the experience of the company Yodle, which venture capitalists initially dismissed as a non-scalable "lifestyle business" because they doubted its model could be replicated beyond its initial city. [Chunk 2]

## The Role of Automation
Taking a business to the next level requires automating successful early-stage approaches through internal or external tools. [Chunk 0] The discussion focuses on how to scale key business functions like operations, customer acquisition, servicing, and retention specifically through the application of machine intelligence. This technological approach is presented as the primary solution for overcoming the manual limitations inherent in a growing enterprise. [Chunk 0]

## A Case Study in Scaling: Yodle
An example of successful scaling is Yodle, a marketing platform for small businesses. After being told its model was not scalable, the company grew from a $1 million run rate with six employees to a business with a run rate of over $200 million and 1200 employees when it was sold ten years later. [Chunk 2] This journey illustrates the process of overcoming initial growth hurdles to build a large, replicable business, validating the potential for scaling that was initially questioned. [Chunk 2]
