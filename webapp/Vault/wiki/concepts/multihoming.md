# Multihoming

## Definition
Multihoming occurs when participants of a network or platform concurrently use two or more competing services. For example, a ride-hailing driver might have both the Uber and Lyft apps open, or a rider might check prices on both before booking a trip. This behavior is common when the cost of adopting an additional platform is low.

## Impact on Profitability
Extensive multihoming can limit a platform's profitability and competitive advantage by intensifying competition. It allows users to easily switch to the most economical or convenient service at any given moment. The text identifies this as a significant challenge for Uber's ride-hailing business and a factor limiting profitability for Airbnb.

## Strategies to Reduce Multihoming
Firms employ various strategies to discourage multihoming and increase user "stickiness." These include signing exclusive contracts (e.g., video game consoles and publishers), increasing switching costs (e.g., high console prices), offering loyalty programs (e.g., usage-based rewards for riders), and providing value-added services that incentivize exclusivity (e.g., Amazon's fulfillment services for sellers).
