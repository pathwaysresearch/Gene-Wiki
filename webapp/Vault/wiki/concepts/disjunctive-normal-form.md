# Disjunctive Normal Form (DNF)

## Definition
A sentence is in disjunctive normal form (DNF) if it is structured as the disjunction of one or more clauses, where each of these clauses is a conjunction of literals. In simpler terms, it is an 'OR' of 'ANDs'.

## Example
The text provides the sentence `(A ∧ B ∧ ¬C) ∨ (¬A ∧ C) ∨ (B ∧ ¬C)` as a canonical example of a formula in DNF. This structure clearly shows three conjunctive clauses joined by disjunctions.

## Logical Equivalence
A key property of DNF mentioned in the text is that any propositional logic sentence is logically equivalent to a sentence in disjunctive normal form. This makes DNF a canonical form for representing logical statements.
