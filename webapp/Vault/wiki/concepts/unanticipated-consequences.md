# Unanticipated Consequences

## The Principle in Practice

The text highlights the "Law of Unanticipated Consequences," a principle stating that decisions and actions often produce unexpected results. This is shown to be particularly relevant for technological innovations that are deployed at scale, where unforeseen user behaviors and societal effects can emerge long after the initial creation.

## Case Study: Google Autocomplete

The creation of the autocomplete feature for Google by Kevin Gibbs serves as a key example. Initially designed as a side project to enhance user efficiency and save time, it was not foreseen that the feature would also promote harmful content. It was launched in 2004 but its full impact, including the negative consequences, became more prominent after it was made a default feature in 2008.

## Negative Outcomes

The unanticipated consequences of autocomplete included inadvertently directing users to hateful, violent, or biased content. The feature's suggestions, derived from public search queries, ended up reflecting and amplifying societal prejudices. This demonstrates how a tool built for convenience could have a damaging social impact that was not intended by its creator.
