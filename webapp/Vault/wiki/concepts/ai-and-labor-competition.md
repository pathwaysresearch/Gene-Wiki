# AI and Labor Competition

## Economic Principle
The economic impact of AI on labor is framed as a form of competition, analogous to opening up free trade. When AI, or "robots from Robotlandia," can perform tasks previously done by humans, they effectively increase the supply of labor for those tasks. This increased competition causes the price of that labor—human wages—to fall.

## Impact on Income and Inequality
This competition means that even if the total number of jobs remains stable in the long term, the quality and pay of those jobs may decline for certain workers. Individuals whose skills are in direct competition with AI may see their income go down or be forced into less appealing jobs. This raises a key policy question not about whether AI creates benefits, but about how those benefits and losses will be distributed across the population, potentially worsening income inequality.

## Competition for High Skills
A significant concern is that AI tools are capable of replacing not just manual labor but also "high" skills related to cognitive work and brainpower. This suggests that a broader range of the workforce, including highly educated professionals, may face increased competition from AI, a departure from previous waves of automation that primarily affected lower-skilled jobs.
