# Advertising Attribution Fallacy

## The Core Problem
The Advertising Attribution Fallacy occurs when businesses assume a strong correlation between ad clicks and sales implies causation. An ad click may be associated with a sale, but it is potentially a fiction to assume the ad *caused* the sale. The customer might have been planning to purchase the product regardless and simply used the ad as the most convenient link, meaning the ad spend did not generate a new sale.

## The eBay Experiment
In 2012, economists at eBay conducted a large-scale experiment to test the true return on investment (ROI) of their search advertising. While traditional statistics suggested an ROI of over 4,000 percent, the experiment revealed a different reality. By turning off all search advertising in one-third of the U.S. for a month, they found the ads had practically no impact on sales and the actual ROI was negative. Savvy eBay customers, not seeing a paid ad, would simply click on the organic search results for eBay instead.

## Implications for Measurement
This case demonstrates the critical need for deep examination and experimentation to determine the true causal effect of advertising. Simple correlation-based metrics can be highly misleading. The eBay study found that the only area where ads provided some benefit was in attracting entirely new users to the platform, highlighting the importance of understanding the specific, causal impact of marketing expenditures.
