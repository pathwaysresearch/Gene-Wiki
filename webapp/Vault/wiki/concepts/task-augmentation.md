# Task Augmentation

## Definition
Task Augmentation is a form of technological innovation that serves as a countervailing force to automation by creating new work. It involves the emergence of new job tasks or categories that require specialized human expertise. In the terminology of Acemoglu and Restrepo (2019), this process is described as "task-reinstating" or "labor-reinstating" because it expands employment opportunities in the occupations it affects.

## Measurement and Exposure
The study measures the impact of this phenomenon through a variable called "augmentation exposure" (AugX). This metric quantifies the exposure of specific industry-by-occupation cells to augmentation innovations. The measure is constructed based on the inverse hyperbolic sine (IHS) of a weighted count of matched patents, identifying innovations that are likely to create new tasks rather than replace existing ones.

## Economic Impact
The analysis finds that task augmentation has a significant and positive causal effect on labor demand. OLS and 2SLS estimates show that augmentation innovations lead to increases in both employment and wagebills, a finding that holds robustly across the 1940–1980 and 1980-2018 periods. Furthermore, augmentation exposure is positively associated with the emergence of new occupational titles. The impact on composition-adjusted wagebills is found to be modestly larger than the effect on employment, suggesting a positive influence on wages once changes in worker demographics are accounted for.
