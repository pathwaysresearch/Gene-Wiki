# Conscious AI

## Feasibility and Pathways

The text asserts that building conscious AI is 'clearly yes' possible in principle, as there is nothing 'spooky or mysterious' about consciousness in biological brains. The central question is not *if* it can be done, but *how*. The text explores two contrasting pathways: one where consciousness emerges spontaneously from sufficiently complex models, and another where it is deliberately engineered by creating more brain-like architectures.

## The Emergence Hypothesis

One view is that consciousness might simply 'come along for the ride' as language models become increasingly intelligent, suggesting that a system like 'GPT-100' might spontaneously have its 'inner lights come on.' The author finds 'very little to motivate this view,' suggesting it is rooted more in human biases and exceptionalism than in a concrete theory of how consciousness arises.

## The Brain-like Architecture Approach

A more plausible path, according to the text, involves making AI more 'brainlike.' Recreating a biological brain atom-by-atom would be sufficient to create consciousness. The key scientific question is how far one can abstract from that biological implementation. This points toward the potential of approaches like neuromorphic computing, which are designed to be more structurally similar to the brain than the current racks of GPU servers used for most large language models.
