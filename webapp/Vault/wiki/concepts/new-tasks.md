# New Tasks

## Definition

New tasks refer to the introduction of new work activities into the production process where labor holds a comparative advantage. While some new tasks can be capital-intensive, the framework's focus is on 'labor-intensive' new tasks that create new roles and responsibilities for workers. This process expands the total set of tasks in the economy and is a key mechanism for reinstating labor demand in the face of automation.

## Role in Labor Demand

The creation of new tasks is identified as a major source of growth in labor demand and a powerful counterweight to the displacement effects of automation. By creating new domains where labor is essential, this process ensures that technological progress does not make labor redundant. The text notes that empirically, about half of US employment growth between 1980 and 2015 occurred in occupations where job titles or tasks changed, highlighting the importance of this mechanism.

## Examples

Examples of new tasks span various industries and skill levels. In the high-tech sector, they include software and app development, database design and analysis, and computer security. New tasks also emerge as specialized functions within existing occupations, such as new responsibilities for administrative assistants, new analytical methods for loan applications, and new roles for medical equipment technicians. In textiles, examples include computerized design and new methods of market research.
