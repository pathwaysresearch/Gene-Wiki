# Optimizer's Curse

## Definition
The optimizer's curse is a bias where the projected benefits of a chosen optimal course of action frequently fail to materialize. This occurs because the process of selecting the best option from a set of uncertain estimates is biased towards options whose values were optimistically overestimated.

## Related Concepts
This phenomenon is closely related to **post-decision disappointment**, a term used in capital investment analysis. A well-known specific case is the **winner's curse** in competitive bidding, where the winner is very likely to have overestimated the value of the auctioned object.

## Underlying Cause
The text identifies the general statistical phenomenon of **regression to the mean** as the underlying cause for both the optimizer's and winner's curses. It suggests that a more complete Bayesian analysis can eliminate the problem by properly accounting for the selection process.
