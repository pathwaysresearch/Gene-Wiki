# Automation Exposure

## Definition
Automation Exposure is a metric that quantifies the degree to which an industry-occupation cell is exposed to technologies that substitute for or automate tasks previously performed by human labor. Like its counterpart, augmentation exposure, this measure is calculated as the Inverse Hyperbolic Sine (IHS) of the weighted count of matched patents, providing a standardized way to assess the impact of labor-displacing technology.

## Empirical Findings
Statistical analysis reveals a significant negative relationship between Automation Exposure and labor market outcomes. The stacked long-difference regressions in Table 6 show that higher automation exposure is associated with a decrease in both employment and the wage bill. This negative effect appears to have intensified over time. Table 8 shows that while the effect on employment was small and statistically insignificant in the 1940-1980 period, it became large and highly significant in the 1980-2018 period, with a coefficient of -2.06 in the OLS specification.

## Measurement and Trends
According to descriptive statistics in Table A1, the mean Automation Exposure at the occupation-decade level rose from 9.81 in 1940-1980 to 11.38 in 1980-2018, suggesting an acceleration of automation technologies in the later period. Similar to augmentation, the manufacturing sector exhibits a higher average exposure to automation (14.04) than the non-manufacturing sector (11.78) over the full 1940-2018 period, as detailed in Table A2.
