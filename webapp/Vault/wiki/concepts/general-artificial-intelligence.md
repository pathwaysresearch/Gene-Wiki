# General Artificial Intelligence (AGI)

## Overview
General Artificial Intelligence (AGI), sometimes referred to as superintelligence, is a form of AI that would possess broad, flexible, human-like intelligence. Unlike narrow AI, which is designed for specific tasks, an AGI could adapt its knowledge and skills to novel situations, much like a human can.

## Current Status
According to the text, the breakthrough that will give rise to AGI remains undiscovered. It cites a policy document from the Executive Office of the US President, where the National Science and Technology Council (NSTC) Committee on Technology concurs with the private-sector expert community that AGI is not an immediate concern and is likely far in the future.

## Contrast with Prediction Machines
AGI is explicitly contrasted with the narrow AI systems, or "prediction machines," that exist today. The text uses the example of AlphaGo Zero, which, despite its superhuman ability in the game of Go, would be unable to adapt to a minor change in the game's rules or perform a simple, unrelated task. A human, by contrast, would adjust to a different board size. This inability to generalize and perform a wide range of tasks is the key distinction between current prediction machines and the theoretical concept of AGI.
