# Structured Representation

## Definition
A structured representation is a way of modeling the world that describes it in terms of distinct objects and their various and changing relationships. This approach is necessary for understanding complex situations that cannot be captured by a fixed set of attributes, as is done in factored representations. For example, describing a scenario where a truck is blocked by a cow requires representing the truck and cow as objects and 'blocking' as their relationship.

## Expressiveness
Structured representations are at the high end of the axis of expressiveness, which also includes simpler atomic and factored representations. A more expressive representation can capture everything a less expressive one can, often far more concisely. The ability to explicitly model objects and relations is crucial for tasks that require a deep understanding of the world.

## Applications in AI
This form of representation is fundamental to many advanced areas of artificial intelligence. It is the foundation for relational databases, first-order logic, first-order probability models, and knowledge-based learning. It is also central to natural language understanding, as most human language concerns the description of objects and their relationships.
