# Preferential Attachment

## Definition
Preferential attachment is a principle that describes how connections evolve in a growing network. It posits that new connections are more likely to be made to nodes that are already well-connected. This process creates a "rich-get-richer" dynamic, leading to the emergence of a small number of highly connected and important hubs within the network.

## Origin
The principle was observed by physicist Albert-László Barabási and his colleagues in the late 1990s while analyzing the structure of the World Wide Web. They noticed that the web's network of connections grew constantly, and a small fraction of nodes became much more connected and hublike than others, attracting a disproportionate share of new links.

## Application in Business Strategy
The concept of preferential attachment is used as an analogy to understand business strategy in the digital age. In this context, firms can be seen as nodes in an economic network. By leveraging digital operating models, companies can attract more connections, benefit from network and learning effects, and become increasingly central and important hubs in their industry ecosystems.
