# AI Tool-Provider Competition

## A New Competitive Tension
Unlike traditional tools, where the manufacturer and the user operate in separate markets, AI creates a new competitive dynamic. The text explains that when a firm, such as a consulting or legal practice, uses a third-party AI, it is often simultaneously training that AI with its data and workflows. This creates a tension where the tool provider is not just a vendor but a potential future competitor.

## The Industry-Wide Utility Effect
As an AI tool is used by multiple firms within an industry, it aggregates their collective knowledge and learns from their operations. This process transforms the tool into an "industry-wide utility" that has deep insights into the business of its users. This accumulated intelligence enables the tool provider to "go deep into a solution provider's business," potentially offering similar services directly and competing with its own customers.

## Strategic Implications
This dynamic fundamentally alters the strategic landscape for firms. Relying on third-party AI without building significant proprietary value on top of it becomes a risky proposition. It means a company could be inadvertently training its replacement. This forces businesses to carefully consider the nature of their relationship with AI vendors and the importance of protecting proprietary knowledge in an era where data is a key input for competitive AI models.
