# Prediction Machines

## Definition
Prediction machines are a form of narrow artificial intelligence, as opposed to general artificial intelligence (AGI) or superintelligence. The text frames the AI discussed within its scope as these decidedly narrower systems, which are specialized for particular tasks rather than possessing broad, human-like cognitive abilities.

## Key Characteristics
The defining characteristic of prediction machines is their narrow focus and lack of adaptability outside their trained domain. The text uses AlphaGo Zero as a prime example. While it could outperform the world's best human Go player, it would struggle if the game board's dimensions were even slightly altered. This illustrates that its intelligence is confined to the specific rules and context in which it was trained.

## Limitations
Prediction machines are fundamentally limited and cannot perform tasks beyond their programming. The text humorously notes that one should not ask AlphaGo Zero to make a grilled cheese sandwich, as "it's not that smart." This highlights the absence of general problem-solving skills or common-sense reasoning that characterizes human intelligence and is the goal of AGI research. The breakthrough required for AGI remains undiscovered, and current AI is exclusively in the realm of prediction machines.
