# Firm-Time Effects

## Definition
Firm-time effects are a type of fixed effect used in regression analysis, particularly event studies with panel data. They account for any aggregate shocks that affect all workers within a given firm at a particular time, regardless of their individual characteristics like AI exposure.

## Application and Purpose
The study employs firm-time effects to rule out a class of confounding explanations for its findings. For example, industry-wide or firm-level shocks, such as changes in interest rates or demand, might correlate with both AI adoption and hiring patterns for young workers. By controlling for firm-time effects, the analysis isolates the impact of AI exposure on employment from these broader firm-level trends.

## Findings
The analysis shows that even after conditioning on firm-time effects, the negative employment impact on young, AI-exposed workers persists. The study finds a 15 log-point relative decline in employment for workers aged 22-25 in the most AI-exposed quintiles compared to the least exposed, demonstrating that the effect is not simply driven by undifferentiated firm-level shocks.
