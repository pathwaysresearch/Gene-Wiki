# Biological Naturalism

## Definition
Biological naturalism is a theory of mind, mounted by philosopher John Searle, that stands as a strong challenge to functionalism. It asserts that mental states are high-level emergent features that are specifically caused by low-level physical processes within the neurons of the brain.

## Core Tenet
The central claim of biological naturalism is that the unspecified, unique properties of biological neurons are what matter for the creation of mental states. Consequently, mental states cannot be duplicated in a non-biological system, such as a computer program, merely by replicating its functional or causal structure.

## Relation to Functionalism
This theory directly contradicts functionalism, which claims that any system with the right causal processes, regardless of its physical makeup, could have mental states. Searle uses the Chinese Room argument to provide intuition for his position that programs are not sufficient for minds.
