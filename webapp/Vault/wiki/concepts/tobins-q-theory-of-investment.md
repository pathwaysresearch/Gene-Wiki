# Tobin's q-theory of investment

## Overview
Tobin's q-theory is a framework for understanding investment decisions based on the ratio of a firm's market value to the replacement cost of its assets. The paper leverages this theory as a core component of its methodology for estimating intangible capital.

## Application to Intangible Capital Measurement
The paper applies q-theory to address the problem of productivity mismeasurement. The approach interprets part of a firm's 'q'—the ratio of its market value to its tangible capital—as a reflection of its intangible capital stock. Specifically, when a firm's market value rises by more than its observed tangible investment, the authors infer this difference as the value of intangible capital investments that were correlated with the tangible investment.

## Integration with Growth Accounting
The methodology combines Tobin's q-theory with neoclassical growth accounting. This integrated approach is designed to simultaneously estimate the magnitude of intangible investment related to General Purpose Technologies (GPTs) and account for the implementation lags that often accompany them, allowing for a more accurate measurement of true productivity growth.
