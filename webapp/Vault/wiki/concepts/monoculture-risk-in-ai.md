# Monoculture Risk in AI

## Definition and Analogy
Monoculture risk in AI refers to the danger that arises from homogeneity in prediction systems. The concept is drawn from ecology, where farmers planting a single, high-yield strain of a crop may see short-term individual benefits but create a system-wide vulnerability. If a disease specific to that strain emerges, the entire crop across a region could be wiped out. This is a situation where individual risk reduction leads to an increase in system-wide risk.

## Application to Prediction Machines
This principle applies directly to information technology and prediction machines. If one AI system, such as an algorithm for self-driving cars, proves to be the safest and is adopted by all manufacturers, it reduces individual-level risk for each car. However, this homogeneity creates a massive systemic vulnerability. An attacker who finds a single exploit in that algorithm could potentially manipulate all cars simultaneously, leading to a catastrophic failure.

## Nature of the Threat
The risk is not limited to malicious attacks. A universal prediction algorithm could have an unforeseen flaw or vulnerability to specific adverse conditions that would cause a massive, simultaneous failure across the entire system. The adoption of a single, superior system, while logical for individual actors, expands the chance of a large-scale, coordinated disaster, whether purposeful or accidental.
