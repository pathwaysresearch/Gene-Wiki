# Productivity Effect

## Definition

The productivity effect is the increase in labor demand that arises from technology-driven growth in value added. As technologies like automation make production more efficient, they increase the size of the economic pie. This expansion of the economy raises the demand for labor to perform the remaining, non-automated tasks.

## Role in Automation

In the context of automation, the productivity effect is the positive force that counteracts the negative displacement effect. While automation displaces workers from specific tasks (reducing labor's slice), the resulting cost savings and efficiencies increase overall output, which in turn boosts demand for labor in other areas (increasing the size of the whole pie). The net effect on labor demand depends on the relative magnitudes of these two effects.

## Role in Other Technologies

The productivity effect is also a key channel through which other types of technology, such as factor-augmenting advances, impact labor demand. For these technologies, the productivity effect is proportional to the factor's share in value added and is typically the dominant force affecting labor demand.
