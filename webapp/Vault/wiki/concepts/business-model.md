# Business Model

## Definition

A firm's business model is defined as the way it promises to create and capture value. It encompasses the company's core strategy, including how it seeks to differentiate itself from competitors by providing and monetizing its unique set of goods or services. The business model essentially defines the theory behind the firm's potential for success.

## Core Components
A business model consists of two primary elements that must work in concert. The first is value creation, which addresses the reason customers choose to use a company's products or services and the specific problem the company is solving for them. The second is value capture, which involves the method the company deploys to monetize the value it has created, ensuring its own profitability and sustainability.

## Relationship to Operating Model
The business model points to the potential value a firm could deliver, acting as the strategic goal. However, it is the operating model that serves as the practical enabler and ultimate constraint on that value. While the business model is the theory, the operating model represents the practice—what the people and resources of the firm actually do every day to deliver on the model's promise.
