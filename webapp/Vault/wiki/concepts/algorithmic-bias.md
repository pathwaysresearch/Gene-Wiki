# Algorithmic Bias

## Definition and Manifestation

Algorithmic bias occurs when automated systems embed and perpetuate societal biases present in the data they are trained on or the rules they follow. This can lead to prejudiced outcomes, as seen when search queries for gender or race reflect and amplify harmful stereotypes. The text notes that as these systems govern critical decisions once made by humans, such as by doctors and judges, the potential for biased outcomes becomes a significant concern.

## Examples

A prominent example discussed is Google's autocomplete feature, which has been shown to reflect societal prejudices found in search queries, sometimes directing users to hateful or biased content. The text also mentions biased beauty contests as another instance where algorithms can produce prejudiced results, demonstrating that the problem exists across different applications.

## Ethical Implications

The presence of bias in algorithms raises significant ethical questions about fairness and the reinforcement of societal inequities. Because these systems influence everything from shopping choices to whom people date, their capacity to embed bias can have a pervasive and often invisible impact on individual lives and societal structures.
