# Augmentation Exposure

## Definition
Augmentation Exposure is a quantitative measure designed to capture the extent to which an industry-occupation cell is impacted by technologies that augment human capabilities. The measure is constructed using patent data, specifically corresponding to the Inverse Hyperbolic Sine (IHS) of the weighted count of matched patents. This approach allows researchers to quantify the level of technology that complements labor in a given sector or job type.

## Empirical Findings
Regression analyses consistently show a strong positive relationship between Augmentation Exposure and positive labor market outcomes. In stacked long-difference regressions covering 1940-2018, higher augmentation exposure is significantly associated with increases in employment and the total wage bill. For example, one specification in Table 6 shows that a one-unit increase in the exposure measure is associated with a 1.36% decadalized increase in employment. This positive effect is observed in both the 1940-1980 and 1980-2018 sub-periods, as shown in Table 8.

## Measurement and Trends
Descriptive statistics in Table A1 show that mean Augmentation Exposure at the occupation-decade level increased from 10.02 in the 1940-1980 period to 10.98 in the 1980-2018 period. This indicates a rising prevalence of labor-augmenting technologies over time. Furthermore, Table A2 reveals that the manufacturing sector has a higher average exposure (14.06) compared to the non-manufacturing sector (11.98) across the entire 1940-2018 timeframe.
