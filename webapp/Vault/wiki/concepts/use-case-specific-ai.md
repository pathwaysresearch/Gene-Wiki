# Use-Case Specific AI

## Definition and Contrast with General AI
The text clarifies that the current reality of AI is not "general AI" but rather "very use case specific AI technologies and tools." [Chunk 7] This means that AI solutions are not capable of open-ended reasoning but are instead built to address particular, bounded problems. To be effective, a problem must be defined with a clear input and output that can be modeled computationally. [Chunk 7]

## Requirements for Implementation
Successfully applying use-case specific AI requires three key elements. First, the problem must be conceptually modelable. Second, a sufficient dataset must be available for training, though the text notes that technology is improving to require less data than in the past (megabytes instead of terabytes). [Chunk 7] Third, a practical "point of insertion" must be identified within an existing workflow, as AI solutions are not typically binary, all-or-nothing replacements for humans. [Chunk 7]

## The Importance of Product Definition
The success of an AI solution is described as being as much about "product definition as the technology." [Chunk 7] The genius lies in finding a point of insertion where AI can automate a portion of a task or workflow, such as 10 percent of the steps. It is not about simply replacing a human with a bot, but about intelligently augmenting human processes. [Chunk 7]
