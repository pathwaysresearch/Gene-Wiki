# AI Agent

## Definition and Characteristics
An AI Agent is defined as a tool distinct from a standard application, characterized by its ability to follow a user across various digital environments. The concept can be jarring for some, as it applies a level of autonomy and seriousness to familiar tools like Grammarly that users might not typically describe as an "agent". This distinction highlights a shift from passive tools to proactive assistants that operate with a higher degree of independence.

## A Three-Stage Functional Model
The functionality of an AI agent can be understood through a hypothetical "Duolingo agent" model. The first stage is passive observation, where the agent watches a user's activity—such as writing a document in Spanish—and gives them credit for language practice without requiring them to open the Duolingo app. The second stage is active interaction, where the agent modifies the user's environment by translating unknown words in a text or quizzing the user by changing known English words into Spanish. The third stage involves the agent remembering the user's progress and context to provide personalized assistance.

## Applications and Impact
The concept of an AI agent is demonstrated by real-world applications that function as coaches or assistants. Grammarly, for instance, acts as a "writing professor in your pocket" by providing real-time feedback that actively improves a user's writing skills, leading to better educational outcomes like increased pass rates in university writing courses. Similarly, Superhuman's "auto labels" feature allows users to give high-level, human-like instructions for sorting email, effectively delegating the task of inbox management to an intelligent agent.
