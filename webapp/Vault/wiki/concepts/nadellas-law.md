# Nadella's Law

## Definition
Nadella's Law is a term coined to describe the accelerated pace of innovation in computing efficiency during the current AI cycle. It posits that this efficiency is doubling every six months, representing a significant speed-up over historical trends.

## Contrast with Moore's Law
This concept is explicitly contrasted with Moore's Law, which governed the semiconductor industry for decades by predicting that computing power at a given cost would double approximately every 18 months. Nadella's Law suggests a new, much faster paradigm of progress is currently underway, driven by advancements specific to the AI era.

## Investment Implications
For investors, Nadella's Law serves as a powerful argument against being skeptical about the long-term return on investment in AI, even in the face of the current productivity paradox. The rapid, exponential improvement in the underlying technology suggests that one should not bet against this wave of innovation, as its foundational capabilities are advancing at an unprecedented rate.
