# Business Architecture (in the age of AI)

## Definition
As described by Karim Lakhani in his book "Competing in the Age of AI," business architecture refers to the foundational structure of a company. It is composed of two main parts: the business model, which dictates how the company creates and captures value, and the operating model, which defines how it delivers that value through scale, scope, and learning. The core argument is that AI is not merely a technology but a force that reshapes this entire architecture.

## Components of the Architecture
The business model is concerned with the logic of value creation and capture. The operating model focuses on execution and has three key dimensions: scale (serving many customers), scope (offering many different things), and learning (improving over time). AI directly impacts all these elements, enabling new business models and radically different operating models.

## Example: Ant Group
Ant Group serves as a prime example of a company with an AI-centric business architecture. By 2019, it was a full-service financial company serving 1.2 billion users with only about 10,000 employees. This is a stark contrast to traditional financial institutions, which would require millions of employees to operate at a similar scale. Ant Group's basis for competition is not human labor performing tasks but organizing machines and algorithms, demonstrating a fundamental shift in its operating model driven by AI.
