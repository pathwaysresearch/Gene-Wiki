# Sampling Bias

## Definition
A sampling bias is a flaw in a sampling method that results in a sample that is not representative of the overall population. This can lead to incorrect conclusions and predictions based on the collected data.

## A Famous Example
The text highlights the 1936 US presidential election poll by the *Literary Digest* as perhaps the most famous example of sampling bias. The poll sent mail to 10 million people, received 2.4 million answers, and incorrectly predicted that Landon would win with 57% of the vote. In reality, Roosevelt won with 62%.

## Causes and Types
The text identifies two primary causes for the bias in the *Literary Digest* poll. First, the source lists for the poll (telephone directories, magazine subscribers, club membership lists) tended to favor wealthier people, who were more likely to vote Republican. Second, a low response rate of less than 25% led to a specific type of sampling bias called *nonresponse bias*, which excluded key groups from the final sample, such as those who did not care much about politics.
