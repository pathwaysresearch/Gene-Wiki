# Dory and Pulse

## Overview
Dory and Pulse is a specific two-part process described as a "golden ritual" used within a company to structure meetings and formalize decision-making. It serves as a concrete example of a team ritual designed to improve collaboration and the quality of outcomes by addressing common meeting pitfalls.

## Part 1: Dory
The first part, Dory, is a method for focusing a meeting's discussion. It is designed to counteract the common problem where one person, often the loudest voice, dominates the conversation. By using this process, the team can surface and prioritize the topics that are of the most interest to the entire group, ensuring a more democratic and relevant discussion.

## Part 2: Pulse
The second part, Pulse, is a technique for gathering unbiased input when a decision is being contemplated. Each participant independently writes their view on the decision, often on a numerical scale with a written rationale, in a shared table. A key feature is that everyone's answers are hidden until all participants have submitted their input. This is explicitly designed to remove social bias and groupthink, as it prevents individuals from being influenced by the answers of those who contributed before them.
