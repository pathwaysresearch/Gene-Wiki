# Humanized AI Narrative

## Sociological Context

The text identifies the 'Humanized AI Narrative' as the sociological starting point for social experiences with AI. It is defined by the 'widespread cultural fascination with humanized machines, specifically, the preference for machines that emulate the human body and traits.' This narrative is amplified by the market prevalence of anthropomorphized chatbots and voice assistants.

## Cultural Manifestations

This narrative is a well-noted trope in science fiction, particularly in the form of 'gynoids' or female robots, which are often depicted as sexually obliging objects in works like 'Blade Runner' and 'Westworld.' These cultural depictions reflect and shape consumer preferences for AI that appears and acts human.

## Negative Social Consequences

While human-like characteristics can help overcome perceptual barriers to interacting with AI, the narrative also has a dark side. AI products modeled as 'obliging, docile, and eager-to-please' helpers can contribute to social alienation. Furthermore, this can reinforce harmful social dynamics, such as the objectification of women, as evidenced by findings that consumers express more frustration with female-voiced AI during conversational failures and may engage in denigrating behavior that mirrors real-world inequality.
