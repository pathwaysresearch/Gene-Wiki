# Enterprise Computing

## Overview
Enterprise computing refers to the diffusion of computing technology from its origins in scientific and engineering applications to the domain of business data processing. This transition represented a move to a much larger and more general form of end use, eventually becoming the core of a computing General Purpose Technology (GPT).

## The Key Transition
The critical step that brought electronic computation to business applications was the creation of computers that were compatible with existing business data processing (BCD) infrastructure. This included compatibility with data storage, retrieval, and input/output devices like card tabulators. This approach permitted incremental improvements to existing BCD applications, which lowered organizational adjustment costs and made adoption feasible.

## Technical Characteristics
A key distinction between early scientific and enterprise computing was the type of arithmetic performed. Scientific applications primarily involved floating-point calculations. In contrast, enterprise computing, once it became electronic, was dominated by integer arithmetic, which was suited for business data processing tasks.
