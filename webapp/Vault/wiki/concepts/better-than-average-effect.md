# Better-Than-Average Effect

## Definition
The 'better-than-average effect' is a psychological phenomenon where individuals tend to believe they are more skilled or competent than a typical person. This overestimation of one's own abilities influences their trust in external systems, including algorithms.

## Influence on Algorithm Trust
This cognitive bias directly contributes to a hesitancy to trust algorithms, particularly in domains where individuals perceive themselves as competent. This reluctance to cede control can hinder the adoption of new technologies.

## Application in Self-Driving Technology
A prominent example of this effect is the public's reluctance to fully embrace self-driving technology. Many individuals perceive themselves as better-than-average drivers and are therefore hesitant to trust an autonomous system over their own perceived abilities.
