# Preference-Driven Analytics

## Definition
Preference-Driven Analytics is a phenomenon where the personal wishes and desires of a decision-maker begin to shape the analytics process, undermining its objectivity. It represents a significant pitfall in making evidence-based decisions, as the goal shifts from an open-minded search for the best course of action to finding data that supports a predetermined conclusion. Stefano Puntoni notes that it is very difficult to ensure these preferences never play a role in analytics.

## The Problem
The core issue with preference-driven analytics is that it leads to poor decision-making. Managers may ask analysts vague or leading questions that are impossible to answer objectively with data, such as inquiring about 'brand purpose' or whether customers are 'engaged with brand positioning'. This directs the analytical work towards confirming a bias rather than challenging it, defeating the purpose of using data for insight.

## Mitigation Strategies
To safeguard against preference-driven analytics, leaders are advised to adopt several key attitudes and practices. The first is 'intellectual humility,' which involves acknowledging the limitations of what one knows and what can be known. The second is to be 'open-minded' by actively engaging with people who hold different perspectives. This highlights the organizational value of diversity, as it introduces different viewpoints that can challenge and refine the decision-making process.
