# Ladder of Causation

## Overview
The Ladder of Causation is a framework that outlines at least three distinct levels of cognitive ability that a causal learner must master: seeing, doing, and imagining. This hierarchy provides a structure for understanding and answering different types of causal questions, from simple observations to complex counterfactual scenarios. The ascent of this ladder represents a progression in the ability to reason about cause and effect.

## Level 1: Association (Seeing)
The first and lowest rung of the ladder is Association, which involves predictions based on passive observation. It is characterized by the question, "What if I see...?" This level is the domain of traditional statistics, dealing with the detection of regularities in data, such as correlations, regressions, and conditional probabilities like P(floss | toothpaste). However, while this level can identify that two variables are associated, it cannot by itself determine which is the cause and which is the effect.

## Level 2: Intervention (Doing)
The second rung is Intervention, which deals with predicting the effects of deliberate actions and is characterized by the question, "What if I do...?" An example is a manager actively setting a new price for a product to see its effect on sales, which may differ from past instances where the price was high for other reasons (e.g., market shortages). While controlled experiments are a direct way to answer such questions, the science of causal inference, using causal models, can sometimes make successful predictions even without an experiment.

## Level 3: Counterfactuals (Imagining)
The third and highest rung is Counterfactuals, which involves imagining and reasoning about what would have happened if things had been different. Characterized by the question, "What if...?", this level allows for learning from history and experience by considering alternative possibilities. This ability, which is linked to the Cognitive Revolution in human evolution, is crucial for understanding why events occurred and for taking corrective measures for the future. It is considered a fundamental aspect of human consciousness.
