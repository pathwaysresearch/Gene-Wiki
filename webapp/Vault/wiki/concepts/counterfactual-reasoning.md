# Counterfactual Reasoning

## Definition
Counterfactual reasoning involves evaluating hypothetical situations that are contrary to what actually happened. It addresses "what if" questions by imagining a fictitious world where a specific action or event was different and determining the outcome in that world.

## Example: The Firing Squad
The text illustrates this concept with a firing squad scenario. If Soldier A had not fired, the prisoner would still have died because Soldier B would have fired. A counterfactual analysis concludes that A's action did not cause the death, as the outcome would have been the same in the alternative world. This type of reasoning is used to assign responsibility and is a hallmark of human causal understanding.

## Role in Artificial Intelligence
Counterfactuals are presented as a key component of human-like causal reasoning that is difficult for computers. The "mini-Turing test" is proposed as a benchmark for an AI's ability to answer such questions. This requires teaching a machine to selectively "break the rules of logic" by modifying a model of the world to reflect the hypothetical scenario, a skill at which children excel but computers do not.
