# Technological Deskilling

## Definition as a Historical Trend
The text presents deskilling not as a new phenomenon unique to AI, but as a consistent consequence of technological advancement. It is described as a process where "technology is always deskilling us," leading to a state where people can no longer perform tasks that were common for previous generations because the technology now performs it for them.

## Illustrative Examples
To illustrate the concept, Stefano Puntoni provides several concrete examples from recent history. These include the decline in the ability to drive a car with a manual gear shift, navigate without GPS, or remember phone numbers without the aid of a digital contact list. These examples ground the abstract concept in relatable experiences of skill loss due to technological convenience.

## Implications for Generative AI
The concept is directly applied to the current wave of Generative AI. The speaker warns that users, especially college students, must be "thoughtful" in how they use these tools. Unmindful reliance on Gen AI for certain tasks could lead to the loss of underlying skills, highlighting the need for a conscious and balanced approach to its integration into workflows and learning processes.
