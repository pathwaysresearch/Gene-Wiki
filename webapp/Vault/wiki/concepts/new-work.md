# New Work

## Definition
New work refers to the introduction of new job tasks or job categories that require specialized human expertise. In the context of the paper by Autor et al., it is a key mechanism for counterbalancing the erosive effect of task-displacing automation on labor demand. The research aims to understand the substantive content of this new work, its origins, and its overall effect on the labor market.

## Measurement Methodology
To empirically measure new work, the authors construct a database of new job tasks introduced between 1940 and 2018. This is achieved by analyzing successive editions of the U.S. Census Bureau's internal Census Alphabetical Index of Occupations and Industries (CAI). New 'micro-titles' are identified when a sufficient number of Census respondents report performing a specific, previously uncommon activity as their primary occupation. Employment in new work is then estimated by multiplying the share of new micro-titles within a broader macro-occupation by the total employment in that occupation.

## Key Findings
The study finds that the majority of U.S. employment in 2018 is in new job titles that were introduced after 1940. The nature of this new work creation has shifted significantly over time: from 1940-1980, it was concentrated in middle-paid production and clerical occupations, but since 1980, it has shifted to high-paid professional and low-paid service occupations. The emergence of new work is strongly driven by augmentation innovations and positive demand shocks, and slowed by automation innovations and adverse demand shocks.
