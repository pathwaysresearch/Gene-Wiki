# AI Exposure Measures (AIOE, AIIE, AIGE)

## Overview and Methodology

The AI Exposure measures—AIOE (AI Occupational Exposure), AIIE (AI Industry Exposure), and AIGE (AI Geographic Exposure)—are designed to quantify how exposed different economic units are to developments in AI. The methodology is distinct from other approaches that look at AI or automation broadly. It works by first identifying specific applications of AI, such as image recognition and speech recognition, and then linking these applications to workplace abilities. Finally, these ability-linked AI exposures are aggregated up to the level of occupations, industries, and geographic regions.

## Key Distinctions

A primary distinction of these measures is their focus on specific AI applications rather than general automation, providing a more granular view. Another key feature is that the measures are constructed to be agnostic as to whether AI will serve as a complement or a substitute for human labor. This neutrality allows researchers to use the data to investigate the conditions under which either effect occurs. The measures also provide a snapshot of exposure based on the current nature of occupations, complementing other approaches that might focus on future automation potential.

## Applications in Research

The measures are intended for a wide range of research fields, including corporate strategy, human resource management, industrial organization, and the geography of innovation. The AIOE measure can be used to study individual and occupation-level outcomes, such as the relationship between AI, human capital, and labor productivity. The AIGE measure is applicable to studying regional-level outcomes, such as how a region's AI exposure might generate tacit knowledge and technology spillovers that foster future innovation. Researchers have already used the AIOE to study AI's role in entrepreneurship, its characteristics as a general-purpose technology, and its effect on firm hiring patterns.
