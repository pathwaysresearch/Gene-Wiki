# Back-door Criterion

## Introduction and Purpose

The back-door criterion was introduced by Judea Pearl in 1993. It provides a formal graphical method for identifying a sufficient set of covariates to control for in order to eliminate confounding bias when estimating the causal effect of one variable on another from observational data. It is accompanied by an adjustment formula for calculating this effect.

## Impact on Epidemiology

The criterion had a significant impact on the field of epidemiology by providing a clear and rigorous tool for dealing with confounding. The text points to the paper by Greenland, Pearl, and Robins (1999) as evidence of its influence and adoption within the field.

## Extensions and Generalizations

The back-door criterion is a foundational tool that has since been extended to handle more complex causal scenarios. The text mentions that extensions for sequential interventions and other nuances are developed in Pearl's later work. It is also a special case of the more general do-calculus.
