# Rational Agent

## Definition
A rational agent is defined as an agent that acts to achieve the best outcome or, when there is uncertainty, the best expected outcome. This approach to artificial intelligence focuses on designing agents that exhibit optimal behavior. An agent itself is broadly defined as anything that acts, originating from the Latin 'agere,' meaning 'to do'.

## Characteristics of Agents
Computer agents are expected to possess a range of capabilities beyond simply performing actions. These include operating autonomously, perceiving their environment, persisting over a prolonged period of time, adapting to change, and both creating and pursuing goals. These characteristics enable them to function effectively in complex and dynamic settings.

## Rationality vs. Correct Inference
The rational agent approach is distinct from the "laws of thought" approach, which emphasizes correct logical inference. While making correct inferences can be a part of rational action—for instance, by logically concluding that a certain action will achieve a goal—it is not the entirety of rationality. The text points out that in some situations, there is no provably correct action, yet an action must be taken. Furthermore, some rational actions, such as the reflex of recoiling from a hot stove, are more successful than slower, deliberated actions and do not necessarily involve logical inference.
