# Disparate Impact

## Definition
Disparate impact is a legal concept describing a situation where a seemingly neutral procedure or policy adversely affects members of a protected class differently than others. Unlike disparate treatment, which involves intentional discrimination, disparate impact can arise from actions that are accidental or not explicitly designed to discriminate.

## Application in AI Advertising
A 2017 study by Anja Lambrecht and Catherine Tucker demonstrated how disparate impact can occur in algorithmic advertising on platforms like Facebook. When placing ads for STEM jobs, Facebook's algorithm was less likely to show the ad to women. This was not due to discriminatory intent, but because younger women are a more expensive demographic for advertisers. The algorithm, optimizing for the highest return per placement, naturally showed the ads to the cheaper demographic (men), resulting in a gender-biased outcome.

## Legal Significance
As explained by economist and lawyer Ben Edelman, this type of algorithmic outcome can be legally serious for companies. An organization can be held liable for discrimination based on disparate impact, even if the discrimination was unintentional. This highlights a significant risk for businesses deploying AI systems that make decisions affecting protected classes in areas like employment and advertising.
