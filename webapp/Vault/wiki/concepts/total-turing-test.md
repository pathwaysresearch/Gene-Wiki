# Total Turing Test

## Definition
The Total Turing Test is an enhanced version of the original Turing Test proposed by Alan Turing. It expands the evaluation criteria by including a video signal, which allows an interrogator to test the subject's perceptual abilities. It also provides an opportunity for the interrogator to pass physical objects to the subject, thereby testing its ability to manipulate them.

## Required Capabilities
To pass the Total Turing Test, a computer system would need to possess capabilities beyond those required for the original test. Specifically, it would require computer vision to perceive objects and robotics to manipulate objects and move about. The text suggests that these disciplines, combined with the skills needed for the original test, compose most of the field of artificial intelligence.

## Relation to Original Turing Test
The original Turing Test deliberately avoided direct physical interaction between the interrogator and the computer, as physical simulation of a person was considered unnecessary for demonstrating intelligence. The Total Turing Test extends this scope to include physical and perceptual competence, representing a more comprehensive assessment of human-like intelligence. Despite the test's relevance, the text notes that AI researchers have generally focused more on studying the underlying principles of intelligence than on the specific goal of passing the test.
