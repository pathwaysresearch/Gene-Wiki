# Causal Models

## Definition
A causal model is a representation of the cause-effect relationships that structure our knowledge of the world. It can be an implicit mental model, such as the one used by early hunters to plan a hunt, or an explicit computational model. The text argues that these models, which form the basis for causal explanations, constitute the bulk of human knowledge and should be a cornerstone of machine intelligence.

## Key Properties
A key feature of causal models is their modularity. This property allows for experimenting with different scenarios by making local alterations to the model without reevaluating every other factor from scratch. For instance, hunters could mentally change the number of hunters in their party from eight to nine and reevaluate the probability of success based on that single, localized change.

## Representation and Application
Causal models are the arena where imagination takes place, enabling us to answer interventional and counterfactual questions. While they can exist in the mind, it is extremely useful to represent them explicitly when trying to solve unfamiliar scientific problems or emulate human thought on a computer. Causal diagrams, with their dots and arrows, are the primary tool for this explicit representation.
