# Architecture of Work

## Core Idea
The concept of the "architecture of work" argues that focusing on whether an individual will be replaced by AI is a framing error. It proposes that AI's true impact is systemic, altering the entire structure, logic, and flow of how work is organized. It is a macro-level change that redefines the relationships between different roles and tasks, rather than simply automating isolated activities.

## The Crane and Port Analogy
The text uses an analogy to clarify this idea: a dock worker worrying about a crane replacing their job is missing the larger point. The introduction of the crane (analogous to AI) changes the entire architecture of the port—it determines which ports become relevant, how logistics are managed, and how the global shipping system functions. Similarly, AI reconfigures the entire business ecosystem, making some workflows and skills obsolete while creating new ones based on a completely different logic.

## Implications for Strategy
This perspective suggests that both individuals and firms must think beyond task-level adoption of AI. The critical question is not just "How can I use AI?" but "How is AI changing the entire system I operate in?" A failure to understand this broader change in the architecture of work can lead to investing in skills or processes that are becoming systemically irrelevant, even if they are enhanced by AI in the short term.
