# AI Exposure Quintiles

## Definition
AI Exposure Quintiles are a method for categorizing occupations based on the degree to which their associated tasks are affected by or could be automated by Artificial Intelligence. The text uses this method to divide the labor market into five equal groups, from least exposed to most exposed, to study the differential effects of AI on employment and compensation. The analysis consistently compares trends for "More exposed" and "Less exposed" groups.

## Measurement Methods
The analysis employs at least two distinct methods for defining these quintiles. The primary method, used in figures like Figure 5 and A12, is based on the "GPT-4 β measures" developed by Eloundou et al. (2024). A secondary method, seen in figures A6 and A17, defines exposure based on "the share of queries to Claude that relate to tasks associated with an occupation," using data from Handa et al. (2025). This use of multiple measures serves as a robustness check for the findings.

## Application in Research
This classification is a central analytical tool in the paper. In numerous charts (e.g., A12, A23, A28), employment headcount trends are plotted separately for "More exposed" and "Less exposed" quintiles within different age groups. This allows researchers to isolate the impact of AI exposure from other economic factors by comparing the trajectories of workers in high-exposure jobs versus those in low-exposure jobs. For instance, Figure A23 specifically analyzes these quintiles for occupations with below-median interest rate exposure to further refine the analysis.
