# Causal Inference

## Definition
Causal inference is described as a "new science" that provides a unified framework for distinguishing cause from effect. It is based on the human realization that certain things cause other things and that manipulating the cause can change the effect. This framework aims to formalize this understanding with a mathematical language that was previously nonexistent in science.

## The Problem It Solves
Traditional statistics and data-centric approaches are described as being "profoundly dumb" about causality. They can identify correlations, such as patients who took a drug recovering faster, but cannot determine the reason why. Causal inference addresses this gap by providing tools to answer causal questions, such as whether a drug was truly effective or if a confounding factor was responsible. It provides a language to express causal convictions that standard algebra cannot, like the fact that atmospheric pressure causes a barometer's reading to change and not the reverse.

## Key Applications
The principles of causal inference have broad applications across diverse fields, including the development of new drugs, control of economic policies, education, robotics, gun control, and global warming. It allows scientists to address questions that were previously considered unscientific or beyond the pale of scientific inquiry, such as determining if cigarette smoking causes health hazards.
