# Lottery-Based Funding System

## Rationale
Proposed by Karim Lakhani, a lottery-based funding system is designed to counteract a fundamental bias against novelty in traditional science funding. He argues that the current model, where expert panels select what they believe are 'good ideas,' systematically filters out novel concepts under the assumption that if an idea is new and has never been done, it is likely a bad idea. This system runs counter to the conventional wisdom of relying on 'very smart people' to identify promising research.

## Challenging Assumptions about Novelty
Lakhani challenges the implicit assumption that all novel ideas are 'left tail distributed,' meaning they are inherently poor quality. He posits that novel ideas could instead be normally distributed, containing a mix of poor, average, and excellent potential. By rejecting novelty outright, the current system's 'bias against novelty is chopping off much of that part of the exploration in science,' thereby missing out on potentially transformative discoveries.

## Implementation Context
The text notes that this is not just a theoretical idea, as some experiments with lottery-based funding are already being conducted in Europe and by private foundations. Lakhani emphasizes that for such a system to be effective, it should provide researchers with substantial funds, enabling them to fully put their novel ideas into practice rather than just receiving small grants.
