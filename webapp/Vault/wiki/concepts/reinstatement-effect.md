# Reinstatement Effect

## Definition

The reinstatement effect is the mechanism by which technology creates new demand for labor, counteracting the job losses from automation. It is driven by the introduction of new tasks in which labor has a comparative advantage, thereby 'reinstating' labor's central role in the production process.

## Source

This effect originates from the creation of new tasks. As technology evolves, it not only automates existing work but also generates entirely new types of work, from software development and data analysis to new forms of marketing and management. These new tasks expand the total scope of work to be done and create new employment opportunities.

## Significance in the Task-Based Framework

The reinstatement effect is the conceptual counterpart to the displacement effect. The framework posits a continuous tension between automation displacing labor from existing tasks and new task creation reinstating labor into new roles. The long-term trajectory of employment and wages depends critically on the pace of this reinstatement relative to the pace of displacement.
