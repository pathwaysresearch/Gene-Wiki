# Counterfactuals

## Definition
Counterfactuals are questions and statements about what would have happened if things had been different. They represent the top rung of the Ladder of Causation and have been seen as a fundamental part of causality since at least the 18th century. The philosopher David Hume provided a key counterfactual definition of a cause: "where, if the first object had not been, the second never had existed.”

## Role in Human Cognition
The ability to reason with counterfactuals is presented as a key moment in the evolution of human consciousness and a uniquely human trait. This mode of thought is linked to the "Cognitive Revolution" and the ability to imagine things that do not exist, such as the 40,000-year-old Lion Man sculpture. This capacity for imagination allows humans to learn from history and the experience of others, something no other species appears to do.

## Applications and Importance
The rewards of counterfactual reasoning are immense. It allows us to understand why a blunder occurred in order to take corrective measures, or why a medical treatment worked for some patients but not others, which can lead to new cures. The profound importance of this type of knowledge is highlighted by the ancient Greek philosopher Democritus, who reportedly said, “I would rather discover one cause than be the King of Persia."
