# Simultaneous Equation Models

## Overview
In economics, the algebraic component of path analysis became known as simultaneous equation models. Unlike their sociological counterparts who adopted diagrams, economists almost never used path diagrams, relying instead on numerical equations and matrix algebra.

## Consequences of Abandoning Diagrams
A significant consequence of this diagram-free approach was the loss of a clear notational distinction between causal and regression equations. Because algebraic equations are nondirectional (e.g., x = y is the same as y = x), economists lacked a formal way to represent the direction of causation.

## Impact on Policy Analysis
This notational ambiguity hindered the ability of economists to answer policy-related questions, even after solving the equations. As late as 1995, most economists were hesitant to explicitly attribute causal or counterfactual meaning to their structural equations, limiting the models' utility for policy analysis.
