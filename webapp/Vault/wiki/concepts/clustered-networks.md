# Clustered Networks

## Definition
A clustered network is one that is grouped around individual local clusters, such as specific urban locations. In such a network, the value for a participant is primarily determined by the number of other participants within their local cluster, not by the total size of the global network.

## Competitive Implications
The more a network is fragmented into local clusters, the less impact global scale and network effects have on local competition. This structure makes it easier for challengers to enter the market, as they only need to reach critical mass in a single local network to compete effectively. Consequently, clustered networks are typically highly competitive, and the impact of scale is capped at the level required to serve a local cluster.

## Example: Uber
Uber's network is described as highly clustered. Drivers and riders in one city, like Boston, care almost exclusively about the number of participants in Boston, not in San Francisco. This means Uber's global scale does not confer a decisive competitive advantage in any single local market, opening the door to extensive competition across its core geographies.
