# User Control in Automation

## Core Principle
The concept of user control in automation posits that user confidence in automated systems is enhanced when people are given some form of control. This addresses the common fear and desire for control that often leads to resistance against full automation.

## Historical Example: Automated Elevators
The evolution of automated elevators serves as a key example. Users were initially resistant to fully automated elevators out of fear. The integration of minimal controls, such as a 'stop' button, helped ease this transition by providing a sense of agency and safety, thereby increasing user acceptance.

## Design Takeaway for AI
The primary lesson for designers of modern automated systems is that providing users with some form of control—however limited—can be a powerful tool for building trust. This sense of control can be crucial for easing the public's transition to accepting new automation and AI technologies.
