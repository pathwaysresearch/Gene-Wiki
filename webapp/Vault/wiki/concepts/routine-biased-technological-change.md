# Routine-Biased Technological Change

## Definition
Routine-biased technological change is a concept within labor economics that describes how technology disproportionately affects jobs. It posits that workers engaged in routine and repetitive tasks are at a higher risk of being displaced by automation technologies. This stands in contrast to non-routine tasks, which are more difficult to automate.

## Economic Context
This concept is a refinement of the broader theory of skill-biased technological change and is often analyzed using the task-based framework. Research in this area distinguishes between technology's task-displacement effects, where machines take over human tasks, and its task-reinstatement effects, where new technologies create a need for new, labor-intensive tasks.

## Consequences for Labor Markets
Several studies have identified routine-biased technological change as a significant driver of wage inequality in the United States. The displacement of workers in routine-task-specialized jobs has led to relative wage declines for this segment of the workforce, contributing to a widening gap between high-skill and low-skill workers.
