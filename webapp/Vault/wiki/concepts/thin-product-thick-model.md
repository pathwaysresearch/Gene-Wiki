# Thin Product, Thick Model

## Definition
The "Thin Product, Thick Model" approach is a strategy for building AI-native products, also referred to as a "model-first" approach. It is characterized by embedding the majority of the functionality, intelligence, and complexity within a powerful AI model (the "thick model"), while the user interface or application layer is intentionally kept simple and lightweight (the "thin product").

## Implications for Product Design
This strategy requires a shift in how products are designed, moving beyond a human-only focus to also building for AI agents. In this paradigm, traditional UI elements like visual layout or animations become less important than structuring data and APIs in a way that is easily consumable by Large Language Models (LLMs) and other agents.

## The Commoditization Challenge
A primary concern with this approach is the risk of product commoditization and the difficulty in establishing a sustainable competitive moat. If the core value resides in the model, which may be accessible to competitors, it raises the question of whether a competitor could quickly replicate the thin product layer or if the underlying model provider could eventually absorb the product's functionality.
