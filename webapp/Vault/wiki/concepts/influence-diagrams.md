# Influence Diagrams

## Purpose and Application
Influence diagrams are presented as a tool for decision analysis and reasoning in belief networks. A 1998 paper by R. D. Shachter, titled 'Bayes-ball: The rational pastime (for determining irrelevance and requisite information in belief networks and influence diagrams),' suggests their use for identifying relevant information and dependencies.

## Core Methodologies
The text cites works that establish key methodologies for working with influence diagrams. These include a 1986 paper by Shachter on 'Evaluating influence diagrams' and a 1990 paper by Tatman and Shachter that connects 'Dynamic programming and influence diagrams,' indicating a method for solving them.

## Relationship to Other Models
Influence diagrams are explicitly linked to belief networks. Shachter's 1998 paper discusses their role alongside belief networks, and a 1990 paper by Shachter et al. on 'Symbolic probabilistic inference in belief networks' further situates them within the context of probabilistic graphical models.
