# Operating Model

## Definition
An operating model is defined as the way a firm delivers value to its customers. It encompasses all the systems, processes, and capabilities that enable the delivery of goods and services. While a business model outlines the strategic goal for value creation, the operating model is the concrete plan and execution to achieve it, representing the practice of what the firm's people and resources do daily.

## Key Objectives
The overarching goals of an operating model are to deliver value at scale, achieve sufficient scope, and respond to changes by engaging in learning. Business historian Alfred Chandler identified the challenges of driving economies of scale and scope as crucial for a firm's survival and growth. The ability to learn and adapt represents a third critical challenge that a robust operating model must address.

## The Modern AI-Driven Operating Model
New operating models, such as that of Ant Financial, are increasingly built around a centralized data architecture to automate data-driven actions and remove human tasks from critical delivery bottlenecks. In this paradigm, as a customer interacts with a business process, software modules gather data, analyze needs, and interact with the customer to deliver value. This approach operationalizes and automates customer centricity in a scalable way, with AI and data-driven algorithms replacing labor as the primary constraint in many activities.
