# Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)

## Definition and Debate
Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) is defined, from an academic perspective, as a system that possesses human-level cognitive abilities. The debate around AGI centers on its feasibility and timeline. While it is widely believed that AI will increasingly replace tasks and workflows, the achievement of a system with comprehensive, human-like cognition is considered by some to be not very near.

## The Energy Efficiency Challenge
A significant challenge to achieving AGI is the immense energy consumption of current AI systems compared to the human brain. The human brain operates with remarkable efficiency, running at an estimated 20 watts. In contrast, the energy cost of today's AI infrastructure is already equivalent to that of a small country, highlighting a vast gap in efficiency that may hinder the development of true, brain-like general intelligence.

## Business and Investment Context
From a business perspective, the potential arrival of AGI is a subject of intense speculation and hype, with some leaders making bold claims that it will arrive in just a few years. This drives investment and strategic planning, but it is counterbalanced by a more cautious view that distinguishes between AI's ability to perform specific tasks and the achievement of a truly general cognitive system.
