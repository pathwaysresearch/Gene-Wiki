# Digital Addiction

## Definition
Digital addiction is a phenomenon where a user's engagement with an online platform becomes compulsive and difficult to control. The provided text describes it as a situation where someone intends to spend a short amount of time on a site like YouTube, but due to features like autoplaying videos, they find they have spent an hour and a half without realizing it. This is framed as a user's "inability to control myself" when faced with systems designed to hold their attention.

## Business Model Driver
The text explicitly links the creation of digital addiction to the advertising-based business models of most online media companies. These companies generate revenue by maximizing the time users spend on their sites, as more time translates to more ad views. This financial incentive drives them to develop features that foster prolonged and often unintentional engagement from users.

## Ethical Considerations
The speaker posits that actively creating digital addiction is "crossing the line" from providing a helpful service to engaging in manipulation. There is a significant gray area between what is helpful to a user and what exploits their inability to self-regulate. The development of features that lead to addiction is presented as an ethically questionable practice driven by a company's business model.
