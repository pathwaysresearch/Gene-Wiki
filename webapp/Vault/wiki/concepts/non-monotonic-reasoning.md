# Non-Monotonic Reasoning

## Definition
The provided text identifies non-monotonic reasoning as a specific form of logical inference within artificial intelligence. It is presented as a topic of formal study, distinct from classical monotonic logic where the addition of new axioms can only increase the set of provable theorems. This form of reasoning allows an agent to make plausible assumptions that can be retracted later.

## Formalisms and Key Work
John McCarthy's 1980 paper, "Circumscription: A form of non-monotonic reasoning," is cited as a key work in this area. This title suggests that circumscription is a specific, named formalism for achieving non-monotonic reasoning, likely by minimizing the extent of certain predicates or assumptions.

## Applications and Relevance
A 1998 paper by L. Morgenstern, "Inheritance comes of age: Applying nonmonotonic techniques to problems in industry," indicates that non-monotonic reasoning has practical applications beyond pure theory. The title suggests its use in solving problems related to inheritance hierarchies, a common challenge in knowledge representation, and that these techniques have matured enough for industrial use.
