# Easy-to-Learn vs. Hard Tasks (in AI)

## Definition of Easy-to-Learn Tasks
Easy-to-learn tasks are defined by two key characteristics: a simple, low-dimensional mapping between an action and its outcome, and the existence of a reliable, observable outcome metric. These are tasks that are relatively straightforward for generative AI to learn and implement. Examples include providing instructions for boiling an egg, verifying a person's identity, or composing well-known programming subroutines.

## Characteristics of Hard Tasks
Hard tasks are those where gaining expertise from outside observation is difficult. They lack a simple mapping between action and desired outcome, making it much harder to train AI models. These tasks often rely on human intuition, experience, and judgment. Because AI training data for such tasks often comes from observing human actions, AI performance is unlikely to surpass human performance or generate novel complementarities.

## Implications for AI-driven Productivity
The distinction is crucial for forecasting AI's economic impact, as productivity gains are expected to be much slower in hard tasks. The text argues that estimates from studies on easy tasks (like those involving GitHub Copilot) cannot be directly extrapolated to the rest of the economy. Cost savings from AI will be most pronounced in easy tasks that are expensive for humans, but are likely to be limited in hard tasks.
