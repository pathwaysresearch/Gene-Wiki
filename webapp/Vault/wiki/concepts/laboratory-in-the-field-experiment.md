# Laboratory-in-the-field experiment

## Overview
The study described in Ssrn 4573321 utilized a preregistered, randomized laboratory-in-the-field experiment to test the impact of AI on knowledge work. This method blends the rigorous control of a laboratory experiment with the real-world relevance of a field study (Chunk 14).

## Key Characteristics in the Study
The experiment maintained the controlled, randomized assignment of a lab setting, with subjects assigned to different AI treatment and control groups. However, it was conducted with a "highly skilled and motivated population" of professional knowledge workers rather than typical student subjects. The tasks were developed in collaboration with the participants' employer and were designed to "closely mirrored their professional activities" (Chunk 14).

## Significance of the Method
This methodology allows the researchers to assess AI’s impact on tasks that approximate real-world workflows, enhancing the external validity of the findings. By using professional subjects and realistic tasks, the study's conclusions about AI's dual role as a booster and disruptor are more directly applicable to professional environments, while acknowledging that the experiment does not capture every complexity of on-the-job work (Chunk 14).
