# Total Factor Productivity (TFP)

## Definition in Context

The paper explicitly distinguishes Total Factor Productivity (TFP) from average labor productivity (output per worker). TFP is used as the key metric for the overall efficiency gains generated by a new technology like AI, reflecting how effectively an economy combines its labor and capital inputs. In contrast, productivity improvement at the micro or task level is treated as being synonymous with cost savings.

## AI's Estimated Impact on TFP

A central objective of the paper is to estimate the impact of recent AI advances on aggregate TFP. The initial calculation, based on a Hulten's theorem framework and existing data, suggests a modest TFP increase of no more than 0.71% over the next 10 years. This serves as an upper-bound estimate for the medium-term macroeconomic consequences of AI.

## Downward Revision of Estimates

The paper argues that even this modest estimate could be an exaggeration. This is because the early evidence on AI's productivity effects comes from easy-to-learn tasks. The author posits that future effects from hard-to-learn tasks—which involve more context-dependent factors and lack objective outcome measures for training—will likely be smaller. Consequently, the paper suggests that a more realistic prediction for TFP gains over the next 10 years is less than 0.55%.
