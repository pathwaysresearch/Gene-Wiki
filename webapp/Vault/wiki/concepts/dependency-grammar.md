# Dependency Grammar

## Overview
Dependency grammar is a formalism used in both pure and computational linguistics that structures sentences based on the relationships between words. It is presented as an extreme form of lexicalization, where more grammatical information is stored in the lexicon (the dictionary of words) and less in the grammar rules themselves.

## Key Characteristics
In a dependency grammar, there are no syntactic categories in the traditional sense. Instead, the structure is defined entirely by the relations between individual words. This contrasts with phrase-structure grammars that group words into phrases and larger constituents.

## Comparison to Other Grammars
The text notes that carrying lexicalization to an extreme leads to formalisms like dependency grammar or categorial grammar. It also mentions that a version of dependency grammar has been shown to be easier to learn than Probabilistic Context-Free Grammars (PCFGs).
