# Probability of Sufficiency (PS)

## Definition and Contrast with PN

The Probability of Sufficiency (PS) is a metric used in causal analysis to assess the likelihood that a cause is sufficient to produce an effect. It stands in contrast to the Probability of Necessity (PN), which assesses if a cause was necessary for an effect that has already occurred. The two metrics can lead to very different conclusions about the same event.

## Application in Causal Disputes

The differing results from PN and PS can explain conflicting scientific conclusions. For example, in the analysis of the 2010 Russian heat wave, two different scientific groups reached opposite conclusions. One group, likely using PN, found a high likelihood that climate change was the cause. The other group, likely using PS, attributed the heat wave to a persistent high-pressure "blocking pattern"—a sufficient cause—and concluded that greenhouse gases had little to do with this specific phenomenon.

## Methodological Considerations

The text suggests that using PS as a metric for causation, especially over a short time period, sets a very high bar for proving a causal link. This implies that a low PS value does not necessarily rule out a causal connection, particularly when other measures like PN might indicate a strong relationship.
