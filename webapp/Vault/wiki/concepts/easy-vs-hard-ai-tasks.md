# Easy vs. Hard AI Tasks

## Definition and Rationale
The text introduces a distinction between "easy" and "hard" tasks to account for the fact that AI integration will face more difficulties in some areas than others. Easy tasks are those where current generative AI can readily achieve significant cost savings, while hard tasks are more challenging, potentially involving complex interactions, context, or lacking clear, observable metrics for success.

## Assumed Cost Savings
The author assigns different cost-saving estimates to each category based on existing studies. Easy tasks are assumed to have a 27% labor cost saving (translating to a 15.4% overall cost saving), while hard tasks are assigned a much lower 7% productivity gain (a 4.0% overall cost saving), motivated by being more challenging than the tasks in the Brynjolfsson et al. (2023) study.

## Impact on TFP Calculation
This classification is applied to the data on AI-exposed tasks from Eloundou et al. The analysis concludes that 74% of the wage bill of exposed tasks are easy (comprising 3.3% of GDP) and the remainder are hard (1.2% of GDP). Incorporating this split significantly reduces the estimated 10-year TFP gains from 0.71% to a more modest 0.55%, which the author considers a more reasonable upper bound.
