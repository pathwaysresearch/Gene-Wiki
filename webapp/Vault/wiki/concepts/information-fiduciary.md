# Information Fiduciary

## Definition
An information fiduciary is defined as a person or business that deals in information and has an obligation to act in a trustworthy manner in the interest of another. Citing Jack Balkin and Jonathan Zittrain, the text explains that unlike financial fiduciaries who handle money, information fiduciaries handle sensitive data and are obligated to keep secrets and not use the information they collect against their clients' interests.

## Traditional and Modern Examples
The text identifies doctors, lawyers, and accountants as traditional examples of information fiduciaries. It then extends this concept to modern digital platforms like Google and Facebook, which control major economic network hubs and acquire extensive consumer information, arguing they should be considered information fiduciaries.

## Responsibilities in Digital Networks
The concept is applied to large tech firms to argue that they have significant responsibilities not to harm the communities from which they collect information. As controlling hubs in economic networks, their role as information fiduciaries becomes critical to maintaining trust and the health of the network itself.
