# Skill-Replacing vs. Skill-Allowing Automation

## Definition
Skill-replacing automation is a type of automation framed as taking care of a task entirely for a user, such as a cooking machine where the "entire cooking process is completed for you at the touch of a button." In contrast, skill-allowing automation is designed to be more involving, assisting the user while still requiring their participation in the process.

## Experimental Evidence
A study conducted at Erasmus University explored this distinction in the domain of cooking. Participants were presented with either standard cooking tools (control), a skill-replacing automated machine, or a skill-allowing automated machine. The descriptions of the automated machines were held constant except for the framing of user involvement.

## Key Findings
The study measured how much participants liked the product, while also measuring their sense of identity related to cooking. The distinction between replacing and allowing skills is a critical factor in how consumers perceive and adopt automated products, particularly in domains tied to personal identity.
