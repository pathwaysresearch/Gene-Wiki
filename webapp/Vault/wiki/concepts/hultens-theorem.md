# Hulten's Theorem

## Overview

Hulten's theorem establishes a direct link between productivity improvements at the microeconomic level (e.g., for a specific firm or task) and growth in aggregate productivity for the economy as a whole. The paper states that as long as AI's primary microeconomic effect is cost savings or productivity improvements at the task level, its macroeconomic consequences will be governed by a version of this theorem.

## Application to AI

The paper applies this theorem to create a back-of-the-envelope calculation for AI's macroeconomic impact. According to this application, the aggregate gains in GDP and total factor productivity (TFP) from AI can be estimated by multiplying the fraction of tasks in the economy that are impacted by AI with the average cost savings (or productivity improvement) that AI generates at the task level.

## Implications for AI's Impact

Using the framework of Hulten's theorem, the paper concludes that the macroeconomic effects of AI, while nontrivial, are likely to be modest in the medium term. The initial estimate derived from existing data on AI exposure and task-level productivity improvements suggests a total factor productivity increase of no more than 0.71% over a 10-year period.
