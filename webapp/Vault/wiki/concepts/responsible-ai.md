# Responsible AI

## Core Components
A Responsible AI program is a formal initiative to manage the ethical dimension of artificial intelligence. The first step in establishing such a program is to create clear policies and to designate responsible roles within the organization to provide oversight. This governance structure is intended to guide the ethical development and deployment of all AI systems.

## Implementation in Industry
To date, most of the companies that have publicly established Responsible AI programs are vendors of AI products and services, including Google, Facebook, Microsoft, Salesforce, and IBM. The officers appointed to lead these efforts typically focus on internal evangelism to embed ethical principles in their company's products and services, as well as external communication to customers about the importance of AI ethics.

## Methods and Challenges
Some organizations have developed specific tools and methods to address ethical issues. For example, Google developed the 'model cards' concept to document data sources and algorithmic intent, which has also been applied at Salesforce. Facebook created a tool called 'Fairness Flow' to evaluate potential algorithmic bias in its machine learning models. However, these initiatives have faced challenges, with public controversy and instability affecting the AI ethics groups at major tech firms like Google and Facebook.
