# Operationality

## Definition
In the context of Explanation-Based Learning (EBL), operationality is a property of a subgoal within a derived rule. A subgoal is considered operational if it is 'easy' to solve, meaning it does not require an arbitrary amount of inference. For example, checking if a term represents a number might be considered operational, whereas a subgoal to simplify a complex algebraic expression might not be.

## Role in EBL
The purpose of enforcing an operationality criterion is to ensure that the rules generated by EBL are efficient and practical. When constructing a generalized proof, an EBL system can prune branches of the proof as soon as an operational subgoal is found, including that subgoal as a conjunct in the new rule. This prevents the creation of rules that are too computationally expensive to apply.

## Tradeoffs and Complexity
There is typically a tradeoff between a subgoal's operationality and its generality; more specific subgoals are often easier to solve but apply to fewer cases. Furthermore, operationality is a matter of degree and is not an absolute property. The cost of solving a given subgoal depends on the other rules available in the knowledge base, which can change as learning progresses, making the selection of optimal rules a complex optimization problem for EBL systems.
