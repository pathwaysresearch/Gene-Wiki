# Productivity J-Curve

## Definition
The Productivity J-Curve describes a pattern in measured productivity growth following the adoption of a new General Purpose Technology (GPT). Initially, as firms invest resources like capital and labor into accumulating unmeasured intangible capital stocks that complement the GPT, measured total factor productivity growth is underestimated. Subsequently, as the service flows from these hidden intangible stocks begin to generate measurable output, measured productivity growth overestimates true productivity growth, causing the error in measurement to follow a J-shaped curve over time.

## Causal Mechanism
The J-curve effect arises from a temporal mismatch between investment and returns in the presence of unmeasured capital. In the early phase of a GPT's adoption, firms commit measurable resources to build unmeasured inputs, such as new business processes or organizational know-how. This diverts resources from producing measured output, causing a dip in measured productivity. In the later phase, the accumulated (but still unmeasured) intangible capital stock acts as a productive input, generating measured output without a corresponding measured input, leading to an apparent surge in productivity.

## Relation to Historical Phenomena
The paper presents the Productivity J-Curve as a framework for understanding historical episodes of delayed productivity gains from technology. It is positioned as an explanation for phenomena like 'Solow's Paradox,' where computerization did not immediately show up in productivity statistics, and is compared to earlier GPT-related lags such as the generation-long reinvention of factory layouts during electrification and 'Engels' Pause' during the British industrial revolution.
