# Lady Lovelace's Objection

## Overview
The book references "Lady Lovelace's Objection," a concept later named by Alan Turing, to argue against the notion that AI is making "thought" itself cheap. The objection stems from Ada Lovelace's commentary on an early mechanical computer.

## The Core Argument
The objection's central claim is that a machine "had no pretensions to originate anything. It can do whatever we know how to order it to perform." Lovelace asserted that a machine can follow analysis but lacks the "power of anticipating any analytical relations or truths," meaning it cannot generate original ideas or think independently.

## Relevance to Modern AI
The authors contend that this objection still holds true for modern AI. Despite significant advances and popular hype, computers still cannot "think" in the human sense of originating novel concepts. Therefore, the economic revolution of AI is not about cheapening thought, but rather about cheapening a specific component of thought: prediction.
