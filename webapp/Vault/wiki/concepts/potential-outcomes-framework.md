# Potential Outcomes Framework

## Overview
The Potential Outcomes Framework, proposed by Donald Rubin in 1974, provides a formal language for discussing causality and counterfactuals within statistics. It legitimized the use of variables representing outcomes that could have happened under different conditions, which were previously considered unobservable and outside the realm of classical statistics.

## Key Idea
The framework defines the causal effect of a treatment on an individual by comparing their potential outcome if they received the treatment versus their potential outcome if they did not. For example, it considers variables like "Blood Pressure of Person X had he received Drug D" and "Blood Pressure of Person X had he not received Drug D" as legitimate objects of study, just like traditional observed variables.

## Impact on Causal Inference
This framework was a pioneering work that prepared the statistical community to accept counterfactual reasoning. By providing a formal structure for "what if" questions, it laid the groundwork for subsequent developments in causal inference, such as Greenland and Robins' concept of exchangeability, which brought counterfactual thinking into epidemiology.
