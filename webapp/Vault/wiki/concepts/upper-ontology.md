# Upper Ontology

## Definition
An upper ontology provides a general framework of concepts that are applicable across a wide range of domains. It consists of very general categories like "Anything," "GeneralizedEvents," "PhysicalObjects," and "AbstractObjects," which can be used to define more specific concepts.

## Structure
The concepts in an upper ontology are organized hierarchically, where lower concepts are specializations of upper ones. For example, "Humans" can be a specialization of both "Animals" and "Agents," indicating that specializations are not necessarily disjoint.

## Development and Challenges
The text notes that general ontological engineering has had limited success, with most major AI applications using special-purpose knowledge. Creating a shared ontology is often a social process, requiring agreement among parties. Ontologies that do exist have been created through various methods, including by expert logicians (e.g., CYC), by importing from databases (e.g., DBPEDIA), by parsing text (e.g., TEXTRUNNER), or by crowdsourcing from amateurs (e.g., OPENMIND).
