# Not All Creativity Is Worth Saving

## Core Argument

This concept, presented as a key takeaway from David Droga, challenges the sentimental notion that all creative jobs must be preserved in the face of technological change. The core argument is that a significant portion of work currently categorized as creative is not worth saving. This perspective reframes the impact of AI not as a threat to creativity itself, but as a filter for mediocrity within the creative industries.

## Eliminating the 'Mediocre Middle'

The argument is substantiated by the claim that much of what passes for creativity today is actually "confirmative research" or falls into a "mediocre middle." This type of work is often formulaic and lacks genuine originality. AI is particularly well-suited to automate or replace these tasks, and doing so is positioned as a positive development that can elevate the overall quality and impact of the creative field by removing uninspired output.

## Implications for Creative Professionals

This viewpoint serves as a call to arms for creative professionals to evolve beyond established roles and business models. Instead of defending commoditized tasks, creatives are urged to focus on their unique human abilities that AI cannot replicate. The true value of a creative person is in asking different questions, applying taste and strategic insight, and pushing into new, more relevant areas. The need for genuine creative thinkers and strategists is seen as greater than ever, provided they adapt to the new landscape.
