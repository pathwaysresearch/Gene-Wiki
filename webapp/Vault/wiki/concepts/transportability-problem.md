# Transportability Problem

## Definition
The transportability problem addresses the issue of generalizing experimental or observational findings from a studied population to a target population that may differ in significant ways. These differences can affect the causal relationships being studied, making the direct transfer of conclusions invalid.

## Graphical Representation
In causal diagrams, the problem is represented by introducing a "difference-producing" variable, denoted by `S`. This variable points to the characteristic by which the two populations differ. For example, if the target population is in Arkansas, and a studied population in Los Angeles is younger, the `S` variable would point to the age variable `Z`, indicating that age distribution is a source of disparity between the populations.

## Examples
The text illustrates the problem with an example of an advertising campaign. Findings from studies in Los Angeles (younger population), Boston (lower car ownership), San Francisco (higher click-throughs), and Toronto (randomized trial with higher click-throughs) may not be directly transportable to a target population in Arkansas due to these specific, causally relevant differences.
