# Demand-Driven Innovation

## Core Principle
The text outlines a model where the direction of innovation is not exogenous but is instead endogenously determined by shifts in consumer demand. This principle posits that changes in what consumers want to buy create powerful incentives for firms to innovate in specific ways to meet that demand.

## Mechanism
According to Proposition 2, a positive demand shift towards a particular sector increases that sector's output and prices. This change in market conditions alters the relative profitability of different innovation strategies. Specifically, it strengthens the incentives for new task creation more than for automation within that sector, while having the opposite effect on other sectors from which demand has shifted away.

## Implications for Labor Markets
This framework directly connects consumer behavior to labor market outcomes through the channel of innovation. By influencing whether firms focus on creating new tasks (augmenting labor) or automating existing ones (displacing labor), demand shifts can determine whether sectoral wagebills and employment expand or contract. This provides a mechanism for understanding how changes in consumption patterns can reshape the nature of work.
