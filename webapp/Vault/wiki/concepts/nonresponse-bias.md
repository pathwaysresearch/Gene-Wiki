# Nonresponse Bias

## Definition
The text defines nonresponse bias as a special type of sampling bias that is introduced when a low percentage of people who receive a poll choose to answer it.

## Mechanism
This bias works by ruling out specific groups from the sample population. In the case of the 1936 *Literary Digest* poll, the less than 25% response rate meant that the sample was missing data from people who did not care much about politics, those who disliked the *Literary Digest*, and other key groups, thus skewing the results.

## Impact
Nonresponse bias was identified as one of the two major flaws in the *Literary Digest*'s sampling method, contributing significantly to its famously incorrect prediction of the 1936 US presidential election outcome.
