# Environmental Cost of AI

## Overview
Beneath the promise of AI's potential lies the spiralling cost of its infrastructure, encompassing monetary, environmental, and social impacts. The so-called "cloud" is not immaterial but is rooted in an energy-hungry, carbon-intensive physical infrastructure required to power modern AI.

## Carbon Footprint
The emissions from digital technologies and data centres now exceed those from commercial aviation. According to Climatiq, digital technology accounts for an estimated 3.7% of global CO₂ emissions, compared to about 2.5% for aviation, underscoring the significant environmental footprint of the AI industry.

## Resource Consumption
Large Language Models (LLMs), which are central to many AI projects, demand immense computational capacity and vast amounts of water for cooling the data centers they run on. A study estimated that training GPT-3 in Microsoft's U.S. data centers could have consumed up to 700,000 litres of water. Even a single AI query on ChatGPT has a tangible cost, consuming about 0.34 watt-hours of energy and a small quantity of water.

## Mitigation Efforts
Despite the high costs, there are signs of progress in efficiency. Google's research on its Gemini applications shows that optimisation and clean energy procurement can dramatically reduce environmental impact. Between May 2024 and May 2025, Google successfully cut the energy required to serve a single Gemini text prompt by a factor of 33.
