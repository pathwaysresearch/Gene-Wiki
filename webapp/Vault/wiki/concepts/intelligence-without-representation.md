# Intelligence without Representation

## Definition
"Intelligence without representation" is a concept introduced in a 1991 paper of the same name by Rodney A. Brooks. The title itself encapsulates the core thesis: that intelligence does not necessarily require internal, symbolic models of the external world, a direct challenge to the prevailing view in AI at the time.

## Theoretical Context
This idea is presented as part of an "Engineering approach to building complete, intelligent beings," as described in a 1989 paper by Brooks. It advocates for a bottom-up, behavior-based approach where intelligence emerges from the interaction of simple, layered behaviors coupled directly to sensorimotor activity.

## Practical Application
The principles of this paradigm are reflected in Brooks's work on mobile robotics. His 1986 paper on "A robust layered control system for a mobile robot" describes an architecture that implements this philosophy, achieving complex navigation and behavior without a central planner or world map.
