# Intangible Capital

## Definition in Growth Accounting
Intangible capital represents a class of productive assets that are not measured in traditional economic statistics. These assets are true outputs when created as investment goods and serve as inputs into the aggregate production function once accumulated. Examples cited in the text include the business process redesign associated with new technologies like the electric dynamo or the proliferation of know-how that accompanied increases in literacy.

## Role in the Production Function
The paper's framework explicitly incorporates intangible capital (U) into the aggregate production function, distinguishing it from tangible capital (K) and labor (L). The true output of the economy is the sum of measured tangible output (Y) and unmeasured intangible investment (Iᵤ). The production function is thus expressed as Y + φIᵤ = A*F*(K, U, L), where A* is the true, factor-neutral productivity.

## Measurement via q-Theory
Because intangible capital is unmeasured by definition, the paper proposes a method to infer its quantity using Tobin's q-theory of investment. The core intuition is that when a firm's market value increases by an amount greater than its observed tangible investment, the difference reflects the value of unmeasured intangible capital investments. These are referred to as 'intangible correlates' of tangible investment.
