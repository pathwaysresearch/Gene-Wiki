# Interpretation as Abduction

## Definition
Interpretation as Abduction is a formal theory of natural language understanding proposed by Jerry R. Hobbs, Mark E. Stickel, Douglas Appelt, and Paul Martin in a 1993 paper in the Artificial Intelligence journal. The theory posits that interpreting a text is equivalent to finding the most plausible logical proof that explains the assertions in the text.

## Core Principle
The central idea is to treat the process of understanding sentences not as a direct translation or parsing, but as an inference problem. An interpreter finds the best "explanation" for why a sentence is true, given a background knowledge base. This abductive framework provides a unified way to handle various linguistic phenomena, including resolving ambiguity and identifying implicit information.

## Context and Influence
This theory is situated within the broader context of formal theories of the commonsense world, an area of research explored in a 1985 book edited by Hobbs and Moore. It has influenced the design of practical natural language processing systems, such as the FASTUS information extraction system, which applies these principles in a more operationalized manner.
