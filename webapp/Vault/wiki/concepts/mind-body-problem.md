# Mind-Body Problem

## Overview
The mind-body problem, considered by ancient Greek and Hindu philosophers, was first analyzed in depth by 17th-century philosopher René Descartes. It questions how the mind's activity of thinking, a process described as having no spatial extent or material properties, can relate to and control the physical processes of the body.

## Dualist Perspective
The dualist theory, articulated by Descartes, posits that the mind and body must exist in separate realms. This perspective's central challenge is to explain how these two separate entities can interact. Descartes speculated this interaction might occur via the pineal gland, but this hypothesis is considered to simply beg the question of how a non-physical mind could control a physical gland.

## Monist (Physicalist) Perspective
In contrast to dualism, the monist theory, often called physicalism, avoids the interaction problem by asserting that the mind is not separate from the body. This view holds that mental states are identical to physical states, such as the molecular and electrochemical processes of the brain. Most modern philosophers of mind are physicalists, and this view allows, at least in principle, for the possibility of strong AI.
