# Cognitive Psychology

## Core Viewpoint
Cognitive psychology treats the brain as an information-processing device. This perspective stands in contrast to behaviorism, which insisted on studying only objective, external measures of stimulus and response, and had less success in understanding humans.

## Historical Context
The cognitive viewpoint can be traced back to figures like William James, but it was largely eclipsed by behaviorism in the United States. It was able to flourish at Cambridge's Applied Psychology Unit, directed by Frederic Bartlett, and was forcefully reestablished by his student and successor, Kenneth Craik, in his 1943 work, *The Nature of Explanation*.

## Key Principles of a Knowledge-Based Agent
Kenneth Craik's work legitimized the use of "mental" terms like beliefs and goals, arguing they are scientifically valid constructs. He specified three key steps for a knowledge-based agent: (1) the stimulus must be translated into an internal representation, (2) this representation is manipulated by cognitive processes to derive new internal representations, and (3) these are in turn retranslated back into action.
