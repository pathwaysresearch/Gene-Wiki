# Functionalism

## Definition
The theory of functionalism says that a mental state is any intermediate causal condition between input and output. Under this theory, the specific physical implementation of a system does not matter, so long as the causal processes are isomorphic; there is a level of abstraction below which the specific hardware is irrelevant.

## Implications for AI
Functionalism's core claim implies that a computer program could have the same mental states as a person. If any two systems have isomorphic causal processes, they would have the same mental states, regardless of whether they are made of biological neurons or electronic circuits.

## The Brain Replacement Experiment
The claims of functionalism are most clearly illustrated by the brain replacement thought experiment. This experiment involves gradually replacing all neurons in a person's brain with microscopic electronic devices that mimic their input-output behavior, suggesting that consciousness would be maintained throughout the process.

## Challenges
Functionalism is strongly challenged by John Searle's biological naturalism, which argues that the specific properties of neurons are essential for creating mental states. Patricia Churchland also pointed out that functionalist logic could extend to replacing an entire brain with a giant lookup table, an outcome that many people find intuitively unlikely to be conscious.
