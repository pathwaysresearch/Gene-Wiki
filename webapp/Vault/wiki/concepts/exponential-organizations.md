# Exponential Organizations

## Definition
Exponential Organizations are a model for companies, typically digital and AI-native, that can sustain exponential growth in value creation once they achieve a certain scale. This is described as a "convex curve" of growth, which contrasts sharply with traditional organizations whose growth follows a "concave curve," where they scale quickly initially but then reach a plateau in their ability to serve more customers and create more value.

## Core Characteristics
These organizations are fundamentally structured with data cutting across the entire enterprise, breaking down traditional functional silos. They have automation at their core and are designed to "de-silofy" traditional ways of organizing. This allows them to effectively leverage the digital footprints and data generated from all aspects of their business, including internal operations, customer interactions, and supplier relationships.

## Strategic Implications
The emergence of exponential organizations creates significant competitive pressure on traditional businesses. Established companies must transform their own structures and operating models to compete with the exponential growth potential and efficiency of these new, data-centric competitors. The core challenge for incumbents is to rewire their organizations to operate more like these AI-first natives.
