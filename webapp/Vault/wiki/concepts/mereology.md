# Mereology

## Definition and Purpose
Mereology, from the Greek word for "part," is a theory that uses the part-whole relation as a substitute for mathematical set theory. It was originally proposed by the Polish logician Leśniewski with the aim of eliminating abstract entities such as sets. An alternative but related approach views a substance like butter as a single object composed of all buttery objects in the universe.

## Historical Development
The theory was first proposed by Leśniewski in 1916. A more accessible explanation of these ideas was later provided by Leonard and Goodman in 1940. Nelson Goodman's 1977 book, *The Structure of Appearance*, further applied these concepts to various problems in knowledge representation.

## Adoption and Critique
The mereological approach gained the support of philosopher W. V. O. Quine. Harry Bunt provided an extensive analysis of its application in knowledge representation in 1985. However, the text notes that some aspects of the approach are considered awkward, such as the requirement for a separate inheritance mechanism based on part-whole relations.
