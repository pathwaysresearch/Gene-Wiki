# Learning-by-Using

## Definition
Coined by economic historian Nathan Rosenberg, 'learning-by-using' is the phenomenon whereby firms improve their product design and performance through interactions with users in real-world settings. Rosenberg's original applications related to the performance of airplanes, which improved in capacity and efficiency as manufacturers learned from their operational use.

## Application to AI
The concept of learning-by-using is particularly important for prediction machines, which are fundamentally based on machine learning. As an AI is used 'in the wild,' the company can collect data on real-world outcomes to refine the machine and improve its predictions for the future. This creates a virtuous cycle where more use leads to more data, which leads to better predictions.

## Strategic Advantage
This process generates learning curves that confer a significant strategic advantage. Firms that get an early start can accumulate more data and learning through use, allowing them to improve their prediction machines faster than competitors. This can create a durable competitive advantage in markets driven by AI technology.
