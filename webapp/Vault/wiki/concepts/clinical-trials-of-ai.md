# Clinical Trials of AI

## Definition
"Clinical trials of AI" is a research framework for rigorously evaluating the impact of artificial intelligence on workers and organizations. This approach involves controlled experiments, often with a group using AI and a control group without it, to measure performance, efficacy, and other effects in a business context, analogous to testing a new drug.

## How It Works
As described by Karim Lakhani, these trials are conducted within organizations like the Boston Consulting Group. In one study, 758 consultants were divided into groups with and without AI access to perform creative and analytical tasks. The goal is to determine AI's efficacy, side effects, and toxicity, moving beyond anecdotal evidence to provide robust data.

## Purpose and Application
The purpose of these trials is to provide leaders with evidence-based guidance on how to strategically implement AI. The findings help identify the "jagged technological frontier" of AI—the specific tasks where it excels and where it may be harmful—thereby informing strategic decisions about AI adoption and workforce integration.
