# Automation Innovations

## Definition
Automation innovations are defined as technological advancements that substitute labor with capital, thereby automating tasks that workers perform. The study's theoretical model, extending Acemoglu and Restrepo (2018), treats task automation as an endogenous choice by entrepreneurs in response to factor prices. This concept is set in direct contrast to augmentation innovations, which complement labor.

## Measurement
The measurement of automation innovations is conducted in parallel with that of augmentation, using the full corpus of U.S. utility patents from 1940 to 2018. The methodology links patent text to occupational content. The key difference is the source text used to characterize occupations: for automation, the study uses the detailed task descriptions found in the Dictionary of Occupational Titles (DOT) to identify innovations that substitute for specific worker inputs.

## Economic Impact
Despite being positively correlated with augmentation innovations across occupations, automation innovations have a distinct and asymmetric impact. The study finds that automation innovations do not predict where new work emerges and, in fact, slow its creation. Occupations more exposed to automation innovations experience a contraction in employment and their total wage bills, demonstrating a direct, task-displacing effect on labor demand.
