# Filter Bubble

## Definition
A filter bubble is a phenomenon, particularly prevalent in social media and news feeds, where algorithms create a personalized environment for a user that limits their exposure to differing perspectives. This can lead to a feedback loop where a user's existing views are constantly reinforced, potentially shaping their opinions and even actions like voting.

## Mechanism as a Feedback Loop
The filter bubble operates as a powerful feedback loop. A user's consumption habits—what they like and view—influence the algorithm's decision of what content to show them next. This new content, being similar to the old, further reinforces the user's preferences, trapping them in a cycle unless there is an intentional intervention by the user or the algorithm's designer to introduce variation.

## Driving Forces
According to Kartik Hosanagar, filter bubbles are a direct consequence of the business models of big tech companies. These companies operate in an 'attention economy,' where their primary incentive is to maximize the time users spend on their platforms. Algorithms that create filter bubbles are effective at this because showing users content they already agree with is a reliable way to maintain engagement and, consequently, revenue.
