# Known Unknowns

## Definition
"Known unknowns" describe situations where there is a known lack of sufficient data, making it clear that prediction will be difficult. This corresponds to Donald Rumsfeld's idea of "things we know there are some things we do not know."

## Impact on Prediction
In this scenario, a prediction is understood to be inaccurate. A prediction from a machine would likely come with a confidence range that reveals its imprecision, and humans would be aware of this limitation. Humans are often better than machines in these settings with thin data.

## Part of a Larger Framework
This concept is one of four categories in a taxonomy used to understand the conditions under which prediction machines succeed or falter. The other categories are known knowns, unknown unknowns, and unknown knowns.
