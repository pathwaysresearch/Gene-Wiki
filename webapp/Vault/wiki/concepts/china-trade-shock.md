# China Trade Shock

## Overview
The China Trade Shock refers to the significant increase in U.S. import competition from China, which is used in the study as a major source of negative demand shifts affecting American occupations. The analysis examines the period from 1990 to 2014 to understand how these demand contractions influenced the labor market.

## Occupational Exposure
The text highlights that exposure to the China trade shock varied substantially, not only between broad occupational categories but also within them. For example, within production occupations, textile sewing machine operators were among the most trade-exposed, whereas power plant operators were far less so. Similarly, among professionals, electrical engineers were more exposed than the average production worker, while primary school teachers were relatively unexposed.

## Impact on New Work
The study estimates the effect of this trade-induced demand contraction on the creation of new job titles. The results show a negative relationship, indicating that occupations more exposed to import competition from China experienced less emergence of new titles. This effect becomes larger in magnitude and more statistically precise when the model includes fixed effects for broad occupational groups, isolating variation across detailed occupations within those groups.
