# Microworlds

## Definition
Microworlds are limited problem domains that were chosen for early AI research because they appeared to require intelligence to solve, but were simple enough to be tractable. This approach was notably championed by Marvin Minsky, who supervised a series of students working on such problems.

## Rationale
By focusing on constrained environments, researchers could develop and test AI programs on challenging problems without having to contend with the full, unconstrained complexity of the real world. This allowed for progress on core issues of reasoning and problem-solving.

## Examples
Notable programs developed within the microworlds paradigm include James Slagle's SAINT program (1963), which could solve closed-form calculus integration problems; Tom Evans's ANALOGY program (1968), which solved geometric analogy problems from IQ tests; and Daniel Bobrow's STUDENT program (1967), which solved algebra story problems.
