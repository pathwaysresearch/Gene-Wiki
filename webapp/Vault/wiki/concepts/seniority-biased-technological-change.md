# Seniority-Biased Technological Change

## Definition
Seniority-Biased Technological Change is a framework for analyzing the labor market effects of technology, positing that the impact on employment and compensation differs significantly across various age and career stages. The provided text investigates this concept in the context of Generative AI, using a detailed breakdown of the workforce into six distinct seniority groups based on age, as mentioned in the title of the Hosseini and Lichtinger (2025) working paper.

## Application in Analysis
The research presented consistently applies this concept by segmenting the labor market into six groups: Early Career 1 (22-25), Early Career 2 (26-30), Developing (31-34), Mid-Career 1 (35-40), Mid-Career 2 (41-49), and Senior (50+). The analysis then examines how AI exposure affects employment trends (headcount) and compensation for each of these groups separately, as shown in numerous figures (e.g., Figure 1, A12, A17, A23). This granular approach is designed to reveal differential impacts that would be obscured by an aggregate analysis.

## Implied Findings
The charts consistently show a divergence in employment trends between workers more and less exposed to AI, particularly after late 2022 or early 2023. For example, Figure A12 and A23 show that for several age groups, the headcount growth for "More exposed" workers slows or flattens relative to "Less exposed" workers after this period. This visual evidence suggests that the effects of AI are indeed heterogeneous across seniority levels, supporting the hypothesis of seniority-biased technological change.
