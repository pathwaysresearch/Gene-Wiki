# Algorithmic Bias and Fairness

## The Challenge of Individual Fairness
A key risk for companies is that their algorithms may be fair on average across a population but unfair to specific individuals, exposing organizations to significant liability questions. The challenge is to ensure that every individual is treated fairly by the system, moving beyond aggregate statistical measures of fairness. This requires addressing fairness upfront in the design phase rather than after the fact.

## Proactive, Design-Phase Governance
The text strongly advocates for addressing bias and fairness "ex-ante," during the design phase of an algorithm, rather than "ex-post," after it has been launched. This proactive approach involves building awareness about bias into the development process from the start. The analogy is made to how Microsoft, under Satya Nadella, retrained its developers to build security into their software development lifecycle in the 2000s to combat cybersecurity issues, rather than patching them after deployment.

## A Cross-Functional Responsibility
The conversation about bias, fairness, and transparency is not solely a task for computer scientists or AI specialists. It is defined as a cross-functional responsibility that must involve business leaders, technology teams, and legal and policy experts working collectively. The example of Toyota's manufacturing process, where quality is an integrated part of the process rather than a separate department's job, is used to illustrate this ideal state of shared ownership.
