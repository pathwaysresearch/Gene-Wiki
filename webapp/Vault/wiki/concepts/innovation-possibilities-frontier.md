# Innovation Possibilities Frontier

## Definition
The innovation possibilities frontier is a conceptual framework used to analyze the direction of technological change. It illustrates the potential trade-offs an economy faces between developing different kinds of innovations, specifically between technologies that automate existing tasks (leading to displacement) and those that create new tasks for labor (leading to reinstatement).

## Application to Automation and New Tasks
The text uses this concept to explain the changing balance between automation and new task creation. One possibility is that the frontier itself has shifted, making automation cheaper or new task creation more difficult. For example, advances in general-purpose technologies like AI and software may have made further automation cheaper to develop.

## Role of Incentives
The authors suggest a more plausible explanation is that the US economy has moved along a given innovation possibilities frontier due to changing incentives. Factors such as the US tax code, which aggressively subsidizes the use of equipment through tax credits and accelerated amortization while taxing labor employment via payroll taxes, create strong incentives for firms to pursue automation over the creation of new labor-intensive tasks. This can lead to an "inefficiently tilted" balance in favor of automation with adverse implications for jobs.
