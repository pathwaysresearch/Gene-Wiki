# New Work vs. Existing Work

## Definition
The study analyzes the evolution of the labor market by distinguishing between "new work," represented by the emergence of new occupational titles in census classifications, and "existing work," comprising the stock of pre-existing job roles. This framework allows for an examination of where job creation is occurring and for which types of workers.

## Occupational Distribution
Figure 3 explicitly compares the occupational distribution of the flow of new work versus the stock of existing work. It shows the difference in employment shares across twelve broad occupational categories, ordered from lowest to highest-paying. This analysis reveals how the composition of new jobs differs from the overall job market.

## Historical Trends
The analysis in Figure 3 is broken into two distinct periods, 1940–1980 (blue bars) and 1980–2018 (red bars), highlighting shifts in the nature of new work over time. By comparing these two eras, the study illustrates how the types of new jobs being created have changed, particularly in relation to the educational requirements and pay levels associated with them.
