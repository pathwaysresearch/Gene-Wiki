# Frictionless Systems

## Definition
The text describes frictionless systems as a feature of the digital age, where economic, social, and political activities like product recommendations and personalized ads can run with virtually no friction. This allows digital signals to reach networks of virtually infinite scale and scope with incredible speed.

## The Problem of Instability
The text warns that removing friction is not always beneficial. It uses the analogy of a car without brakes or a skier who cannot slow down to explain that frictionless systems are prone to instability and have difficulty finding equilibrium. Once a digital signal, like a viral meme or a phony headline, is in motion, it is almost impossible to stop, even by the organization that launched it or controls the network hubs.

## Real-World Consequences
A key example provided is the spread of videos from the Christchurch shooting, which were posted and reposted in multiple variants across platforms like Facebook and Google despite frantic efforts to remove them. This illustrates how frictionless, AI-driven processes can amplify harmful content to billions of people, a scale of reach and impact that was inconceivable with older, "friction-heavy" media like newspapers.
