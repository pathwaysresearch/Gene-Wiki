# Phoneme

## Definition
A phoneme is the smallest unit of sound that has a distinct meaning to the speakers of a particular language. It is a category of sounds that are perceived as being the same, even if there are slight acoustic variations (phones) within that category.

## Distinction from Phone
The key difference between a phone and a phoneme is that a phoneme is language-specific and defined by its ability to change a word's meaning. The text provides an example: in English, the 't' sound in "stick" and the 't' in "tick" are acoustically different phones, but English speakers perceive them as the same phoneme because swapping them does not change the meaning of a word. In the Thai language, however, this same acoustic difference is significant and corresponds to two different phonemes, which can distinguish one word from another.

## Application in Speech Recognition
For speech recognition in a specific language like English, the goal is to create a representation that can distinguish between different phonemes. The system does not need to distinguish nonphonemic variations in sound, such as those caused by a speaker's pitch, volume, speed, or gender. By focusing on phonemes, the system can abstract away from irrelevant acoustic details and focus on the meaningful components of speech.
