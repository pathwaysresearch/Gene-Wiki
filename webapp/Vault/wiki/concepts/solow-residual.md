# Solow Residual

## Definition
The Solow Residual is the portion of growth in output that cannot be explained by the growth in measured inputs, typically tangible capital and labor. It is derived from traditional growth accounting frameworks, as represented by Equation (3) in the text, and is used as the standard measure of total factor productivity (TFP) growth.

## Sources of Mismeasurement
The paper argues that the standard Solow Residual is an inaccurate measure of true productivity growth when intangible capital is present. The mismeasurement arises because the standard framework fails to account for two key dynamics: 1) when measured inputs (capital, labor) are used to produce unmeasured output (intangible investment), and 2) when unmeasured inputs (the stock of intangible capital) are used to produce measured output. The standard residual only correctly handles cases where measured inputs produce measured output or unmeasured inputs produce unmeasured output (by ignoring the latter).

## Impact on Measured Productivity
This mismeasurement leads to a systematic bias in observed productivity statistics. During periods of high intangible investment, resources are diverted from producing measured output, causing the Solow Residual to be artificially low. Conversely, when the accumulated intangible capital begins to yield returns in the form of measured output, the Solow Residual is artificially high. This dynamic is the direct cause of the Productivity J-Curve.
