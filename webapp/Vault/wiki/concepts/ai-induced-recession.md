# AI-Induced Recession

## The Mechanism
An AI-induced recession is a potential consequence of the unique scalability of artificial intelligence. Unlike human labor, software scales rapidly and cheaply. Once an AI becomes better than humans at a particular task, it can be deployed widely, causing job losses to happen very quickly. This rapid displacement of labor on a large scale could trigger a significant economic downturn.

## Short-Term vs. Long-Term Effects
While new jobs are expected to arise in the long term to replace those lost to AI, this provides little comfort for those unemployed during the transition period. The concept of an AI-induced recession focuses on this painful short-to-medium-term adjustment period, where unemployment rises significantly while the economy restructures and creates new roles for human workers.

## Analogy to Free Trade
The economic shock is compared to the effects of opening up free trade with a hypothetical "Robotlandia." While free trade ultimately creates overall economic benefits, it also produces winners and losers and can cause significant disruption. Similarly, even if AI is beneficial in the long run, the rapid pace of AI-driven job displacement poses a risk of a temporary but severe recession.
