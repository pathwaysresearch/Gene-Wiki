# Argument from Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem

## The Core Claim
This argument, advanced by philosophers such as J. R. Lucas (1961) and later by Sir Roger Penrose, posits that machines are mentally inferior to humans. The claim is based on the idea that computers are formal systems subject to Gödel's incompleteness theorem. This means they cannot establish the truth of certain statements about themselves (their "Gödel sentence"), whereas the proponents of the argument claim that humans have no such limitation.

## Counterargument from Finitude
The text presents a rebuttal by challenging the premise that computers are the type of formal system to which Gödel's theorem applies. While the theorem applies to infinite systems like Turing machines, which are powerful enough to do arithmetic, physical computers are finite. Any finite computer can be described as a very large system in propositional logic, which is not subject to Gödel's incompleteness theorem.

## Counterargument from Human Fallibility
A second counterargument points out that humans are not without their own logical limitations. The text illustrates this with a self-referential sentence: "J. R. Lucas cannot consistently assert that this sentence is true." If Lucas were to assert it, he would be contradicting himself, demonstrating that humans can also be trapped by logical paradoxes. This undermines the claim of their unique mental superiority in this regard.
