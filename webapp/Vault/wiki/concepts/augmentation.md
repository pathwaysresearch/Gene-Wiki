# Augmentation

## Definition
Augmentation is a key theoretical concept representing a technological force that generates new, labor-using job tasks. It is the primary mechanism for new work creation within the model, counteracting the labor-displacing effects of automation.

## Economic Effects
The theoretical framework predicts that, all else equal, augmentation in a sector attracts workers of both high and low skill types, leading to an expansion of employment in that sector. The empirical results strongly support this, showing a robustly positive and causal relationship between augmentation innovations and the emergence of new occupational job titles. This new-work-generating effect of augmentation appears to have diminished over time, with the effect being about 20% smaller in the 1980-2018 period compared to 1940-1980.

## Measurement
Augmentation exposure is measured empirically by linking the text of patents to the text of the Classified Index of Industries and Occupations (CAI). Using TF-IDF weighted word embeddings and cosine similarity, the analysis identifies patents that are textually similar to the descriptions of work in specific occupation-by-industry cells. These patent-occupation matches are then used to construct a measure of an occupation's exposure to augmentation innovations over a given decade.
