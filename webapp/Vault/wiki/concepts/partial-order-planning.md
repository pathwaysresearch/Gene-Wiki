# Partial-Order Planning

## Overview
Partial-order planning is an approach that constructs plans where the actions are only partially ordered, in contrast to methods that build a totally ordered sequence. This approach dominated planning research for about 20 years, with TWEAK being the first clear formal exposition, followed by widely used implementations like SNLP and UCPOP.

## Applications and Strengths
For certain tasks, such as operations scheduling, partial-order planning combined with domain-specific heuristics remains the technology of choice. It is also highly valued in domains where human understanding of the plan is critical. For example, operational plans for spacecraft and Mars rovers are often generated by partial-order planners because the plan refinement approach makes it easier for human operators to understand and verify the plan's correctness.

## Historical Context and Evolution
Partial-order planning fell out of favor in the late 1990s as faster methods like state-space search with new heuristics and GRAPHPLAN emerged. However, later research suggested a reconsideration was merited. For instance, the REPOP planner, using accurate heuristics derived from a planning graph, was shown to scale much better than GRAPHPLAN in parallelizable domains and was competitive with the fastest state-space planners.
