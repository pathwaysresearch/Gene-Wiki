# Architectural Inertia

## Definition

Architectural inertia is the resistance that constrains an organization and hampers its response to change, particularly in the face of architectural innovations. It is a consequence of a firm's established and successful operating patterns.

## Causes

This inertia arises from the very techniques, processes, and routines that make an organization efficient and unique, such as the Toyota Production System (TPS). While these embedded patterns improve the performance of daily activities, they can also make it difficult for the organization to reconfigure the relationships between its core components.

## Consequences

The text links architectural inertia directly to the failure of major established firms to adapt to significant technological shifts. It is presented as the underlying reason for the failures of companies like RCA, IBM, and Microsoft to successfully navigate architectural innovations in their industries.
