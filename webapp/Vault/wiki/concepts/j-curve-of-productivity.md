# J-Curve of Productivity

## Definition
The J-Curve of Productivity is a pattern observed when new technologies, such as AI, are implemented in an organization. It posits that instead of an immediate, linear increase in productivity, there is often an initial decline before a subsequent, substantial rise, creating a "J" shape on a graph of productivity over time.

## How It Works
The initial dip in the J-curve occurs because organizations must adapt to the new technology. This involves changing established work processes and workflows to fully leverage the technology's capabilities. Simply layering a new tool onto old processes is ineffective and can temporarily reduce efficiency. The subsequent upswing happens once the organization successfully re-imagines and re-engineers its workflows around the new technology.

## Implications for Management
According to Karim Lakhani, executives and managers should anticipate this J-curve effect rather than expecting immediate returns on AI investment. The key management challenge is to minimize the depth (amplitude) and duration (phase) of the initial dip. Achieving a positive ROI from AI is contingent on actively managing this process and making the necessary, often difficult, changes to workflows, such as when a company reduces a 100-hour task to 1.5 hours and must rethink its entire pipeline.
