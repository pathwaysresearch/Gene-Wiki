# Automation Threshold

## Definition
In the context of the economic model presented, tasks are indexed, and labor has a comparative advantage in tasks with a higher index. The automation threshold, denoted $I_j$ for a sector $j$, is the specific task at or below which production is accomplished by capital. Tasks with an index $i$ greater than $I_j$ are performed by labor, with the price of task output determined by the respective input cost (capital or labor).

## Economic Determination
The model defines a potential threshold, $\tilde{I}_j$, where the cost of labor equals the cost of capital for that task. This is determined by the equation $W_j/R_j = \gamma_j(\tilde{I}_j)$, where $W_j$ is the wage, $R_j$ is the rental rate of capital, and $\gamma_j$ is the labor productivity schedule. The actual threshold, $I_j^*$, is the minimum of this economic threshold and the current state of automation technology, $I_j^* = \min\{\tilde{I}_j, I_j\}$.

## Assumption on Adoption
The model employs a simplifying assumption (Assumption 3) that the threshold is always constrained by the state of automation, meaning $I_j^* = I_j$. This implies that when a new automation technology becomes available (advancing $I_j$), it is always adopted because it is profitable to do so. This assumption simplifies the analysis by ensuring the technological frontier for automation is the binding constraint on which tasks are performed by capital.
