# LLM-Generated Impact Scores

## Proposed Application
The text suggests that LLM-generated “impact scores" could be a useful tool for editors seeking to predict whether a submitted paper is likely to have an impact. This could potentially reduce power dynamics in the field and encourage more diverse and impactful submissions.

## Rationale and Context
This idea is presented in response to the finding that for the last 10 years, around 70% of consumer research articles garner hardly any citations. Using LLMs to help identify articles that are likely to make an impact is proposed as a way to incentivize researchers to move beyond this disappointing status quo and focus on more relevant marketplace phenomena.

## Challenges and Considerations
The text acknowledges that such an automated approach requires thought leadership to agree upon informative metrics for quantifying impact. It highlights the debate over using citation counts and impact factors as appropriate yardsticks, mentioning an alternative proposal to track a paper's percentile relative to others in the same journal and year. A potential worry is that this approach could penalize highly original research, which might take longer to accumulate citations.
