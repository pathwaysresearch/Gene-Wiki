# Displacement Effect

## Definition

The displacement effect is the direct negative impact on labor demand caused by automation. It occurs when new technology enables capital to perform tasks that were previously allocated to labor, thereby displacing workers from those activities. This effect fundamentally shifts the task content of production against labor.

## Mechanism

When a set of tasks is automated, the labor that was performing them is no longer required for that specific part of the production process. This directly reduces the share of labor in industry value added. The displacement effect represents the loss of this slice of the economic pie for labor.

## Net Impact

The displacement effect always works to reduce labor's share of income and overall labor demand. The ultimate impact of an automation technology on employment depends on whether this negative displacement effect is outweighed by the positive productivity effect it also generates. If the productivity gains are small, the displacement effect can dominate, leading to a net job loss.
