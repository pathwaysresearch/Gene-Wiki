# Dualism

## Definition
Dualism is a philosophical viewpoint that posits a clear distinction between mind and matter. It holds that there is a part of the human mind, which can be called the soul or spirit, that exists outside of nature and is therefore exempt from physical laws. This concept was given its first clear discussion by René Descartes.

## Proponents and Context
René Descartes (1596–1650) was a strong proponent of dualism. He argued that while animals could be treated as machines, humans possessed this dual quality. This separation of mind and body was part of his broader philosophical framework of rationalism, which emphasized the power of reasoning in understanding the world.

## Implications and Alternatives
One of the key problems that dualism attempts to solve is that of free will. A purely physical conception of the mind, governed entirely by physical laws, seems to leave little room for free will. Dualism addresses this by positing a non-physical soul that is not bound by these laws. The primary alternative to dualism discussed in the text is materialism, which holds that the mind is simply the product of the brain's operation according to the laws of physics.
