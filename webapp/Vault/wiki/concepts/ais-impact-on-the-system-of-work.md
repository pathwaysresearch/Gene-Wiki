# AI's Impact on the System of Work

## Core Thesis
This concept, based on the analysis of Sangeet Paul Choudary, posits that focusing solely on whether AI augments or displaces individual jobs is a dangerous oversimplification. The primary impact of AI is on the entire "system of work," which includes how jobs sit within organizational workflows and how organizations themselves operate within a larger competitive ecosystem. AI changes the rules of competition, forcing organizations to change what they prioritize, which in turn reshapes roles and workflows.

## Critique of Augmentation vs. Displacement
The popular phrase "AI won’t take your job, but someone using AI will" is criticized for two main fallacies. First, it incorrectly assumes AI will only augment workers and ignores that AI will, in some cases, directly displace them. Second, and more importantly, it misses the broader systemic impact of AI, which can make entire job categories or bundles of tasks irrelevant, regardless of whether an individual adopts AI tools.

## Historical Analogy: Container Shipping
To illustrate how technology changes a system of work, the example of container shipping is used. Before containerization, the work of docks was centered around dock workers moving breakbulk cargo. The introduction of the shipping container fundamentally changed the entire system of logistics and shipping, making the previous set of tasks and the jobs built around them obsolete, rather than simply augmenting the existing workers.
