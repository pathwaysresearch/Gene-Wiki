# Stimulus Sampling

## Definition
Stimulus sampling is a methodological solution in experimental research aimed at addressing the problem of confounding variables. It involves using a diverse set of stimuli within experimental conditions to ensure that the observed results are attributable to the theoretical variable of interest, rather than to idiosyncratic features of any single stimulus.

## Problem Addressed
The text illustrates the need for this technique with an example where an "identity-relevant ad" differs from a control ad on multiple dimensions beyond identity relevance, such as emotional tone ("never back down") and visual appeal (professional athletes vs. generic joggers). Without controlling for these confounds, a researcher cannot validly conclude that identity relevance caused an observed difference in purchase intentions.

## Implementation and Limitations
The technique is implemented by adding more “replicates” or examples of stimuli to each condition, such as adding more types of ads for different products. However, the text also notes a potential limitation, citing that sampling diverse stimuli might simply introduce random variation into the experiment, which could complicate the interpretation of results.
