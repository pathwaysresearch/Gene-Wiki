# Expertise

## Definition and Value
Expertise is defined as a scarce skill that commands a market premium, contrasted with work requiring no formal training or specialized knowledge. The text uses the example of an air traffic controller, which requires years of education and on-the-job training, versus a crossing guard, a role that can be filled by most people without specialized preparation. The value of expertise is determined by its scarcity and the demand for it.

## The Evolution of Expertise
The paper argues that the forms of expertise that are valued by the market are in constant flux across technological epochs. Forms that once commanded a high premium, such as farriery, typesetting, and fur-trapping, have become antiquated or automated. Conversely, many of today's most highly-paid jobs, like software engineers and patent lawyers, were created by specific technological or social innovations.

## Impact of Technology
Technological revolutions consistently reshape the landscape of expertise. The Industrial Revolution eroded the value of artisanal expertise through mass production. Later, pre-AI computing eroded the value of mass expertise based on following rules. The current era of Artificial Intelligence is poised to cause another major transformation in which forms of expertise are eclipsed and which gain new currency.
