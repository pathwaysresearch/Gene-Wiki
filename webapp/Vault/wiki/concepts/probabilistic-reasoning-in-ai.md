# Probabilistic Reasoning in AI

## Definition and Scope
Probabilistic reasoning is presented as a major paradigm within intelligent systems for handling plausible inference under uncertainty. The title of Judea Pearl's 1988 book, "Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems," establishes it as a core subject. The approach involves building and reasoning with formal models based on probability theory to draw conclusions from incomplete or noisy data.

## Key Formalisms and Techniques
The bibliography highlights several key formalisms. Judea Pearl's 1986 work on "belief networks" is central, focusing on methods for fusion, propagation, and structuring of probabilistic information. The concept is extended in works like Olesen's (1993) on "causal probabilistic networks with both discrete and continuous variables" and Milch et al.'s (2005) on "probabilistic models with unknown objects," showing the evolution of the core ideas to handle more complex scenarios.

## Methods and Applications
The texts point to various methods for working with these models. Pearl's 1987 paper on "evidential reasoning using stochastic simulation of causal models" suggests that simulation techniques are used for inference. The application of these models is broad, as demonstrated by the use of "Dynamic Bayesian networks for audiovisual speech recognition" (Nefian et al., 2002) and "Markov chain Monte Carlo data association for multi-target tracking" (Oh et al., 2009), showcasing their utility in complex, real-world problems.
