# Automation

## Definition
Automation is one of the key forces shaping labor demand in the paper's theoretical framework. It is defined as the process of reallocating existing tasks from labor to capital. This stands in contrast to augmentation, which creates entirely new tasks for labor.

## Economic Effects
According to the model presented, automation in a given sector leads to a contraction of that sector by reducing the employment of both high- and low-skill workers. When automation occurs in a low-skill intensive sector, it reduces the overall demand for low-skill labor, which in turn raises the economy-wide skill premium (the ratio of high-skill to low-skill wages). The empirical analysis finds that automation's impact on the emergence of new job titles is small, negative, and statistically insignificant.

## Measurement
In the empirical analysis, automation exposure is measured by establishing textual links between patents and the Dictionary of Occupational Titles (DOT). The procedure involves representing patents and DOT occupation descriptions as document vectors and calculating their cosine similarity. A high similarity score suggests a patent is relevant to automating the tasks of an occupation. Because the DOT only contains occupation-level information, the resulting automation measures are always defined at the occupation level.
