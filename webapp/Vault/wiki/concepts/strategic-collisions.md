# Strategic Collisions

## Definition and Context
The text defines a strategic collision as a competitive dynamic that arises when firms from previously separate industries are thrust into direct competition. This phenomenon is characteristic of the digital era, where the blurring of traditional industry boundaries forces incumbents to confront new, often digitally native, challengers.

## Competitive Dynamics and Patterns
These collisions are not random events but follow a repeating pattern. The text analyzes the specific competitive dynamics that emerge, such as how digital firms leverage network effects and data to challenge established players. Understanding these patterns is crucial for both incumbents and digital firms to navigate the new competitive landscape.

## Industry Examples
The book illustrates the concept of strategic collisions with several concrete examples. A prominent case is the travel industry, where companies like Airbnb collided with hotel chains like Hilton. Other cited examples include the automotive industry, retail, computing, and the clash between traditional and digital phone providers.
