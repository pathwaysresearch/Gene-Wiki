# Embodied Cognition

## Core Principle
The embodied cognition approach asserts that it is nonsensical to consider the brain as a separate, isolated entity. Instead, it holds that cognition is an activity that takes place within a physical body, which is itself embedded in and constantly interacting with an environment. The whole agent must be considered to understand how it works.

## The System as a Whole
According to this view, one must study the entire system of brain, body, and environment to understand intelligence. The brain is not just a passive reasoner but actively augments its cognitive processes by referring to and interacting with the environment. An example given is a reader perceiving and creating marks on paper to transfer knowledge.

## Implications for AI
This perspective shifts the focus of AI research. Fields that deal directly with an agent's interaction with the world—such as robotics, vision, and other sensory systems—are elevated from peripheral concerns to a central role in the quest to understand and build intelligence.
