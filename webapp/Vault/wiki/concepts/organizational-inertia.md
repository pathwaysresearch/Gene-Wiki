# Organizational Inertia

## The Problem of Scale and Scope
As organizations expand in scale or scope, managerial processes can stop working well, leading to inefficiency and even failure. This imposes overall limits to growth, as plants, restaurants, and even R&D teams can become unwieldy to manage beyond an optimal size, causing their productivity and innovativeness to suffer. These factors embed norms, incentives, and rewards that collectively foster inertia.

## Role of Traditional IT
Traditional information technology has generally failed to loosen these constraints. As traditional enterprises create more functional silos, they deploy a myriad of separate IT systems, such as CRM and general ledger software, each tailored to a specific function. This results in a fragmented and disconnected technological landscape that reinforces organizational divisions.

## Resistance to Change
Integrating these disparate, disconnected legacy systems is a long and painful process that requires custom software. Over time, this custom integration work itself becomes a source of inertia and resistance to change, further cementing the organization's inability to adapt.
