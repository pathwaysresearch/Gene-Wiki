# Data as a Complement to Prediction

## Core Principle
The text establishes that data and prediction machines are complements. This means that procuring or developing an AI will be of limited value unless the organization also has access to the data required to feed it. The effectiveness and continuous improvement of a prediction machine are fundamentally dependent on a steady pipeline of relevant data.

## Strategic Implications for Data Ownership
This complementarity drives critical strategic decisions about organizational boundaries and data control. For AI-focused companies, especially startups like Ada Support, owning the feedback data generated from user interactions is crucial for improving their product over time. If a company's core strategy is centered on its prediction machine, it must control both the data and the machine to facilitate continual learning and maintain a competitive advantage.

## Data Sourcing Strategies
When a company does not own the necessary data, it must develop a strategy to acquire it. If data resides with consumers, it can be exchanged for a better product or service. If it resides with other companies, a data swap might be possible if there is mutual value. However, relying on an exclusive or monopoly provider of data is risky, as that provider could appropriate the entire value generated by the AI. For non-core applications, a company might purchase prediction as a service, much like it purchases energy, but this is not a viable strategy for AI-centric firms.
