# Identity Motives in Consumption

## Definition
Identity motives in consumption refer to the drive to make purchasing decisions based on the desire to be a certain kind of person. This represents a quest for meaning where consumption choices and the use of products become a form of self-expression and identity signaling. People are motivated to consume not just for functional benefits but to affirm and communicate who they are or who they aspire to be. This quest for meaning remains a powerful driver of buying decisions even in an age of advanced technology.

## Impact on Routines and Product Use
For consumers driven by identity motives, certain routines can become personally relevant and integral to their sense of self. The act of performing a task, rather than just its outcome, can be a form of identity expression. Consequently, being freed from such a personally relevant routine by an automated device or service might be seen as undesirable, as it removes an opportunity for identity signaling. This creates a potential burden and a psychological barrier to adopting certain innovations.

## Implications for Technology Adoption
Identity motives have significant implications for the adoption of new technologies like the Internet of Things (IoT). Consumer adoption of an IoT application will be less likely when the task it automates is highly relevant to the consumer's identity. Conversely, adoption is more likely when the associated tasks are less relevant for identity signaling. This means a person might oppose an IoT application in one context (e.g., automated cooking for a passionate chef) but willingly accept it in another with less personal relevance (e.g., an automated vacuum cleaner).
