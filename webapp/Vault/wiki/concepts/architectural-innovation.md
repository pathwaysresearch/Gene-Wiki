# Architectural Innovation

## Definition

As defined in a 1990 paper by Rebecca Henderson and Kim Clark, architectural innovations are those that require changing the architecture between technological components. This is distinct from innovations that change the components themselves.

## Danger for Established Firms

This type of innovation is described as a "particular danger for established firms." The productive patterns and routines that make a firm efficient can also build inertia, which hampers the organization's ability to respond to changes in product or system architecture.

## Historical Examples of Failure

The text provides several examples of established firms that failed to adapt to architectural innovations. These include RCA's failure to rearchitect and miniaturize its tabletop radios in the face of competition from Sony, IBM's failure to transition from mainframes to PCs, and Microsoft's failure to rearchitect PCs into smartphones.
