# Classification Experience

## Definition
The Classification Experience is a key part of the Consumer AI Experience framework, arising from the "predicting" capability of AI. It describes how consumers interact with and interpret the ultra-customized offerings and recommendations generated by sophisticated algorithms. Consumers often infer from these outputs that the AI has classified them as a certain type of person, an inference amplified by the natural human tendency for categorical thinking.

## Core Psychological Tension
This experience is characterized by a fundamental tension between feeling understood and feeling misunderstood. Consumers may feel understood when AI provides relevant and satisfying recommendations. However, they can feel misunderstood due to perceived incorrect classification, the discriminatory use of classification, or a combination of both. This negative response is especially likely when a consumer perceives the identity assigned by the AI as inaccurate, non-central to their self-concept, or dated, as it can threaten their motives for uniqueness and individual agency.

## Organizational Challenges and Bias
Classification errors pose a significant challenge for organizations because they can become self-perpetuating. An initial bias in an algorithm can lead to certain outcomes, and the data generated from those outcomes can then be used to train future versions of the algorithm, entrenching the original bias. Unlike technical data capture failures, addressing classification errors requires organizations to scrutinize their marketing and consumer-facing departments for entrenched social and racial biases within their databases and core assumptions about their customers.
