# Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT)

## Definition
A Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT) is a type of controlled experiment where a treatment is randomly assigned to some individuals while others, the control group, do not receive it. The outcomes in the two groups are then compared to determine the causal effect of the treatment. This method is the primary situation in which mainstream statistics traditionally permits discussion of cause and effect.

## Core Principle
The fundamental principle of an RCT is to create two groups that are as identical as possible in all relevant ways, except for the treatment being studied. By comparing a treatment group with a similar control group, researchers can infer that any observed difference in outcomes is caused by the treatment, as it is the only systematic difference between them. This comparison serves as a practical substitute for the impossible task of observing the same individual with and without the treatment simultaneously.

## Limitations
Despite being the preferred method, RCTs are not always feasible. Interventions may be physically impossible, such as randomly assigning obesity to patients. They can also be unethical, as in forcing a randomly selected group to smoke for a study. Furthermore, practical difficulties in recruiting subjects for inconvenient procedures can lead to volunteer bias, where the study participants are not representative of the intended population.
