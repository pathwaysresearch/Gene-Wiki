# Worker Attrition

## Definition and Measurement
Worker attrition, also known as employee turnover, is analyzed in the paper as a broad indicator of how workers respond to the implementation of AI assistance in their jobs. The study measures this by comparing attrition rates between agents who received AI assistance and untreated agents with equivalent tenure, while controlling for other factors like location and time.

## Impact of AI Assistance
The analysis reveals that access to AI assistance is associated with a significant reduction in worker attrition. The effect was most pronounced among newer agents, specifically those with less than six months of experience. For this group, the reduction was approximately 10 percentage points, which translates to a 40% decrease relative to their baseline attrition rate of 25%.

## Findings by Skill Level
When examining attrition by pre-existing worker skill levels, the study found a significant decrease in attrition across all skill groups. However, unlike the clear gradient observed with tenure, there was no distinct pattern showing a larger or smaller effect for higher or lower-skilled workers. This suggests the positive impact on retention is widespread across skill levels.

## Methodological Caveats
The authors advise that the findings on attrition should be interpreted with more caution than the main productivity results. This is because the analysis could not include agent-level fixed effects, as attrition is a one-time event. This limitation means the results might overstate the AI's impact if, for example, the firm was more likely to grant AI access to agents it already considered more likely to stay.
