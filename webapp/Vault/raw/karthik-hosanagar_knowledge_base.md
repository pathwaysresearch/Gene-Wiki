### 2. [#4: The Language Advantage: Understanding and Mastering ChatGPT](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kartik-hosanagar-22272115_4-the-language-advantage-understanding-activity-7311795345514987521-q6cI) (2025) — blog_post

# Kartik Hosanagar’s Post

[Image: View profile for Kartik Hosanagar](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kartik-hosanagar-22272115?trk=public_post_feed-actor-name)
Kartik Hosanagar
1y · Edited

During a recent talk, I was asked "what has changed in recent years for AI to take off in such a big way?"

An anecdote and recent history here. In 2017-18, I was working on my book "A Human's Guide to Machine Intelligence." My goal was to write one chapter with AI to illustrate AI's emerging capabilities. [Dokyun Lee](https://www.linkedin.com/in/dokyun?trk=public_post-text) & I tried a few things but just couldn't pull it off. Too early. In late 2018/ early 2019, [Daehwan Ahn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/daehwanahn/en?trk=public_post-text) introduced me to GPT2 and we saw GPT2 and BERT (i.e Transformer models) were generating text much better than RNN-based methods which were more popular until then. But still impossible to write a chapter. In 2020, GPT3 came out and it was obvious we were at the cusp of being able to write a coherent chapter using these tools. My attempt had been 2.5 years too early.

Coming back to the question of what changed:

1. A technical (yet incomplete) answer: Transformer models introduced in the 2017 "Attention is All You Need" paper changed the game. In short, the idea behind their attention layers  was that the meaning of a word depended a lot on the surrounding text. E.g., the meaning of the word "model" changes if the word before it is "fashion" versus "mathematical."

2. But the above answer is incomplete. Language itself is a huge building block for intelligence. Language allowed humans to communicate complex ideas, share knowledge, and do collective problem-solving. The ability to plan collectively and coordinate activities likely provided humans an evolutionary advantage over other species by facilitating cooperation. Unlike other species, where skills and information are acquired through individual experience or genetic inheritance, humans could pass down accumulated knowledge through language. Once AI got language, AI could do many other things including coding, processing legal docs, agentic collaboration, etc. I think this second piece is often underemphasized.

# If you want to pitch this investor, you have to walk him home

Kartik Hosanagar, Wharton professor, investor and entrepreneur, outside his home in Society Hill.

It was 2008, during the Great Recession, when [**Kartik Hosanagar**](/person/kartik-hosanagar) started to noticed the switch.

**Wharton** students began choosing entrepreneurship over careers in consulting or banks. That meant Hosanagar, a Wharton professor who cofounded startups and has invested in nearly a dozen others including **Monetate** and **RJMetrics**, was suddenly more in demand than ever.

It ramped up even more over the last three years: he’d get emails from students every day, asking to meet with him to get his feedback on a startup idea or advice on a startup job offer. Coupled with normal office hours for students in his classes, it started to get out of hand.

His solution? Walks.

Hosanagar does a daily “brisk walk” from Penn’s campus to Center City (he lives in Society Hill with his wife, [**SmartyPAL**](/organization/smartypal) founder [**Prasanna Krishnan**](/person/prasanna-krishnan)), accompanied by a student who’s signed up to walk with him — and pitch him or ask for career advice. He uses a Google spreadsheet to organize the walks.

Even throughout the winter’s snow, ice and rain, Hosanagar kept up the walks. [During a recent interview](https://technical.ly/philly/2015/03/31/smartypal-national-science-foundation/), Krishnan said she knows all too well about her husband’s walks.

“Sometimes I’m forced to do a walk with Kartik,” she said, winking at him.

Hosanagar’s recent investments include those in [Sunnyvale, Calif.-based **OpsClarity**](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/opsclarity) and [New York City-based **MaestroIQ**](http://techcrunch.com/2015/01/29/maestroiq-launch/), both of which were founded by Wharton students. He’s also working on SmartyPAL, the iPad platform for children’s books that his wife is running.
